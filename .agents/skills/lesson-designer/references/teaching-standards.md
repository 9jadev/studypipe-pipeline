# Teaching Standards (StudyPipe Phenomenon-Based Inquiry Standard)

Every StudyPipe lesson is designed around a student-centred, phenomenon-based inquiry methodology combined with rigorous WAEC / NECO / JAMB examination preparation. Follow every principle below without exception. *(Note: apply this methodology naturally; never name any external national curriculum in the lesson text.)*

## A. Holistic Phenomenon-Based Course Planning
1. **Anchor in a Real Phenomenon First.** Every lesson opens with an authentic, observable real-world mystery set in a familiar Nigerian context (market, kitchen, workshop, power supply, clinic, quality-control lab). Students encounter the phenomenon and form predictions *before* formal definitions or equations are introduced.
2. **The 5-Phase Inquiry Cycle.** Structure every lesson across five purposeful phases:
   - **0. Do Now (Spaced Retrieval · 5 min):** Effortful recall of prior knowledge before new learning begins.
   - **1. Wonder (8–10 min):** 3D animated character story + *Wonder Wall* prediction board. Curiosity and student questions drive the investigation.
   - **2. Investigate (20–25 min):** Hands-on sorting/classification followed by a 3D Virtual Practical where the student manipulates variables, records measurements, and analyses patterns before being lectured.
   - **3. Make sense (15 min):** Connect experimental findings to precise scientific definitions, equations, comparison tables, and a 3D micro/vector/energy zoom-in view.
   - **4. Apply (20 min):** Step-by-step worked examples → resolving the opening story mystery (*The Bridge*) → 12 exam-style instant-feedback questions.
   - **5. Reflect & Assignment (10 min + 40 min home):** Metacognitive self-rating (`1–4`), Exit Ticket, Blank-Page Recall, and a 30-mark structured assignment + home investigation.
3. **Transversal Competences (`L1`–`L7`).** Every lesson weaves in 2–3 broad life and learning competences (documented in the Teacher Notes):
   - **L1 · Thinking and learning to learn:** Formulating hypotheses, testing predictions, and evaluating one's own learning.
   - **L2 · Cultural interaction and self-expression:** Connecting scientific ideas to local Nigerian practices, crafts, and clear communication.
   - **L3 · Managing daily life and safety:** Practicing laboratory, electrical, mechanical, and household safety.
   - **L4 · Multiliteracy:** Interpreting 3D simulations, data tables, graphs, symbolic equations, and apparatus scales.
   - **L5 · Digital and simulation competence:** Using interactive models to investigate cause and effect.
   - **L6 · Working-life skills and entrepreneurship:** Understanding quality control, measurement accuracy, and engineering reliability.
   - **L7 · Building a sustainable future:** Promoting honest commerce, environmental stewardship, and efficient resource use.
4. **Pacing & Brain Breaks.** In the 80-minute classroom timetable, always schedule a **5-minute active movement break** around minute 45–50 (between the hands-on investigation and formal concept synthesis) so students reset attention through physical movement.

## B. Cognitive Load, Multimedia & Visual Choreography
5. **Show, Don't Write on the Side.** Every core mechanism, practical procedure, or invisible force/particle interaction must be enacted in an interactive 3D or 2D stage (`SceneKit` / `StoryStage`), never left as a wall of text.
6. **Guided Spotlight Narration.** Narration is not a static audio file; it is an interactive tutor (`StudyNarrator`) that scrolls smoothly to each element, spotlights specific DOM sections or 3D parts (`{ scene, part }`), triggers live demonstrations (`action`), and pauses with **"Your turn!"** (`wait`) until the student completes an interaction.
7. **Concrete → Representational → Abstract (C → R → A).** Move from the concrete Nigerian story and 3D lab apparatus → to tables, graphs, and vector/particle models → to symbolic equations (`.eq`).
8. **Worked Examples with Explicit Reasoning.** Provide 3–4 worked examples per lesson. Every step must explain *why* the step is taken, ending with a clear `.answer-line` showing units and significant figures as required by WAEC/NECO.

## C. Assessment for Learning & Student Agency
9. **Low-Stakes Metacognitive Reflection.** In the *Reflect* section, students rate themselves honestly from `1` (*Not yet*) to `4` (*I could teach it*) on 5 specific *"I can…"* statements. Frame reflection as a supportive guide for future practice, never a high-stress judgment.
10. **Effort Before Reveal (Locked Solutions).** All open-ended analysis questions, exit tickets, and assignment questions use locked solutions (`details.solution`) that unlock only after the student types a genuine attempt (`≥ 12` characters) or ticks *"I've attempted this on paper"*.
11. **Spaced & Cumulative Retrieval.** Start with *Do Now* retrieval cards and close with *Blank-Page Recall* checklists so memory is strengthened through active reconstruction.

## D. Originality, Language & Exam Rigour
12. **100% Paraphrased & Original.** Never copy sentences, definitions, or questions verbatim from any online source, textbook, syllabus document, or past paper. Paraphrase all source material into fresh, original wording.
13. **WAEC / NECO / JAMB Precision.** Use British English spelling (*colour, neutralise, metre,osh, sulphur, centre*) and standard SI units (`m`, `s`, `kg`, `mol dm⁻³`, `m s⁻²`). Include examiner rules (e.g. burette readings to `2 d.p.`, stopwatch timing of `20` oscillations, precautions written in the **past tense**: *"Parallax error was avoided by…"*).
