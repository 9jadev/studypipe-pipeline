## Chapter 4: Example 1

| # | Narration | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.1 | Now let's solve our equation. x squared, plus six x, minus two, equals zero. | New board: "Example 1"<br>Kit panel off<br>Board: x^2 + 6x - 2 = 0 |
| 4.2 | Step one. Move the constant term to the right hand side. Add two to both sides. | Board: x^2 + 6x = 2 (note: +2 to both sides) |
| 4.3 | Step two. Take half the coefficient of x. Half of six is three. Then square it. Three squared is nine. | Board: (62 )^2 = 3^2 = 9 (note: half, then square)<br>Highlight b, h, sq |
| 4.4 | Step three. Add nine to both sides. This is important. Whatever you add to the left, you must also add to the right. | Board: x^2 + 6x + 9 = 2 + 9 (note: +9 to BOTH sides)<br>Highlight sq |
| 4.5 | Step four. The left side is now a perfect square: x plus three, all squared. And two plus nine is eleven. | Board: (x + 3)^2 = 11<br>Highlight h |
| 4.6 | Step five. Take the square root of both sides. Don't forget plus or minus, because both a positive and a negative number square to give eleven. | Board: x + 3 = ±√11 (note: never forget ±) |
| 4.7 | Step six. Subtract three from both sides. So x equals minus three, plus or minus root eleven. | Board: x = -3 ± √11<br>Box a7 |
| 4.8 | That's the exact answer. As decimals, x is about zero point three two, or minus six point three two, to two decimal places. | Board: x ≈ 0.32 or x ≈ -6.32 |
| 4.9 | Always check your answer. Put zero point three one six six back into the equation. The left side comes out almost exactly zero. It works. | Board: (0.3166)^2 + 6(0.3166) - 2 ≈ 0 ✓ |

| # | Narration | On screen |

| 6.2 | Complete the square. x squared plus six x is x plus three all squared, minus nine. So y equals x plus three, all squared, minus eleven. | Board: y = (x + 3)^2 - 9 - 2<br>Board: y = (x + 3)^2 - 11<br>Box t3 |

## Chapter 8: Where the quadratic formula comes from

| # | Narration | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8.1 | Here's something powerful. Let's complete the square on the general equation: a x squared, plus b x, plus c, equals zero. | New board: "Where the formula comes from"<br>Board: ax^2 + bx + c = 0 |
| 8.2 | Divide every term by a, and move the constant to the right. | Board: x^2 + b/ax = -c/a |
| 8.3 | Half of b over a is b over two a. Add its square to both sides. | Board: x^2 + b/ax + (b/2a )^2 = (b/2a )^2 - c/a<br>Highlight sq |
| 8.4 | The left side is a perfect square. The right side simplifies to b squared minus four a c, over four a squared. | Board: (x + b/2a )^2 = (b^2 - 4ac)/(4a^2) |
| 8.5 | Square root both sides, plus or minus, then subtract b over two a. | Board: x = -b ± √b^2 - 4ac2a<br>Box q5 |
| 8.6 | That is the quadratic formula. It is simply completing the square, done once, for every quadratic equation. | — |
