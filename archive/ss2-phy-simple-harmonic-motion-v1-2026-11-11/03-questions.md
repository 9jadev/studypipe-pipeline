# Assessment Bank: Simple Harmonic Motion & the Simple Pendulum

## Part 1: 12 Interactive Quiz Questions (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **[WAEC-style · Understand · Obj 1]** Which statement correctly defines **simple harmonic motion (SHM)**?
   - A: Any motion that repeats itself in equal intervals of time
   - B: Motion in which acceleration is proportional to displacement from equilibrium and always directed towards it ✓
   - C: Motion with constant speed along a circular path
   - D: Motion in which restoring force acts in the same direction as displacement
   - **Explanation:** All SHM is periodic, but only motions satisfying $a = -\omega^2 x$ (acceleration proportional to displacement and directed **towards** the equilibrium centre) are simple harmonic.

2. **[WAEC Practical · Apply · Obj 3]** A student records $36.0\text{ s}$ for $20$ complete oscillations of a simple pendulum. What are the **period $T$** and **frequency $f$**?
   - A: $T = 0.56\text{ s},\; f = 1.80\text{ Hz}$
   - B: $T = 1.80\text{ s},\; f = 0.56\text{ Hz}$ ✓
   - C: $T = 18.0\text{ s},\; f = 0.056\text{ Hz}$
   - D: $T = 720\text{ s},\; f = 0.0014\text{ Hz}$
   - **Explanation:** Period $T = t / N = 36.0 / 20 = \mathbf{1.80\text{ s}}$. Frequency $f = 1 / T = 1 / 1.80 = \mathbf{0.56\text{ Hz}}$.

3. **[JAMB-style · Analyse · Obj 3]** If the length of a simple pendulum is **quadrupled** ($L \to 4L$) at the same location, its period $T$:
   - A: becomes four times as large ($4T$)
   - B: doubles ($2T$) ✓
   - C: is halved ($\tfrac{1}{2}T$)
   - D: remains unchanged ($T$)
   - **Explanation:** Since $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, period is proportional to $\sqrt{L}$. Multiplying $L$ by $4$ multiplies $T$ by $\sqrt{4} = \mathbf{2}$.

4. **[NECO-style · Apply · Obj 3]** A simple pendulum has a brass bob of mass $50\text{ g}$ and period $1.60\text{ s}$. If the bob is replaced with a $150\text{ g}$ lead bob of the same size at the same effective length $L$, the new period is:
   - A: $4.80\text{ s}$
   - B: $2.77\text{ s}$
   - C: $1.60\text{ s}$ ✓
   - D: $0.53\text{ s}$
   - **Explanation:** In $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, mass $m$ cancels out completely because gravitational force and inertia both scale with $m$. The period stays **$1.60\text{ s}$**.

5. **[WAEC-style · Understand · Obj 4]** As a pendulum bob passes through its **equilibrium centre ($x = 0$)**, which pair of quantities correctly describes its motion?
   - A: Velocity is zero; acceleration is maximum
   - B: Velocity is maximum; acceleration is zero ✓
   - C: Both velocity and acceleration are maximum
   - D: Potential energy is maximum; kinetic energy is zero
   - **Explanation:** At $x = 0$, $a = -\omega^2(0) = 0$ and all gravitational potential energy has been converted into kinetic energy, so speed reaches $v_{\max} = \omega A$.

6. **[JAMB-style · Apply · Obj 4]** A body performs SHM with amplitude $A = 0.05\text{ m}$ and angular frequency $\omega = 10\text{ rad s}^{-1}$. What are its **maximum speed $v_{\max}$** and **maximum acceleration $a_{\max}$**?
   - A: $0.50\text{ m s}^{-1}$ and $5.0\text{ m s}^{-2}$ ✓
   - B: $5.0\text{ m s}^{-1}$ and $0.50\text{ m s}^{-2}$
   - C: $0.50\text{ m s}^{-1}$ and $0.05\text{ m s}^{-2}$
   - D: $200\text{ m s}^{-1}$ and $2000\text{ m s}^{-2}$
   - **Explanation:** $v_{\max} = \omega A = 10 \times 0.05 = \mathbf{0.50\text{ m s}^{-1}}$; $a_{\max} = \omega^2 A = 10^2 \times 0.05 = \mathbf{5.0\text{ m s}^{-2}}$.

7. **[WAEC Practical · Analyse · Obj 4]** In a simple pendulum experiment, a student plots length $L\text{ (m)}$ on the vertical axis against $T^2\text{ (s}^2\text{)}$ on the horizontal axis and obtains a straight-line slope $S = 0.248\text{ m s}^{-2}$. The value of $g$ is given by:
   - A: $g = 4\pi^2 S \approx 9.79\text{ m s}^{-2}$ ✓
   - B: $g = 4\pi^2 / S \approx 159\text{ m s}^{-2}$
   - C: $g = 2\pi S \approx 1.56\text{ m s}^{-2}$
   - D: $g = S / 4\pi^2 \approx 0.0063\text{ m s}^{-2}$
   - **Explanation:** From $T^2 = (4\pi^2/g)L$, rearranging for $L$ gives $L = \left(\frac{g}{4\pi^2}\right)T^2$. Thus the slope of $L$ vs $T^2$ is $S = g / (4\pi^2)$, so $g = 4\pi^2 S = 39.478 \times 0.248 \approx \mathbf{9.79\text{ m s}^{-2}}$.

8. **[WAEC Practical · Evaluate · Obj 5]** Why do WAEC practical instructions ask candidates to place the fiducial reference mark at the **centre of the swing** rather than at one extreme end?
   - A: The bob stops at the centre so it is easier to see
   - B: The bob moves fastest through the centre, so the exact instant of crossing is sharp and minimises timing uncertainty ✓
   - C: Air resistance is zero only at the centre
   - D: The string is shortest at the centre
   - **Explanation:** At the extreme ends the bob slows to a momentary halt and reverses gradually, making it hard to judge the exact turnaround instant. At the centre it crosses the mark rapidly and crisply.

9. **[NECO-style · Apply · Obj 4]** A particle in SHM has angular frequency $\omega = 3.0\text{ rad s}^{-1}$ and amplitude $A = 0.10\text{ m}$. Its speed when it is at displacement $x = 0.08\text{ m}$ from equilibrium is:
   - A: $0.30\text{ m s}^{-1}$
   - B: $0.24\text{ m s}^{-1}$
   - C: $0.18\text{ m s}^{-1}$ ✓
   - D: $0.06\text{ m s}^{-1}$
   - **Explanation:** $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = 3.0\sqrt{0.10^2 - 0.08^2} = 3.0\sqrt{0.0036} = 3.0 \times 0.06 = \mathbf{0.18\text{ m s}^{-1}}$.

10. **[WAEC Practical · Evaluate · Obj 5]** Which precaution is written in the exact **past-tense style** required by WAEC Paper 3 examiners?
    - A: Avoid conical oscillation during the experiment.
    - B: I will make sure the fan is switched off.
    - C: Conical oscillation was avoided by displacing the bob slightly so it swung in a single vertical plane. ✓
    - D: Do not push the bob when releasing it.
    - **Explanation:** WAEC Paper 3 requires precautions to be reported in the **past tense** (passive or active), stating clearly *what* was done and *how*.

11. **[JAMB-style · Analyse · Obj 1]** Why does a pendulum clock that keeps accurate time in Lagos **lose time** when taken to the top of a high mountain where gravity $g$ is slightly smaller?
    - A: Smaller $g$ makes the period $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ longer, so each swing takes more time and the clock ticks more slowly ✓
    - B: Smaller $g$ makes the period $T$ shorter, so the clock ticks too fast
    - C: The brass bob loses mass at high altitude
    - D: Air resistance increases at the top of a mountain
    - **Explanation:** Because $g$ is in the denominator of $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, a decrease in $g$ increases $T$. Each tick takes slightly longer than $1\text{ s}$, so the clock falls behind (loses time).

12. **[NECO-style · Understand · Obj 4]** When air resistance gradually reduces the amplitude of a freely swinging pendulum over several minutes, this effect is called:
    - A: Resonance
    - B: Damping ✓
    - C: Diffraction
    - D: Polarization
    - **Explanation:** **Damping** is the progressive decrease in amplitude and mechanical energy of an oscillation due to dissipative forces such as air drag and friction at the support.

## Part 2: Home Investigation & 30-Mark Written Assignment

- **Part A (Practical Task):** Build a simple pendulum at home using sewing thread, a small stone or metal key/nut, a ruler/measuring tape, and a phone stopwatch. Measure $t$ for $20$ swings at $L = 40\text{ cm}$ and $L = 80\text{ cm}$, compute $T$ and $T^2$, and estimate local $g$.

  2. `b2` (3 marks): Explain why all simple harmonic motions are periodic, but not all periodic motions are simple harmonic, giving one example of each.
  3. `b3` (5 marks): A body oscillates with $A = 0.12\text{ m}$ and $f = 2.5\text{ Hz}$. Calculate (a) $\omega$, (b) $v_{\max}$, (c) $a_{\max}$, and (d) speed at $x = 0.09\text{ m}$.
  4. `b4` (4 marks): State at which point in a pendulum's swing (centre $x=0$ or extreme $x=\pm A$) each quantity is maximum: (a) kinetic energy, (b) restoring force, (c) linear speed, (d) gravitational potential energy.
  5. `b5` (5 marks): From a practical table ($L = 0.50\text{ m}, t = 28.40\text{ s}$ and $L = 1.00\text{ m}, t = 40.16\text{ s}$ for $N = 20$ swings), calculate $T$ and $T^2$ for each, find the slope $\Delta L / \Delta T^2$, and determine $g$.
  6. `b6` (3 marks): State how and why the period $T$ of a simple pendulum changes if (a) bob mass is doubled, (b) string length is multiplied by $9$, (c) the apparatus is moved to the Moon where $g_{\text{Moon}} \approx \tfrac{1}{6}g_{\text{Earth}}$.
  7. `b7` (4 marks): Mama Ngozi's faulty school wall clock has period $T = 2.15\text{ s}$ instead of $2.00\text{ s}$. Explain why adding lead shot to the bob failed, and state which way the regulating nut must be turned.
  8. `b8` (2 marks): State two acceptable precautions in the **past tense** taken during a simple pendulum practical.
