# Pedagogy: how an explainer is structured

## The arc of every video

| Part | Share of time | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 5–10% | A puzzle or surprise from the lesson's story or everyday Nigerian life ("where did 3.6 V go?", "this equation won't factorise") |
| Objectives | one line | What the student will be able to do by the end, phrased as exam tasks |
| Core ideas | 30–40% | One idea per chapter, each built on the last, with a visual for each |
| Worked examples | 25–35% | Every step on the board, every step spoken, a check at the end |
| Common mistakes | 5–10% | The errors that cost marks, each shown wrong then right |
| Summary | 5% | Three to five lines that fit on one board |
| Your turn | 5–10% | One practice question, `hold` 12–15 s pause-and-try, then the full solution |

## Chapters

- One idea per chapter, 3–14 lines. Title chapters by what the student learns ("Two readings, same cell"), not by type ("Example 3").
- Start each chapter with a `clear` and a heading. Keep about six board lines per board; clear again for a new step group.
- End chapters that introduce a formula with the formula boxed.

## Scene patterns

**Equation / worked solution.** State the question with givens on the board. One line per step: say what you're doing and why ("Step two. Move the constant to the right"), write the line, highlight the term that changed with its role. Box the final answer. Check by substitution, and say it ("It works"). Use `note` for the move ("+2 to both sides", "never forget ±").

**Experiment.** Show the setup in its kit before anything happens. Change one variable per line and say what the student should watch ("look at the voltmeter"). Name each observation, then explain it, then write the equation. Add a safety line where real.

**Process (germination, electrolysis over time).** Show stages in order with the kit's stage field. Pair each stage with its cause.

**Concept model (tollgate, area model).** Introduce the analogy in one line, run it stage by stage, then translate it into the formula in the next chapter. Always say where the analogy stops being true if students could over-apply it.

**Graph.** Write the equation, connect it to y = mx + c, plot real data, then read off intercepts and gradient one at a time, each with its meaning.

**Past question (WAEC 1999 Q2 style).** Read the question, list givens, name the method, solve, check, then one sentence on what examiners look for (units, significant figures, working shown).

## Pacing and attention

- Lines of 8–40 words. Alternate longer explaining lines with short punchy ones ("There they are.").
- Something should change on screen at least every 8–10 seconds.
- Use the student's lesson story and characters in hooks and examples; return to the hook's question when it is answered.
- Use `hold` only for genuine thinking: practice questions, "predict before I show you".

## Accuracy

- Follow WAEC / NECO wording for definitions and conventions.
- Keep the lesson's numbers, but add an accuracy note if they are unrealistic and phrase the narration so it stays true.
- If a source answer is wrong, do not copy it: flag it in the script.

## Choosing a theme

Primary and JSS: `fiesta` or `safari`. SS1–SS2: `safari` or `vegetation` (biology, agriculture). SS3 exam prep or teachers: `classic`.
