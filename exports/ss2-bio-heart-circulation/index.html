The Unit Mystery · Oja Oba Market, Ibadan

Health Saturday at Oja Oba

Dr Aisha has set up a free screening tent at Oja Oba market. Chidi’s pulse jumps from 68 to 132 beats a minute after a sprint through the stalls, then settles. Mama Bisi, the pepper seller, keeps a thumb dent in her swollen ankles, and her cuff reads 160/100.

Then an okada rider from the Oyo road arrives bleeding. His card says group B, Rh negative, and the cool box holds six bags: O+, O−, A−, B+, B− and AB−. Baba Sule the butcher has an answer for everything, and most of his answers are wrong. “Take the AB, it has both letters!” he shouts.

Why does a sprint speed up the heart, why do ankles swell by evening, and which bags are a safe first choice for the rider?

  • Clue 1The pulse returned to normal in five minutes.
  • Clue 2The swelling is worst in the evening, after standing all day.
  • Clue 3The rider’s card says Rh negative.
  • Clue 4Only two of the six bags are a safe first choice.
  • Clue 5Baba Sule’s goat heart has one wall far thicker than the other.

04 / Learning Outcomes

What you will master by the end of Lesson 7.

Every skill is practised on a 3D bench and tested with WAEC, NECO and JAMB-style questions.

01

Explain why we need blood

Give four reasons big, active animals cannot rely on diffusion, using SA : V = 6/L, and compare open, single and double circulation.

02

Dissect and draw the heart

Name chambers, valves, chordae and great vessels, measure wall thickness, and draw a labelled section to WAEC rules.

03

Trace any route

Follow blood through the 13-step double circuit, the hepatic portal system and the coronary circulation, always via the lungs.

04

Read the heartbeat

Describe the cardiac cycle, lub and dub, the SAN to Purkinje chain, and measure pulse, heart rate and cardiac output.

05

Compare the three vessels

Match wall, lumen, valves, pressure and speed to the job of each vessel, in a table with both columns filled.

06

Follow the fluid out and back

Explain tissue fluid, lymph and oedema using NFP = Pc − πc, and why blood crawls through capillaries.

07

Know blood and clotting

State plasma and cell contents, red cell adaptations, the clotting chain, and count red cells on a haemocytometer.

08

Match blood safely

Predict safe donors from antigens and antibodies, explain Rhesus, and link common circulatory disorders to a cause and a prevention.

The 6 calculation archetypes

Almost every number question on the heart and blood in WAEC, NECO and JAMB is one of these six. Each step has a note under it.

Type 1 · Lesson 1

Surface area : volume of a cube

  1. SA ÷ V = 6L² ÷ L³ = 6/LA cube has 6 square faces of area L²; its volume is L³.
  2. 6 ÷ 20 mm = 0.30 mm⁻¹Double the side and the ratio halves: big bodies have little surface for their size.
Type 2 · Lesson 4

Heart rate and cycle time

  1. 18 × 60 ÷ 15 = 72 bpm18 beats counted in 15 s: scale up to a full minute (60 s).
  2. T = 60 ÷ 72 = 0.83 sCycle time is the number of seconds in a minute divided by the heart rate.
Type 3 · Lesson 4

Cardiac output

  1. 68 × 70 cm³ = 4760 cm³ min⁻¹Heart rate times stroke volume (the blood pushed out by one beat).
  2. 4760 ÷ 1000 = 4.76 dm³ min⁻¹1 dm³ is 1000 cm³, so divide by 1000 to change the unit.
Type 4 · Lesson 5

Net filtration pressure

  1. NFP = Pc − πcBlood pressure pushes fluid out; the plasma proteins pull it in.
  2. 36 − 25 = +11 mmHg (out)Arterial end: positive, so fluid leaves the capillary.
  3. 16 − 25 = −9 mmHg (in)Venous end: negative, so fluid returns to the blood.
Type 5 · Lesson 5

Blood speed from flow and area

  1. Q = 2.4 × 35 = 84 cm³ s⁻¹Aorta: cross-section 2.4 cm² times speed 35 cm s⁻¹ gives the flow.
  2. v = 84 ÷ 2400 = 0.035 cm s⁻¹The same flow through 2400 cm² of capillaries moves far more slowly.
Type 6 · Lesson 6

Red cell count

  1. 480 ÷ 0.020 mm³ = 24 000 mm⁻³480 cells in 5 squares that hold 0.020 mm³ in all (diluted blood).
  2. 24 000 × 200 = 4.8 × 10⁶ mm⁻³Multiply by the dilution factor to get the count for undiluted blood.

05 / How Every Lesson Works

See it first, then explain it.

After a short Do Now warm-up, every lesson follows the same five-step inquiry loop, with a movement break at 45 minutes, so you test an idea before you memorise it.

  1. 1 · Wonder

    Watch the 3D story and pin your prediction

    Dr Aisha, Chidi, Mama Bisi, Baba Sule and the okada rider meet a real puzzle. Post your guess on the Wonder Wall.

  2. 2 · Investigate

    Work the 3D practical bench

    Cut cubes, dissect a heart, count a pulse, probe a capillary or type blood yourself, and record the results.

  3. 3 · Make sense

    Zoom in and build the rules

    See valves, vessels, cells and clots up close, then build the tables and rules from what you saw.

  4. 4 · Apply

    Worked examples, the Bridge and a quiz

    Step-by-step calculations, a case-solving challenge and 12 exam-style questions every lesson.

  5. 5 · Reflect

    Rate yourself and unlock solutions

    Rate each goal 1–4, do the blank-page recall, then try the 30-mark paper before opening the model answers.
