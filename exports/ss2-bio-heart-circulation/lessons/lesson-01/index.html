The agar-cube bench: how far does acid get in?

Agar made with dilute alkali and phenolphthalein is pink. Wherever acid reaches, it turns colourless. So the colourless edge shows how far a substance can spread in 8 minutes, and the pink core shows where it never arrived. Four cubes (5, 10, 20 and 30 mm) go into the same beaker of dilute hydrochloric acid.

Safety, as in a real lab: dilute acid is handled with forceps, never fingers. Cubes are cut on a tile with a sharp blade, away from your body, and eyes must be protected from the acid. Here the bench is a simulation, so you can focus on reading the results.

Model answers unlocks after you try
  1. Acid spreads into agar by diffusion, and in the same time (8 minutes) it moves about the same distance, 3.0 mm, whatever the size of the cube.
  2. The 5 mm cube. Its half-width is only 2.5 mm, less than 3.0 mm, so the acid reached the centre from every side.
  3. It halves, because SA : V = 6/L. For example 10 mm gives 0.60 mm⁻¹ and 20 mm gives 0.30 mm⁻¹.
  4. Cells deep inside the body, too far from the surface to be supplied in time by diffusion alone.
  5. It is a single tiny cell with a huge SA : V, so everything it needs diffuses in through its surface quickly enough.

3Make sense · 15 min

Why large animals need a transport system

Your cube results explain Dr Aisha's first clue. Let us name what you saw.

What is a transport system?

A transport system is the set of parts that carries materials in bulk between the places where they are taken in, made, used and removed. In a mammal it has three parts:

Pumpthe heart
Channelsarteries, capillaries and veins
Mediumblood (with lymph as a second fluid)

Why big animals need one

Four reasons: (1) their SA : V is small, so the surface cannot supply the whole volume; (2) most cells lie far from the surface, and diffusion is far too slow over long distances; (3) active animals burn oxygen and food quickly; (4) materials are made in one organ and used in another (hormones, urea, absorbed food).

SA : V = 6L² ÷ L³ = 6 ÷ L   every time the side L doubles, SA : V halves

Materials carried by the blood

  • 1oxygen
  • 2carbon dioxide
  • 3products of digestion (amino acids, glucose, glycerol and fatty acids)
  • 4water
  • 5mineral salts
  • 6hormones
  • 7urea
  • 8antibodies
  • 9heat

4Apply · Worked examples

Worked examples, step by step

Attempt each problem on paper first, then check the working one line at a time.

EXAMPLE 1

SA : V with a unit change

Find SA : V for a cube of side 2.0 cm, first in cm⁻¹ and then in mm⁻¹.

  1. Surface area: SA = 6L² = 6 × (2.0)² = 24 cm².
  2. Volume: V = L³ = (2.0)³ = 8.0 cm³.
  3. Ratio: SA : V = 24 ÷ 8.0 = 3.0 cm⁻¹ (check: 6 ÷ 2.0 = 3.0).
  4. Convert: 1 cm = 10 mm, so "per cm" is ten times "per mm": 3.0 cm⁻¹ = 3.0 ÷ 10 = 0.30 mm⁻¹.
3.0 cm⁻¹ = 0.30 mm⁻¹ (the same as the 20 mm bench cube)
EXAMPLE 2

Amoeba against Chidi

Treat an Amoeba as a cube of side 0.20 mm and Chidi's body as a cube of side 0.40 m. How many times larger is the Amoeba's SA : V?

  1. Same units first: 0.40 m × 1000 = 400 mm.
  2. Amoeba: 6 ÷ 0.20 = 30 mm⁻¹.
  3. Chidi: 6 ÷ 400 = 0.015 mm⁻¹.
  4. Compare: 30 ÷ 0.015 = 2000.
About 2000 times larger, so diffusion through the surface serves the Amoeba but cannot serve Chidi
EXAMPLE 3

Share of a cube reached by acid

In the bench, acid moved 3.0 mm into every face of the 20 mm cube. What percentage of the cube turned colourless?

  1. Pink centre side: 20 − (2 × 3.0) = 14 mm.
  2. Pink volume and whole cube: 14³ = 2744 mm³ and 20³ = 8000 mm³.
  3. Colourless volume: 8000 − 2744 = 5256 mm³.
  4. Percentage: 5256 ÷ 8000 × 100 = 65.7%.
65.7% colourless; about a third of the cube was still unreached

Part A · Home investigation: dye cubes

How far does colour travel into cubes of different sizes?

Home investigation

You need: a raw Irish potato or a piece of white yam, a kitchen knife and chopping board (an adult does the cutting), a 30 cm ruler, a cup of strong zobo drink or water with a few drops of food colouring, a spoon and a clock.

  1. Ask an adult to cut three cubes from the peeled potato or yam, of side 10 mm, 25 mm and 40 mm. Check each side with the ruler.
  2. Lower all three cubes into the cup of coloured liquid at the same moment, using a spoon, and make sure the liquid covers them.
  3. After 30 minutes, lift all three out together, rinse them quickly in clean water and pat them dry.
  4. Ask the adult to cut each cube in half through its centre. Measure the depth of the coloured edge on each cut face.
  5. For each cube, calculate SA : V, the volume still uncoloured, and the percentage of the volume that has been coloured.
  6. Wash your hands, the knife and the board, and throw the used cubes away; do not eat them.

Part B · Written paper (30 marks)

1. Definitions

3 marks

Define each term: (a) transport system; (b) open circulation; (c) double circulation.

Model solution and mark scheme unlocks after you try

Model solution: (a) A transport system is the set of organs and fluids that moves materials in bulk between the places where they are taken in or made and the places where they are used or removed. (1) (b) Open circulation is a system in which the blood (haemolymph) leaves the vessels and flows through a body cavity, the haemocoel, bathing the organs directly. (1) (c) Double circulation is a system in which blood passes through the heart twice during one complete trip round the body: once on the way to and from the lungs, and once on the way to and from the rest of the body. (1)

Mark scheme note: Equivalent wordings that keep the key ideas are credited. In (a) the idea of bulk movement between distant places is needed; "the circulation of blood" alone is too narrow. In (b) "blood flows slowly" alone scores zero; the mark is for blood outside vessels in a body cavity. In (c) "two circulations" without "through the heart twice per circuit" is not credited.

2. Why Chidi Needs a Transport System

4 marks

Give four reasons why a large, active animal such as Chidi cannot depend on diffusion alone to supply its cells.

Model solution and mark scheme unlocks after you try

Model solution: (1) Its ratio of surface area to volume is small, so the body surface is too little to supply the whole volume. (1) (2) Most of its cells are far from the surface, and diffusion over long distances is far too slow. (1) (3) It is very active, so its cells use oxygen and food quickly and need a fast supply. (1) (4) Many substances are made in one organ and used or removed in another (for example urea is made in the liver but removed by the kidneys; hormones act far from the gland), so they must be carried. (1)

Mark scheme note: "It is big" and "its surface area is small" repeat the same idea and earn one mark only; the credited point is the ratio. "Diffusion is slow" without "over long distances" is too vague. Saying the body surface area is smaller than an Amoeba's is wrong and is penalised.

3. The Parts of the System and What They Carry

4 marks

(a) Name the pump, the channels and the medium of the mammalian transport system. (b) Name one waste that blood carries from the liver to the kidneys.

Model solution and mark scheme unlocks after you try

Model solution: (a) Pump: the heart. (1) Channels: the blood vessels (arteries, capillaries and veins). (1) Medium: blood (lymph is accepted as a second medium). (1) (b) Urea. (1)

Mark scheme note: Each part scores only in its own slot; "blood" written as the pump scores zero for that slot. In (b) "urine" is not accepted: urine is made in the kidney, so it is not carried there by the blood.

4. Surface Area to Volume Ratio

5 marks

An agar cube has a side of 0.80 cm.

(a) Calculate its surface area in mm². (b) Calculate its volume in mm³. (c) Calculate its SA : V in mm⁻¹. (d) Express the SA : V in cm⁻¹. (e) A second cube has a side of 16 mm. State what happens to SA : V when the side is doubled, and give its new value.

Model solution and mark scheme unlocks after you try

Model solution: (a) Side = 0.80 cm × 10 = 8.0 mm; SA = 6L² = 6 × 8.0² = 384 mm². (1) (b) V = L³ = 8.0³ = 512 mm³. (1) (c) SA : V = 384 ÷ 512 = 0.75 mm⁻¹. (1) (d) 1 cm = 10 mm, so a ratio per cm is ten times the ratio per mm: 0.75 × 10 = 7.5 cm⁻¹ (check: 6 ÷ 0.80 = 7.5). (1) (e) It halves: 6 ÷ 16 = 0.375 mm⁻¹. (1)

Mark scheme note: Work carried forward is allowed from (a) to (c). Leaving the side as 0.80 in (a) and writing "3.84 mm²" loses the mark (no conversion). In (d), dividing by 10 (giving 0.075 cm⁻¹) is the commonest slip. Ratios given with no unit, or with mm² or mm³ as the unit, lose the mark in (c), (d) and (e).

5. Practical: Reading the Cube Results

5 marks

In a school version of the cube bench, acid moved 2.5 mm into every face of an 18 mm cube in 8 minutes.

(a) Calculate the SA : V of the cube. (b) Find the side of the pink centre that remained. (c) Calculate the volume of the pink centre. (d) Calculate the percentage of the cube's volume that turned colourless. (e) State what the pink centre represents in a large animal.

Model solution and mark scheme unlocks after you try

Model solution: (a) SA : V = 6 ÷ 18 = 0.33 mm⁻¹. (1) (b) Pink centre side = 18 − (2 × 2.5) = 13 mm. (1) (c) Pink volume = 13³ = 2197 mm³. (1) (d) Whole cube = 18³ = 5832 mm³; colourless share = (5832 − 2197) ÷ 5832 × 100 = 62.3%. (1) (e) It represents cells deep inside the body, too far from the surface for diffusion to supply them in time. (1)

Mark scheme note: Subtracting 2.5 mm only once in (b) (centre 15.5 mm) loses (b) but later marks may be earned on error carried forward. Giving the pink share (37.7%) in (d) is not credited. "Dead cells" in (e) is not accepted; the idea is cells that diffusion cannot reach fast enough.

6. Practical: Precautions and Errors

3 marks

(a) State two precautions that were taken in the agar-cube experiment. (b) A student lifted one cube out of the acid two minutes late. State and explain the effect on its percentage colourless.

Model solution and mark scheme unlocks after you try

Model solution: (a) Any two, in the past tense: all the cubes were lowered into the acid at the same moment and lifted out together (1); the acid fully covered every cube (1); each cube was checked with a ruler after cutting (1); the cubes were handled with forceps and the eyes were protected from the acid (1). (Maximum 2.) (b) Its % colourless was too high, because the acid had extra time to move further into that cube. (1)

Mark scheme note: Precautions written as commands ("cover the cubes") or in the future tense lose the marks. General rules such as "a lab coat was worn" are not specific to this experiment and are not accepted. In (b), the direction (too high) and the reason (extra time) must both be given for the mark.

8. Solving the First Clue

2 marks

Baba Sule says a heart that pumps blood only once per circuit, as in a fish, would serve Chidi just as well. State two advantages of Chidi's double circulation that prove him wrong.

Model solution and mark scheme unlocks after you try

Model solution: (1) Blood coming back from the lungs is pumped a second time, so it reaches Chidi's working muscles at high pressure and fast. (1) (2) The four-chambered heart with its septum keeps oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood apart, so the blood sent to his muscles is fully oxygenated. (1)

Mark scheme note: "It is faster" without a reason linked to the second pumping is not credited. "No mixing" must refer to oxygenated and deoxygenated blood; "blood does not mix with lymph" scores zero.
