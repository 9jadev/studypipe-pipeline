The Unit Mystery · Ngwa Road, Aba

Why does Chinedu’s 12.0 V battery plunge to 8.4 V the moment the load is switched on?

At Chinedu’s workshop in Aba, apprentice Kelechi tests a newly charged 12.0 V lead-acid accumulator with a digital voltmeter while the main switch is open: it reads a steady 12.0 V.

Yet the instant he closes the brass changeover switch to drive a 2.8 Ω starter motor load, the workshop ceiling lamp dims and the voltmeter across the battery terminals drops sharply to 8.4 V! Even more puzzling: when his supervisor Engr. Nkechi tests the exact same battery on a 100 cm constantan potentiometer board, the null-balance point reports the full 12.0 V without losing a single tenth of a volt.

Where do the missing 3.6 V disappear to when current flows? Why do parallel cells keep one cell’s voltage while cutting internal resistance? How do we size a shunt or multiplier for a 10 mA galvanometer? And why does a potentiometer at null balance (I_g = 0) eliminate internal-resistance error completely?

At constant temperature, the resistance R of a constantan wire stays constant, so potential difference V is strictly proportional to current I. The gradient of V (vertical) against I (horizontal) gives R directly in ohms (Ω).

The 6 calculation archetypes

Over 90% of WAEC, NECO, and JAMB numerical problems in Current Electricity follow one of these six patterns:

Type 1 · Lesson 1

Resistivity & Diameter Ratios

Given length L and diameter d, use R = 4ρL/(πd²) or ratio R₁/R₂ = (L₁/L₂)(d₂/d₁)².

Type 2 · Lesson 2

Two Readings, Same Cell

When load R₁ draws I₁ and load R₂ draws I₂, equate I₁(R₁ + r) = I₂(R₂ + r) to find r and E.

Type 3 · Lesson 3

Parallel Cells & Networks

For n identical parallel cells, use E_eq = E and r_eq = r/n, then I = E_eq/(R + r_eq).

Type 4 · Lesson 3

Power & Cell Efficiency

Use P = I²R = V²/R and cell efficiency η = [R / (R + r)] × 100% = (V / E) × 100%.

Type 5 · Lesson 4

Shunt & Multiplier Sizing

Parallel ammeter shunt R_s = I_g R_g / (I − I_g); series voltmeter multiplier R_m = (V / I_g) − R_g.

Type 6 · Lesson 5

Metre Bridge & Potentiometer

Bridge: R_L/R_R = l/(100 − l). Potentiometer: E₁/E₂ = l₁/l₂ and r = R(l₁ − l₂)/l₂.
