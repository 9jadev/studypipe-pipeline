01 / The Phenomenon

A battery that reads 12.0 V at rest but only 8.4 V at work.

Every lesson in this unit closes one piece of the same case file, from the keke on Ngwa Road to the potentiometer on Engr. Nkechi’s bench.

The Unit Mystery · Ngwa Road, Aba

The Ngwa Road Keke Battery Drop

Emeka, a keke rider on Ngwa Road, drops off a battery that was charged last week but now turns his engine weakly. At Engr. Nkechi’s workshop, her apprentice Chinedu clips a voltmeter across the resting battery: 12.0 V, normal-looking.

The instant he switches in the starter test load, the reading drops to 8.4 V and the lamp dims. Tobe, who sells batteries next door, says it is finished: “Dump it!” Nkechi shakes her head: “Measure it a cleverer way before you throw it away.”

Where do the missing 3.6 volts go, and is the battery really dead?

  • Clue 1Resting reading: 12.0 V.
  • Clue 2Under-load reading: 8.4 V.
  • Clue 3The lamp dims only while current flows.
  • Clue 4On the potentiometer, where no current is drawn, it still balances at 12.0 V.

Realism note: a real keke starter motor can draw well over a hundred amperes for a moment. This unit uses Nkechi’s 2.8 Ω starter test load (relay and headlamp), which draws 3.0 A, so every number is easy to follow. The physics, V = E − Ir, is exactly the same.

04 / Learning Outcomes

What you will master by the end of Lesson 5.

Every skill is practised on a 3D bench, plotted on a live graph and tested with WAEC, NECO and JAMB-style questions.

01

Measure resistance

Define current and p.d., state Ohm’s law and its condition, and find a resistance from the slope of a V–I graph.

02

Size up a wire

Use R = ρL/A and R ∝ L/d², and tell ohmic conductors from non-ohmic devices by their graphs.

03

Find the hidden r

Use E = I(R + r) and V = E − Ir, the two-readings method, and a graph’s intercept and gradient.

04

Collapse any network

Combine resistors and cells in series and in parallel, and find branch currents and potential drops.

05

Count the power

Calculate P = IV = I²R = V²/R, kilowatt-hours, and efficiency η = R/(R + r).

06

Convert a galvanometer

Size a shunt for an ammeter and a multiplier for a voltmeter, and tell the total resistance from the resistor you add.

07

Balance a bridge

Use R/S = l/(100 − l) on the metre bridge to measure an unknown coil exactly.

08

Read the true EMF

Use the potentiometer, E₁/E₂ = l₁/l₂ and r = R(l₁ − l₂)/l₂, and write precautions in the past tense.

The 6 calculation archetypes

Almost every number question on current electricity in WAEC, NECO and JAMB is one of these six. Each step has a note under it.

Type 1 · Lesson 1

Ohm’s law, power and charge

  1. I = V/R = 240 ÷ 48 = 5.0 AA 48 Ω pressing-iron element on the 240 V mains.
  2. P = IV = 5.0 × 240 = 1200 WPower is the current times the p.d. across the element.
  3. Q = It = 5.0 × 120 = 600 CTwo minutes is 120 s; charge is current times time.
Type 2 · Lesson 1

Resistance of a wire

  1. ρ = RA/L = (4.40 × 5.0 × 10⁻⁷) ÷ 2.00Rearrange R = ρL/A to make the resistivity the subject.
  2. ρ = 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Ω mA typical value for nichrome heating wire.
  3. R₂ = 4.40 × (3.00/2.00) × (1/2)² = 1.65 ΩR ∝ L/d²: 1.5 times longer, but twice the diameter quarters it.
Type 3 · Lesson 2

EMF and internal resistance

  1. I = V/R = 8.4 ÷ 2.8 = 3.0 AThe terminal p.d. across the 2.8 Ω starter test load gives the current.
  2. lost volts = E − V = 12.0 − 8.4 = 3.6 VThe volts spent inside the battery.
  3. r = 3.6 ÷ 3.0 = 1.2 ΩLost volts = Ir, so divide by the current.
Type 4 · Lesson 3

Networks and cell arrays

  1. R_p = (6.0 × 12.0)/(6.0 + 12.0) = 4.0 ΩProduct over sum for two resistors in parallel.
  2. I = 14.0 ÷ (3.0 + 4.0 + 1.0) = 1.75 AAdd the series 3.0 Ω and the internal resistance 1.0 Ω.
  3. η = R/(R + r) = 4.5 ÷ 5.0 = 90 %Efficiency: the share of the energy delivered to the load.
Type 5 · Lesson 4

Shunt and multiplier

  1. R_s = (0.015 × 18.0) ÷ (3.0 − 0.015) = 0.0905 ΩEqual p.d. across coil and shunt: I_g R_g = (I − I_g) R_s.
  2. R_m = 40 ÷ 0.015 − 18 = 2649 ΩTotal resistance V/I_g, minus the coil’s own R_g.
Type 6 · Lesson 5

Null methods

  1. R = 7.0 × 44.0 ÷ 56.0 = 5.50 ΩMetre bridge: R_left/R_right = l/(100 − l), balance at 56.0 cm.
  2. r = 3.0 × (75.0 − 60.0) ÷ 60.0 = 0.75 ΩPotentiometer: balance on open circuit, then across a known R.
