1 · Metre bridge

Balance two ratios

A metre bridge is a Wheatstone bridge built from a uniform 100 cm wire. Slide the jockey until the galvanometer reads zero (the null point). The two wire lengths then have the same ratio as the two resistors.

Null pointR1 ÷ R2 = l ÷ (100 − l)

ExampleStandard R2 = 6.0 Ω, balance at l = 40.0 cm: R1 = 6.0 × 40/60 = 4.0 Ω.

Two-wire practicalFor two wires of the same material and length, slope s = R2/R1 = (d1/d2)², so √s = d1 ÷ d2.

Exam trapMeasure l from the end next to R1. Readings are most accurate when the null point is near the middle (about 50 cm).

2 · Potentiometer

Measure EMF while drawing zero current

A steady current in a uniform wire makes the voltage along it proportional to length (V = kl). At the null point no current is drawn from the test cell, so there are no lost volts (Ir = 0) and you read its true EMF.

Compare cellsE1 ÷ E2 = l1 ÷ l2
Internal rr = R(l1 − l2) ÷ l2

ExampleE1 = 1.50 V balances at 50.0 cm and E2 at 70.0 cm, so E2 = 1.50 × 70/50 = 2.10 V.

Exam trapIn the r formula, l1 is the balance with the cell alone (the longer length), and l2 is with resistor R connected across the cell (shorter).

3 · Driver-resistor trap

When the driver cell has a resistor in series

If a resistor Rs is in series with the potentiometer wire (resistance Rw), the driver cell’s voltage is shared. First find the voltage across the whole wire, then scale it by the balance length.

Whole wireVAB = Edriver × Rw ÷ (Rw + Rs)
Unknown EMFEx = (l ÷ 100) × VAB

ExampleEdriver = 3 V, Rs = 2 Ω, Rw = 10 Ω, l = 50 cm: VAB = 3 × 10/12 = 2.5 V, so Ex = 0.5 × 2.5 = 1.25 V.

Exam trapDon’t put the full 3 V across the wire. Part of it is dropped across Rs.

4 · WAEC Paper 3 precautions

Write them in first person, past tense

  1. “I ensured tight and clean electrical connections.”
  2. “I did not drag the jockey along the wire, to keep its cross-sectional area uniform.”
  3. “I opened the key after each reading to prevent the wire heating up.”
  4. “I avoided parallax error when reading the metre rule.”

Why they earn marksClean contacts avoid extra resistance. A uniform wire keeps V proportional to l. A hot wire changes its resistance. No parallax means correct lengths.

Step-by-step WAEC, NECO & JAMB calculations

01Metre Bridge Unknown Resistance & Parallel Shunt Change (WAEC / JAMB)X = 8.0 Ω

Question: An unknown resistor X is connected in the left gap of a metre bridge and a standard 12.0 Ω resistor in the right gap. A null point is obtained at l = 40.0 cm from the left end. Calculate the resistance of X.

  1. Metre bridge equation: X ÷ R2 = l ÷ (100 − l)
  2. Substitute values: X ÷ 12.0 = 40.0 ÷ (100 − 40.0) = 40.0 ÷ 60.0 = 2 ÷ 3
  3. Solve for X: X = 12.0 × (2 ÷ 3) = 8.0 Ω
02WAEC Paper 3 Two-Wire Metre Bridge Deduction (√s = d₁/d₂) (WAEC Practical Classic)d₁ : d₂ = 1.80 : 1

Question: In a metre bridge practical comparing two nichrome wires 1 and 2 of equal length, a student plots values to obtain a slope s = R2 ÷ R1 = 3.24. Evaluate √s and state its physical significance.

  1. Evaluate √s: √s = √3.24 = 1.80
  2. Physical deduction: Since both wires have the same resistivity ρ and length L, R = 4ρL ÷ (πd2) ∝ 1 ÷ d2
  3. Therefore s = R2 ÷ R1 = (d1 ÷ d2)2  ⇒  √s = d1 ÷ d2 = 1.80 (Wire 1 has 1.80 times the diameter of Wire 2).
03Potentiometer Driver Circuit with Series Resistor (JAMB & WAEC Favourite)E_x = 1.25 V

Question: A potentiometer wire AB of length 100 cm and resistance 10.0 Ω is connected in series with a driver accumulator of EMF 3.0 V (negligible internal resistance) and a 2.0 Ω resistor. If a test cell balances at l = 50.0 cm along AB, find the EMF of the test cell.

  1. Step 1 — Potential difference across the entire 100 cm wire AB:
    VAB = Edriver × [Rw ÷ (Rw + Rs)] = 3.0 × [10.0 ÷ (10.0 + 2.0)] = 30.0 ÷ 12.0 = 2.50 V
  2. Step 2 — Balance p.d. at l = 50.0 cm:
    Ex = (l ÷ 100) × VAB = (50.0 ÷ 100) × 2.50 V = 1.25 V
04Determining Internal Resistance r of a Cell with a Potentiometer (WAEC / NECO)r = 1.00 Ω

Question: On a potentiometer, a dry cell gives a balance length of l1 = 75.0 cm on open circuit. When the cell is shunted by a 4.0 Ω resistor, the balance length falls to l2 = 60.0 cm. Calculate the internal resistance r of the cell.

  1. Formula: Since E ∝ l1 and terminal p.d. V ∝ l2, r = R × [(E − V) ÷ V] = R × [(l1 − l2) ÷ l2]
  2. Substitute values: r = 4.0 × [(75.0 − 60.0) ÷ 60.0] = 4.0 × (15.0 ÷ 60.0) = 1.00 Ω

Task 1 · Metre Bridge & Two-Wire Diameter Ratio

In a metre bridge experiment, an unknown wire coil R1 in the left gap balances against a standard 9.0 Ω resistor in the right gap at l = 40.0 cm. (a) Calculate R1 [4]. (b) If a second wire R2 of the same material and length has resistance 13.5 Ω, calculate the slope s = R2/R1 and deduce the ratio of their diameters d1 : d2 [4]. (c) State two WAEC precautions [2].

Requires ≥ 40 characters of genuine working

Official Marking Scheme (10 Marks)

  • (a) Value of R1 [4 marks]:
    R1 ÷ 9.0 = 40.0 ÷ (100 − 40.0) = 40 ÷ 60 = 2 ÷ 3 [2] → R1 = 6.0 Ω [2].
  • (b) Slope s and diameter ratio [4 marks]:
    s = R2 ÷ R1 = 13.5 ÷ 6.0 = 2.25 [2].
    Since √s = d1 ÷ d2, d1 ÷ d2 = √2.25 = 1.50 (or 3 : 2) [2].
  • (c) Two WAEC precautions [2 marks]:
    1. I did not drag the jockey along the constantan wire. [1]
    2. I ensured tight electrical connections and opened the key between readings. [1]
10 Marks

Task 2 · Potentiometer Driver Voltage Divider & Internal Resistance

A 100 cm potentiometer wire of resistance 8.0 Ω is connected in series with a 4.0 V driver accumulator and a 2.0 Ω series resistor. (a) Find the potential difference VAB across the 100 cm wire [3]. (b) A dry cell balances at l1 = 50.0 cm on open circuit; calculate its EMF [3]. (c) When shunted by a 3.0 Ω resistor, the cell balances at l2 = 40.0 cm; calculate the cell’s internal resistance r [4].

Requires ≥ 40 characters of genuine working

Official Marking Scheme (10 Marks)

  • (a) Wire p.d. VAB [3 marks]:
    VAB = 4.0 × [8.0 ÷ (8.0 + 2.0)] = 32.0 ÷ 10.0 = 3.20 V [3].
  • (b) Open-circuit EMF [3 marks]:
    E = (50.0 ÷ 100.0) × 3.20 V = 1.60 V [3].
  • (c) Internal resistance r [4 marks]:
    r = R × [(l1 − l2) ÷ l2] = 3.0 × [(50.0 − 40.0) ÷ 40.0] = 3.0 × 0.25 = 0.75 Ω [4].
