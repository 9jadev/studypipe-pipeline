3Make sense · 12 min

Ohm’s Law, Resistivity & Wire Dimensions

Let’s connect your 3D bench observations to the exact equations tested in WAEC, NECO, and JAMB Physics.

1 · Ohm’s Law

Ohm’s law states that the electric current I flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference V across its ends, provided temperature and other physical conditions (such as mechanical strain) remain constant:

V ∝ I   ⇒   V = IR     or     R = V ÷ I  (in ohms, Ω)

2 · Factors Affecting Resistance & Resistivity (ρ)

For a uniform cylindrical wire at constant temperature, electrical resistance R:

  • is directly proportional to its length L (R ∝ L — a longer wire offers a longer path of lattice collisions);
  • is inversely proportional to its cross-sectional area A (R ∝ 1/A — a wider wire acts like a multi-lane highway);
  • depends on the nature of the material (its electrical resistivity ρ, measured in ohm-metres, Ω m);
  • increases with temperature T for pure metals (and decreases with temperature for semiconductors and electrolytes).
R = ρL ÷ A = 4ρL ÷ (πd2)   since circular area A = πr2 = πd2 ÷ 4
Ratio form for two wires of the same material:   R1 ÷ R2 = (L1 ÷ L2) × (d2 ÷ d1)2
Electrical conductivity:   σ = 1 ÷ ρ   (SI unit: Ω−1 m−1 or S m−1)

Worked examples, step by step

Attempt each problem on paper first, then check the step-by-step WAEC/JAMB working.

EXAMPLE 1

Ohm’s law, current & total electric charge flow

A constantan coil of resistance 8.0 Ω is connected across a steady potential difference of 12.0 V for 2.5 minutes. Calculate: (a) the current flowing through the coil, and (b) the total quantity of electric charge that passes through a cross-section of the coil in that time.

  1. Current I from Ohm’s law: I = V ÷ R = 12.0 ÷ 8.0 = 1.50 A.
  2. Convert time to SI unit (seconds): t = 2.5 × 60 = 150 s.
  3. Total charge Q: Q = I × t = 1.50 × 150 = 225 C.
(a) I = 1.50 A  ·  (b) Q = 225 C
EXAMPLE 2

Calculating resistivity ρ and diameter scaling

A uniform resistance wire of length 1.50 m and diameter 0.40 mm has a resistance of 3.60 Ω. Calculate: (a) the resistivity ρ of the alloy, and (b) the resistance of a second wire of the same alloy having length 3.00 m and diameter 0.80 mm. (Take π = 3.142)

  1. Convert diameter to metres & find area A: d = 0.40 mm = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ m. Area A = πd² ÷ 4 = 3.142 × (4.0 × 10⁻⁴)² ÷ 4 = 1.257 × 10⁻⁷ m².
  2. Resistivity ρ: ρ = RA ÷ L = (3.60 × 1.257 × 10⁻⁷) ÷ 1.50 = 3.02 × 10⁻⁷ Ω m.
  3. Second wire resistance R₂: using R₂ ÷ R₁ = (L₂ ÷ L₁) × (d₁ ÷ d₂)² = (3.00 ÷ 1.50) × (0.40 ÷ 0.80)² = 2 × (0.5)² = 0.50. Therefore R₂ = 0.50 × 3.60 = 1.80 Ω.
(a) ρ = 3.02 × 10−7 Ω m  ·  (b) R2 = 1.80 Ω
EXAMPLE 3

WAEC Paper 3 V–I graph slope & precautions

In an Ohm’s law practical, a candidate plots potential difference V (vertical axis) against current I (horizontal axis). Two widely separated points on the line of best fit are (0.25 A, 0.55 V) and (1.15 A, 2.53 V). Determine the resistance R of the wire from the slope and state two acceptable precautions.

  1. Slope S = ΔV ÷ ΔI: S = (2.53 − 0.55) ÷ (1.15 − 0.25) = 1.98 ÷ 0.90 = 2.20 Ω.
  2. Precautions (past tense): (i) “I removed the plug key immediately after each reading so the wire did not heat up.” (ii) “I ensured tight and clean electrical connections at all terminals.”
Resistance R = Slope = 2.20 Ω

Exit ticket

In two sentences: why does doubling the diameter of a wire divide its resistance by 4 instead of 2, and why must the plug key be opened between readings in an Ohm's law practical?

2 marks
Model answer unlocks after you try

Cross-sectional area is proportional to diameter squared (A = πd²/4), so doubling d quadruples A and divides R = ρL/A by 4 (1). Opening the plug key between readings prevents continuous current from heating the wire and increasing its resistance (1).

Assignment: Ohm’s Law & Resistivity

Complete Part A (home cable audit) and Part B (30-mark structured WAEC paper). Type your attempt first to unlock each model solution.

Part A · Home Appliance Cable Audit

Compare the flexible power cord of a high-power pressing iron or electric kettle (≈ 1500–2000 W) with a phone charger cable. Why must the pressing iron cord use much thicker copper cores? Explain using R = ρL/A and Joule heating I²R.

Practical task
Sample answer unlocks after you try

A pressing iron draws a large current (8–10 A). Using a thick copper core (large cross-sectional area A) keeps cable resistance R = ρL/A very low so the cord does not overheat (P = I²R) or melt its insulation.

Part B · Structured Written Questions (30 marks)

1. (a) State Ohm's law. (b) Explain using the free-electron lattice model why a tungsten filament lamp disobeys Ohm's law at high voltages. (c) A constantan wire of length 2.00 m and diameter 0.50 mm carries a current of 0.40 A when connected across a 1.60 V cell. Calculate its resistance and resistivity.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Current I through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to potential difference V across its ends, provided temperature and other physical conditions remain constant (2).
(b) At high voltage and current, the tungsten filament heats up; positive lattice ions vibrate with larger amplitude, colliding more frequently with drifting electrons and increasing resistance R (3).
(c) R = V ÷ I = 1.60 ÷ 0.40 = 4.00 Ω (2). Area A = πd²/4 = 3.142 × (5.0 × 10⁻⁴)² ÷ 4 = 1.964 × 10⁻⁷ m² (1). Resistivity ρ = RA ÷ L = (4.00 × 1.964 × 10⁻⁷) ÷ 2.00 = 3.93 × 10⁻⁷ Ω m (2).

2. Two wires P and Q made of the same alloy have lengths in the ratio 3 : 1 and diameters in the ratio 2 : 1. If the resistance of wire P is 6.0 Ω, calculate the resistance of wire Q.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

Using R = 4ρL ÷ (πd²), for the same alloy (same ρ): R_P ÷ R_Q = (L_P ÷ L_Q) × (d_Q ÷ d_P)² (4).
Substitute L_P/L_Q = 3/1 and d_Q/d_P = 1/2: 6.0 ÷ R_Q = 3 × (1/2)² = 3/4 = 0.75 (4).
Therefore R_Q = 6.0 ÷ 0.75 = 8.0 Ω (2).

3. In a WAEC Paper 3 Ohm's law practical, a student records (I = 0.20 A, V = 0.44 V) and (I = 1.00 A, V = 2.20 V). (a) Calculate the gradient of the V–I graph and state what physical quantity it represents. (b) State two acceptable precautions in the past tense.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Gradient S = (2.20 − 0.44) ÷ (1.00 − 0.20) = 1.76 ÷ 0.80 = 2.20 Ω (4). It represents the electrical resistance R of the conductor (2).
(b) 1. “I ensured tight and clean electrical connections at all terminals.” (2)
2. “I removed the plug key immediately after each reading to prevent heating of the resistance wire.” (2)
