Practical analysis questions

Answer these five questions, then compare with the model answers.

  1. Why does the wire give a straight line but the lamp a curve?
  2. Why must the plug key be opened between readings?
  3. How does the slope of the graph give the resistance?
  4. If the wire's length is doubled, what happens to the slope?
  5. If its diameter is doubled, what happens to the resistance?
Model answers unlocks after you try
  1. The wire keeps a constant resistance at steady temperature, so V ÷ I is constant and the graph is straight. The lamp's filament heats as the current rises, its resistance grows, and the V–I graph curves.
  2. So the current does not heat the wire and change its resistance; a warm wire would push the later points up and make the slope too high.
  3. Because V = IR, a graph of V (vertical) against I (horizontal) has gradient ΔV ÷ ΔI = R, read from a large triangle on the best-fit line.
  4. R ∝ L, so doubling the length doubles the resistance, and the slope of the V–I line doubles.
  5. A = πd²/4, so doubling the diameter quadruples the area and the resistance falls to one quarter.
3Make sense · 15 min

Current, p.d. and resistance

Now connect what you measured to the definitions and equations WAEC, NECO and JAMB expect.

Movement break (45 min mark, 5 minutes): stand up. Walk slowly on the spot to be an electron drifting through a cool wire. Now shake your shoulders fast, like hot lattice ions — feel how much harder it is to pass through without colliding. Do it twice, then sit down.

Definitions

Current is the rate at which charge flows past a point, I = Q/t (amperes). Potential difference between two points is the electrical energy changed to other forms per coulomb that passes, V = W/Q (volts). Resistance is a component's opposition to current, R = V/I (ohms).

Ohm's law: for a metallic conductor kept at a steady temperature and physical condition, the current through it is directly proportional to the p.d. across it, so V/I is constant and equals its resistance R. A filament lamp, a diode or an electrolyte do not keep this ratio constant, so they are non-ohmic.

I = Q/t  ·  V = IR  ·  R = ρL/A  with A = πd²/4, so R ∝ L/d²  ·  P = IV = I²R = V²/R

Worked examples, step by step

Try each on paper first, then check the working.

EXAMPLE 1

Current, power and charge

A workshop pressing-iron element of resistance 48 Ω runs from the 240 V mains. Find (a) the current, (b) its power, and (c) the charge that flows in 2.0 minutes.

  1. Current: I = V/R = 240 ÷ 48 = 5.0 A.
  2. Power: P = IV = 5.0 × 240 = 1 200 W = 1.2 kW.
  3. Charge: t = 2.0 min = 120 s, so Q = It = 5.0 × 120 = 600 C.
I = 5.0 A · P = 1.2 kW · Q = 600 C
EXAMPLE 2

Resistivity, then a second wire

A nichrome heating wire is 2.00 m long with area 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ m², and a V–I test gives it a resistance of 4.40 Ω. (a) Find the resistivity. (b) A second wire of the same material is 3.00 m long but twice the diameter; find its resistance.

  1. Resistivity: ρ = RA/L = (4.40 × 5.0 × 10⁻⁷) ÷ 2.00.
  2. = (2.20 × 10⁻⁶) ÷ 2.00 = 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m.
  3. Second wire: twice the diameter is four times the area, so R₂ = R₁ × (L₂/L₁) × (d₁/d₂)² = 4.40 × (3.00/2.00) × (1/2)².
  4. = 4.40 × 1.50 × 0.25 = 1.65 Ω.
ρ = 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m · R₂ = 1.65 Ω
EXAMPLE 3

Resistance from a V–I graph

A V–I graph for a wire passes through (0.20 A, 0.44 V) and (1.00 A, 2.20 V) on its line of best fit. Find the resistance.

  1. Choose two well-separated points on the line.
  2. ΔV = 2.20 − 0.44 = 1.76 V; ΔI = 1.00 − 0.20 = 0.80 A.
  3. R = slope = ΔV/ΔI = 1.76 ÷ 0.80 = 2.20 Ω.
R = 2.20 Ω (straight line through the origin, so the wire is ohmic)

Part A · Home investigation

Pencil-line resistors: shade a narrow and a wide graphite strip of the same length, measure the resistance with a multimeter at 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 cm, and plot R against L for both. What do the two lines show?

Practical task
Sample answer unlocks after you try

For each strip, R rises in proportion to length, so R against L is roughly a straight line through the origin (R ∝ L). The wide strip, with about three times the area, has about one third of the narrow strip's resistance at each length (R ∝ 1/A). Like Emeka's thin jumper cable, the narrow strip has the smaller cross-section, the larger resistance, and heats more at the same current.

Part B · Written paper (30 marks)

b1. Define (a) electric current; (b) potential difference between two points; (c) resistance of a conductor.

3 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Current is the rate at which charge flows past a point, I = Q/t; unit the ampere. (1)
(b) The p.d. between two points is the electrical energy changed to other forms per coulomb that moves between them, V = W/Q; unit the volt. (1)
(c) Resistance is the ratio of the p.d. across a conductor to the current through it, R = V/I; unit the ohm. (1)

b4. A uniform wire has resistivity ρ, length L and diameter d. (a) Show that R = 4ρL/(πd²). (b) Hence show that twice the diameter gives one quarter of the resistance.

4 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) The cross-section is a circle, A = π(d/2)² = πd²/4. (1) Substituting into R = ρL/A gives R = ρL ÷ (πd²/4) = 4ρL/(πd²). (1)
(b) With d₂ = 2d, the area is π(2d)²/4 = 4 × πd²/4, four times as large. (1) So R₂ = 4ρL/(π(2d)²) = 4ρL/(4πd²) = R/4. (1)

b5. A bench heater needs a 6.0 Ω coil of constantan (ρ = 4.9 × 10⁻⁷ Ω m), diameter 0.50 mm, across 12.0 V. Find (a) the area, (b) the length, (c) the current, (d) the power, (e) the charge in 2.5 min.

5 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) A = πd²/4 = π × (5.0 × 10⁻⁴)² ÷ 4 = 1.96 × 10⁻⁷ m². (1)
(b) L = RA/ρ = (6.0 × 1.96 × 10⁻⁷) ÷ (4.9 × 10⁻⁷) = 2.40 m. (1)
(c) I = V/R = 12.0 ÷ 6.0 = 2.0 A. (1)
(d) P = IV = 2.0 × 12.0 = 24 W. (1)
(e) t = 150 s; Q = It = 2.0 × 150 = 300 C. (1)

b6. A wire of length 0.80 m and diameter 0.30 mm gives: I/A 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50 and V/V 0.36, 0.71, 1.09, 1.43, 1.80. (a) Which axis is V? (b) How does the graph show it is ohmic? (c) Find R from a large triangle. (d) Find the resistivity.

6 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) V on the vertical axis, I on the horizontal. (1)
(b) The points lie on a straight line through the origin, so V ∝ I (V/I constant). (1)
(c) Using (0.05 A, 0.18 V) and (0.50 A, 1.80 V): slope = (1.80 − 0.18) ÷ (0.50 − 0.05) = 1.62 ÷ 0.45 (1) → R = 3.6 Ω. (1)
(d) A = πd²/4 = π × (3.0 × 10⁻⁴)² ÷ 4 = 7.07 × 10⁻⁸ m². (1) ρ = RA/L = (3.6 × 7.07 × 10⁻⁸) ÷ 0.80 = 3.2 × 10⁻⁷ Ω m. (1)
