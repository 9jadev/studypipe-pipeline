Galvanometer Conversion: Ammeter Shunts & Voltmeter Multipliers

How can a delicate moving-coil galvanometer that burns out above 10 mA safely measure a 5.0 A starter current or a 15.0 V battery?

  • 01Define full-scale deflection current (Ig), coil resistance (Rg), and full-scale coil voltage Vg = IgRg.
  • 02Convert a galvanometer into an ammeter using a low-resistance parallel shunt Rs = IgRg ÷ (I − Ig).
  • 03Convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter using a high-resistance series multiplier Rm = (V ÷ Ig) − Rg.
  • 04Distinguish between the added multiplier resistance (Rm) and the total voltmeter resistance (Rtotal = Rg + Rm = V ÷ Ig).
  • 05Explain why ammeters go in series (ideal RA = 0) while voltmeters go in parallel (ideal RV = ∞).

1 · The galvanometer

Start with the sensitive coil

A moving-coil galvanometer is a very sensitive meter. It reaches full-scale deflection with a tiny current Ig through a coil of resistance Rg. Push more current through it and the coil can burn out.

Full-scale p.d.Vg = IgRg

ExampleIg = 10 mA and Rg = 20 Ω, so Vg = 0.010 × 20 = 0.20 V.

Exam trapConvert milliamperes to amperes before you multiply: 10 mA = 0.010 A.

2 · Ammeter conversion

Ammeter: a small shunt in parallel

To measure a large current I, connect a low-resistance shunt Rs in parallel with the coil. The shunt carries the extra current (I − Ig) around the coil. Parallel parts share the same voltage, so:

Same p.d.IgRg = (I − Ig)Rs
ShuntRs = IgRg ÷ (I − Ig)

Example0–5 A from a 10 mA, 20 Ω coil: Rs = 0.20 ÷ 4.99 = 0.0401 Ω.

RememberThe finished ammeter goes in series with the load. An ideal ammeter has resistance close to 0 Ω.

3 · Voltmeter conversion

Voltmeter: a large multiplier in series

To measure a large voltage V, connect a high-resistance multiplier Rm in series with the coil. The same current Ig flows through both, and together they must take the full voltage V:

Same IV = Ig(Rg + Rm)
MultiplierRm = (V ÷ Ig) − Rg

Example0–15 V from a 10 mA, 20 Ω coil: Rm = 15 ÷ 0.010 − 20 = 1500 − 20 = 1480 Ω.

RememberThe finished voltmeter goes in parallel across the component. An ideal voltmeter has very large (→ ∞) resistance.

4 · Exam traps

Three ways students lose marks

  1. Units: change mA to A first (10 mA = 0.010 A).
  2. Rm or Rtotal? The added multiplier is Rm = V/Ig − Rg. The whole voltmeter is Rtotal = V/Ig. Read which one the question wants.
  3. Which way round: the shunt goes in parallel to make an ammeter, then the ammeter goes in series in the circuit. The multiplier goes in series to make a voltmeter, then the voltmeter goes in parallel.

Memory hook“Ammeter = Small Shunt, side by side. Voltmeter = Mighty Multiplier, in a line.”

4Apply · Worked examples

Step-by-step WAEC, NECO & JAMB calculations

01Converting a Galvanometer into a 5.0 A Ammeter (WAEC / JAMB Classic)R_s = 0.0401 Ω

Question: A moving-coil galvanometer has a coil resistance of 20 Ω and gives full-scale deflection when a current of 10 mA passes through it. Explain how to convert it into an ammeter reading up to 5.0 A, and calculate the required resistance.

  1. Method: Connect a low-resistance shunt (Rs) in parallel with the galvanometer.
  2. Convert units: Ig = 10 mA = 0.010 A; Rg = 20 Ω; I = 5.0 A
  3. Shunt current: Is = I − Ig = 5.0 − 0.010 = 4.99 A
  4. Calculate Rs: Rs = (Ig × Rg) ÷ (I − Ig) = (0.010 × 20) ÷ 4.99 = 0.20 ÷ 4.99 = 0.04008 Ω ≈ 0.0401 Ω in parallel.
02Converting the Same Galvanometer into a 15.0 V Voltmeter (WAEC / NECO)R_m = 1480 Ω

Question: Using the same galvanometer (Rg = 20 Ω, Ig = 10 mA), calculate (a) the multiplier resistance required to convert it into a voltmeter reading up to 15.0 V, and (b) the total resistance of the resulting voltmeter.

  1. Method: Connect a high-resistance multiplier (Rm) in series with the galvanometer.
  2. Total resistance required: Rtotal = V ÷ Ig = 15.0 ÷ 0.010 = 1500 Ω
  3. Added series multiplier: Rm = Rtotal − Rg = 1500 − 20 = 1480 Ω
03Fraction of Main Current Passing Through a Shunted Galvanometer (JAMB Speed Question)I_g / I = 1 / 100

Question: A galvanometer of resistance 99 Ω is shunted by a 1.0 Ω resistor. What fraction of the total main current passes through the galvanometer, and by what factor is the current range multiplied?

  1. Equal parallel p.d.: Ig Rg = (I − Ig) Rs  ⇒  Ig ÷ I = Rs ÷ (Rg + Rs)
  2. Substitute values: Ig ÷ I = 1.0 ÷ (99 + 1.0) = 1 ÷ 100 (1% of main current passes through the coil; 99% passes through the shunt).
  3. Range multiplication factor: I ÷ Ig = 100 (the full-scale range is multiplied 100 times).
04Voltmeter Sensitivity & 100 V Range Extension (NECO / JAMB)R_m = 19,975 Ω

Question: A moving-coil meter of resistance 25 Ω deflects full-scale at 5.0 mA. Find the series resistance needed to measure up to 100 V.

  1. Convert current: Ig = 5.0 mA = 0.005 A
  2. Apply multiplier formula: Rm = (V ÷ Ig) − Rg = (100 ÷ 0.005) − 25 = 20,000 − 25 = 19,975 Ω

10 Marks

Task 1 · Dual Instrument Conversion (Ammeter & Voltmeter)

A moving-coil galvanometer has a resistance of 50 Ω and gives full-scale deflection when a potential difference of 1.0 V is across its terminals. Calculate: (a) its full-scale deflection current Ig [2], (b) the shunt resistance needed to convert it into a 0–2.0 A ammeter [4], and (c) the multiplier resistance needed to convert it into a 0–20.0 V voltmeter [4].

Requires ≥ 40 characters of genuine working

Official Marking Scheme (10 Marks)

  • (a) Full-scale current Ig [2 marks]:
    Ig = Vg ÷ Rg = 1.0 V ÷ 50 Ω = 0.020 A (20 mA) [2].
  • (b) Shunt resistance for 2.0 A ammeter [4 marks]:
    Rs = Vg ÷ (I − Ig) = 1.0 ÷ (2.0 − 0.020) = 1.0 ÷ 1.98 = 0.505 Ω connected in parallel [4].
  • (c) Multiplier resistance for 20.0 V voltmeter [4 marks]:
    Rm = (V ÷ Ig) − Rg = (20.0 ÷ 0.020) − 50 = 1000 − 50 = 950 Ω connected in series [4].
10 Marks

Task 2 · Circuit Placement & Loading Error Explanation

Explain with clear circuit reasoning: (a) why an ammeter is connected in series and must have very low resistance [5], and (b) why a voltmeter is connected in parallel and must have very high resistance [5].

Requires ≥ 40 characters of genuine working

Official Marking Scheme (10 Marks)

  • (a) Ammeter in series with low resistance [5 marks]:
    Connected in series so the entire branch current passes through it [2]. Must have very low resistance (ideal 0 Ω) so it does not increase the total circuit resistance or reduce the current being measured [3].
  • (b) Voltmeter in parallel with high resistance [5 marks]:
    Connected in parallel across the two points whose potential difference is required [2]. Must have very high resistance (ideal ∞ Ω) so it draws negligible current from the circuit and does not alter the p.d. across the component [3].
