1 · Combining resistors

Series adds up, parallel shares out

In series there is only one path, so the same current flows through every resistor and their voltages add up. In parallel every branch joins the same two junctions, so each branch has the same voltage and their currents add up.

SeriesReq = R1 + R2 + R3
Parallel1/Req = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3
Two onlyReq = R1R2 ÷ (R1 + R2)

Example4 Ω and 12 Ω in series give 16 Ω. In parallel they give 48 ÷ 16 = 3 Ω.

Exam trapAfter adding the 1/R values, flip the answer to get Req. Quick check: a parallel Req is always smaller than the smallest branch.

2 · Current divider

The smaller resistor takes the bigger current

When the main current I reaches a parallel junction, it splits. Each branch gets a share in inverse proportion to its resistance, so the branch with less resistance carries more current.

Branch 1I1 = I × R2 ÷ (R1 + R2)
Branch 2I2 = I × R1 ÷ (R1 + R2)

Example4 A enters 4 Ω ∥ 12 Ω. I4Ω = 4 × 12/16 = 3 A and I12Ω = 4 × 4/16 = 1 A. Check: 3 + 1 = 4 A ✓

Exam trapTo find the current in R1, the other resistor (R2) goes on top. Always check that the branch currents add back up to I.

3 · Cell arrays

Series cells add EMF; parallel cells cut r

For n identical cells, each with EMF E and internal resistance r, work out the equivalent cell first, then use I = Eeq ÷ (R + req).

SeriesEeq = nE · req = nr
ParallelEeq = E · req = r ÷ n

Use it whenSeries cells suit a large load R (it needs more voltage). Parallel cells suit a small load R, like a pump or starter motor, where a large r would waste most of the energy.

Exam trapParallel cells do not add EMF. Four 1.5 V cells in parallel still give 1.5 V.

4 · Power & efficiency

Choose the power formula by what is shared

Power is the rate of energy transfer, measured in watts (J/s). Efficiency compares the useful power in the load with the total power the cell supplies.

PowerP = IV = I²R = V² ÷ R
Efficiencyη = [R ÷ (R + r)] × 100%

Use it whenIn series (same I), use P = I²R: the bigger R gets more power. In parallel (same V), use P = V²/R: the smaller R gets more power. The load gets maximum power when R = r, but η is then only 50%.

Exam trapEnergy = P × t needs t in seconds: 5 minutes = 300 s.

Step-by-step WAEC, NECO & JAMB calculations

01Series-Parallel Composite Network & Current Divider (WAEC / JAMB Standard)I_4Ω = 1.50 A

Question: A battery of EMF 14.0 V and internal resistance r = 1.0 Ω is connected in series with a 3.0 Ω resistor and a parallel combination of 4.0 Ω and 12.0 Ω resistors. Calculate: (a) the total external resistance, (b) the main current from the battery, and (c) the current flowing through the 4.0 Ω resistor.

  1. Parallel pair equivalent resistance: Rp = (4.0 × 12.0) ÷ (4.0 + 12.0) = 48 ÷ 16 = 3.0 Ω
  2. Total external resistance: Rext = 3.0 Ω + 3.0 Ω = 6.0 Ω
  3. Main circuit current: Imain = E ÷ (Rext + r) = 14.0 ÷ (6.0 + 1.0) = 14.0 ÷ 7.0 = 2.00 A
  4. Current in the 4.0 Ω branch (Current Divider): I4Ω = Imain × [12.0 ÷ (4.0 + 12.0)] = 2.00 × (12 ÷ 16) = 1.50 A. (The 12 Ω branch takes the remaining 0.50 A.)
02Identical Cells in Series vs Parallel on a Heavy Load (JAMB Favourite)2.50 A vs 1.25 A

Question: Three identical cells, each of EMF 2.0 V and internal resistance 1.5 Ω, are used to drive a low-resistance DC motor of resistance R = 0.30 Ω. Compare the current delivered when the cells are connected (a) in series, and (b) in parallel.

  1. (a) In Series: Eeq = 3 × 2.0 = 6.0 V and req = 3 × 1.5 = 4.5 Ω
    Iseries = 6.0 ÷ (0.30 + 4.5) = 6.0 ÷ 4.8 = 1.25 A
  2. (b) In Parallel: Eeq = 2.0 V and req = 1.5 ÷ 3 = 0.50 Ω
    Iparallel = 2.0 ÷ (0.30 + 0.50) = 2.0 ÷ 0.80 = 2.50 A (exactly twice the current!).
03Circuit Efficiency & Power Dissipated (WAEC / NECO)η = 75.0%

Question: A battery of EMF 12.0 V and internal resistance 1.0 Ω is connected across an external resistor of 3.0 Ω. Calculate (a) the power delivered to the external load, (b) the power wasted inside the battery, and (c) the efficiency of the battery.

  1. Current: I = 12.0 ÷ (3.0 + 1.0) = 3.0 A
  2. Useful load power: Pload = I2R = 3.02 × 3.0 = 27.0 W
  3. Internal wasted power: Plost = I2r = 3.02 × 1.0 = 9.0 W
  4. Efficiency: η = [R ÷ (R + r)] × 100% = (3.0 ÷ 4.0) × 100% = 75.0%
04Power Ratio in Series vs Parallel Resistors (JAMB Speed Question)1 : 3 vs 3 : 1

Question: Two resistors of 4 Ω and 12 Ω are connected (a) in series, and (b) in parallel across a constant-voltage source. Find the ratio of power dissipated P4Ω : P12Ω in each case.

  1. (a) In Series (same I): Use P = I2R → P4Ω : P12Ω = 4 : 12 = 1 : 3
  2. (b) In Parallel (same V): Use P = V2 ÷ R → P4Ω : P12Ω = (1/4) : (1/12) = 3 : 1

Task 1 · Series-Parallel Network & Current Divider

A battery of EMF 18 V and internal resistance 1.0 Ω is connected in series with a 4.0 Ω resistor and a parallel combination of 6.0 Ω and 12.0 Ω resistors. Calculate: (a) the equivalent external resistance [3], (b) the main current from the battery [3], and (c) the current and power dissipated in the 6.0 Ω resistor [4].

Requires ≥ 40 characters of genuine working

Official Marking Scheme (10 Marks)

  • (a) Equivalent external resistance [3 marks]:
    Parallel pair: Rp = (6.0 × 12.0) ÷ (6.0 + 12.0) = 72 ÷ 18 = 4.0 Ω [2].
    Total external resistance: Rext = 4.0 + 4.0 = 8.0 Ω [1].
  • (b) Main current [3 marks]:
    I = E ÷ (Rext + r) = 18 ÷ (8.0 + 1.0) = 18 ÷ 9.0 = 2.0 A [3].
  • (c) Current & power in 6.0 Ω resistor [4 marks]:
    I6Ω = I × [12 ÷ (6 + 12)] = 2.0 × (12 ÷ 18) = 1.33 A (4/3 A) [2].
    P6Ω = I6Ω2 × 6.0 = (4/3)2 × 6 = 10.67 W [2].
10 Marks

Task 2 · Series vs Parallel Cell Banks & Efficiency

Four identical cells, each of EMF 1.5 V and internal resistance 2.0 Ω, are connected in parallel across a 1.5 Ω load. Calculate: (a) the equivalent EMF and internal resistance of the battery bank [4], (b) the load current and terminal voltage [3], and (c) the electrical efficiency of the circuit [3].

Requires ≥ 40 characters of genuine working

Official Marking Scheme (10 Marks)

  • (a) Battery bank parameters [4 marks]:
    In parallel, Eeq = E = 1.5 V [2] and req = r ÷ 4 = 2.0 ÷ 4 = 0.50 Ω [2].
  • (b) Current and terminal voltage [3 marks]:
    I = 1.5 ÷ (1.5 + 0.50) = 1.5 ÷ 2.0 = 0.75 A [2].
    V = I R = 0.75 × 1.5 = 1.125 V (1.13 V) [1].
  • (c) Efficiency [3 marks]:
    η = [R ÷ (R + req)] × 100% = (1.5 ÷ 2.0) × 100% = 75.0% [3].
