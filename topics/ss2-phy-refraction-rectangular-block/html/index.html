The Unit Mystery · Surulere & Yaba, Lagos

Why does a laser beam through a 6.0 cm glass block emerge parallel yet shifted sideways by 1.98 cm?

At Dr Kunle’s Eye & Optical Calibration Clinic in Surulere, Lagos, SS2 student Zainab and her classmate Kelechi watch a green calibration laser strike a polished 6.0 cm rectangular crown-glass block (n = 1.50) at an angle of incidence i = 45°.

Inside the block, the ray bends sharply towards the normal to r = 28.1°. Yet when it exits the opposite parallel face into air, it returns to e = 45° — travelling strictly parallel to its original path, but displaced sideways by d = 1.98 cm! At the same time, a printed calibration cross underneath the 6.0 cm block looks as if it is floating at a depth of 4.0 cm (raised by 2.0 cm), and when technician Tunde tilts an internal laser beyond 41.8° inside a glass core at Yaba, the beam refuses to exit into air at all — bouncing with 100% brightness as Total Internal Reflection!

Why does slowing light from 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ to 2.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ bend its path while keeping frequency constant? Why does a rectangular glass block produce lateral shift without angular dispersion? How do we measure apparent depth by the no-parallax method? And how do we align four optical pins (P₁, P₂, P₃, P₄) to conquer all 7 WAEC Paper 3 graph variants?

Snell’s Law Linear Graph (sin i against sin r)

WAEC Paper 3 Variant A — Classical Four-Pin Ray-Tracing Graph

Governing Equation
sin i = n sin r · n = sin i / sin r
Graph Slope & Intercept
Straight line through (0, 0) · Slope = Δ(sin i) / Δ(sin r) = n
Key Exam Rule
If sin r is plotted on vertical axis, Slope = 1 / n

By Snell’s second law of refraction, the ratio sin i / sin r is a constant equal to the refractive index n of the glass block. Plotting sin i on the vertical axis against sin r on the horizontal axis gives a straight line through the origin with gradient n ≈ 1.50.

The 6 calculation archetypes

Over 95% of WAEC, NECO, and JAMB numerical problems on Refraction Through a Rectangular Glass Block follow one of these six patterns:

Type 1 · Lesson 1

Snell’s Law, Speed & Wavelength

Given i, r or glancing angle g = 90° − i, use n = sin i / sin r = c / v = λ₁ / λ₂ while frequency f stays constant.

Type 2 · Lesson 1

90° Reflected–Refracted Shortcut

When the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular (90° apart), r = 90° − i so sin r = cos i and n = tan i.

Type 3 · Lesson 2

Lateral Displacement & Far-Face Shift

Use d = t sin(i − r) / cos r for sideways shift and EF = t tan r (or refractometer n = t / √((EF)² + t²) / sin i).

Type 4 · Lesson 3

Real Depth, Apparent Depth & Rise

Use n = R / A and upward shift d_v = R − A = R(1 − 1/n). For layered media, add A_total = t₁/n₁ + t₂/n₂.

Type 5 · Lesson 4

Critical Angle & Wave Speed

Use sin c = 1 / n (or n₂ / n₁) and the JAMB speed shortcut v = c₀ sin c; check i > c for TIR.

Type 6 · Lesson 5

WAEC Paper 3 Triangle & Cosine Graphs

Use LE = w tan r, LN = w sec r, LE / LN = sin r, and for cos i vs cos r compute n = √((1 − x₁²) / (1 − y₁²)).
