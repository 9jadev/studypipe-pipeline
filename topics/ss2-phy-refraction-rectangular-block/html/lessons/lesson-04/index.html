Critical Angle, Total Internal Reflection & Optical Fibres

Why does a laser beam inside a crown-glass block suddenly stop exiting into air once its internal angle exceeds 41.8° — reflecting off the transparent boundary with 100% brightness?

  • 01Define the critical angle c and apply sin c = 1 ÷ n (for medium to air) and sin c = n2 ÷ n1 (for two media where n₁ > n₂).
  • 02Combine n = c₀/v and sin c = 1/n to use the JAMB/WAEC speed shortcut v = c0 sin c.
  • 03State the two mandatory conditions for Total Internal Reflection (TIR) and distinguish the three regimes: i < c, i = c, and i > c.
  • 04Explain how step-index optical fibres (n_core > n_cladding) guide high-speed laser signals across cities and oceans.
  • 05Explain why 45°–45°–90° glass prisms outperform silvered mirrors in periscopes and binoculars (no tarnishing, no multiple ghost images) and how hot-road mirages form.

Critical Angle, Total Internal Reflection & Optical Fibres

Let’s connect the critical angle equations to optical fibre engineering, reflecting prisms, and mirages.

1 · Critical Angle (c) & Wave Speed Shortcut

The critical angle (c) is the angle of incidence in an optically denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the optically less dense medium is 90° (the refracted ray grazes the boundary):

n1 sin c = n2 sin 90° = n2   ⇒   sin c = n2 ÷ n1 = v1 ÷ v2
For medium (n) into air (n2 = 1):   sin c = 1 ÷ n   ·   Speed in medium:   v = c0 ÷ n = c0 sin c

2 · Total Internal Reflection (TIR) & Optical Fibres

Total Internal Reflection (TIR) is the complete reflection of a light ray back into an optically denser medium when it strikes the boundary with a less dense medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle (i > c):

Mandatory Condition 1:   n1 > n2 (Denser → Less Dense)   ·   Mandatory Condition 2:   i > c
Step-Index Optical Fibre:   ncore > ncladding   ·   sin c = ncladding ÷ ncore

Active 5-Minute Movement Break (The Three Regimes Arm Sweep): Stand up and hold your left arm straight out horizontally in front of you ("the glass–air boundary") and point your right arm upward at 30° from the vertical ("sub-critical ray"). Slowly tilt your right arm further from the vertical until it reaches 41.8° — snap your left hand flat along the boundary ("critical angle: r = 90° graze!"). Now tilt your right arm to 45° and bounce it downward symmetrically ("Total Internal Reflection!").

Worked examples, step by step

Attempt each problem on paper first, then check the step-by-step WAEC/JAMB working.

EXAMPLE 1

Critical angle & speed of light in the medium

The critical angle for light travelling from a certain dense flint glass into air is 37.3°. Taking the speed of light in air as c₀ = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, calculate: (a) the refractive index n of the flint glass, and (b) the speed of light v inside the flint glass.

  1. Refractive index from sin c = 1/n: n = 1 ÷ sin 37.3° = 1 ÷ 0.6060 = 1.65.
  2. Wave speed v inside glass: v = c₀ sin c = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 0.6060 = 1.82 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.
(a) n = 1.65  ·  (b) v = 1.82 × 108 m s−1
EXAMPLE 2

Optical fibre core–cladding critical angle

An optical fibre used in a telecommunications link has a central glass core of refractive index n₁ = 1.55 surrounded by a cladding of refractive index n₂ = 1.40. Calculate: (a) the critical angle c at the core–cladding interface, and (b) state what happens to a laser ray inside the core that strikes the interface at an angle of incidence of 72°.

  1. Critical angle at core–cladding boundary: sin c = n₂ ÷ n₁ = 1.40 ÷ 1.55 = 0.9032 ⇒ c = sin⁻¹(0.9032) = 64.6°.
  2. Outcome at i = 72°: Since the ray is in the denser core (1.55 > 1.40) and i = 72° > c = 64.6°, it undergoes 100% Total Internal Reflection back into the core at an angle of reflection of 72°.
(a) c = 64.6°  ·  (b) 100% Total Internal Reflection at 72°
EXAMPLE 3

Ray hitting the side wall of a rectangular glass block

A ray of light in air enters the top horizontal face of a rectangular glass block (n = 1.50, c = 41.8°) at an angle of incidence i₁ = 45° and refracts at r₁ = 28.1°. The refracted ray then strikes the vertical side wall of the block. Calculate the internal angle of incidence i₂ on the vertical side wall and state whether the ray escapes into the air through the side wall.

  1. Geometry of perpendicular faces: Because the vertical side wall is perpendicular (90°) to the top horizontal face, their normals are perpendicular: i₂ = 90° − r₁ = 90° − 28.1° = 61.9°.
  2. Compare i₂ with critical angle c: Since i₂ = 61.9° > c = 41.8°, the ray does NOT escape through the vertical side wall; it undergoes Total Internal Reflection back into the block at 61.9°!
i2 = 61.9° (> 41.8°)  ·  Undergoes Total Internal Reflection on the side wall!

Part B · Structured Written Questions (30 marks)

1. (a) Define critical angle and Total Internal Reflection. (b) State the two conditions required for Total Internal Reflection. (c) The speed of light in air is 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ and in a glass block is 1.95 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. Calculate the refractive index and critical angle of the glass.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Critical angle is the angle of incidence in the optically denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the less dense medium is 90° (2). Total Internal Reflection is the complete reflection of light back into the denser medium when i > c (2).
(b) (i) Light must travel from optically denser to less dense medium; (ii) i > c (2).
(c) n = c₀ / v = 3.0 / 1.95 = 1.54 (2); sin c = v / c₀ = 1.95 / 3.0 = 0.6500 ⇒ c = 40.5° (2).

2. (a) Explain with a labelled structure how a step-index optical fibre transmits laser pulses over long distances, stating the required relationship between n_core and n_cladding. (b) An optical fibre has core refractive index 1.56 and cladding refractive index 1.41. Calculate the critical angle at the core–cladding boundary. (c) State three advantages of optical fibres over copper cables in telecommunications.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) A central transparent core of high refractive index (n_core) is surrounded by cladding of lower refractive index (n_cladding < n_core) so rays strike the boundary at i > c and undergo repeated TIR (4).
(b) sin c = 1.41 / 1.56 = 0.9038 ⇒ c = 64.7° (3).
(c) Much higher bandwidth/data capacity, lower signal attenuation over long distances, and immunity to electromagnetic interference (3).

3. (a) Explain why right-angled isosceles glass prisms (45°–45°–90°) are preferred to plane silvered mirrors in periscopes and prism binoculars. (b) Explain how an optical mirage is formed on a tarred Nigerian highway on a hot sunny afternoon.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Prisms reflect 100% of light by TIR (i = 45° > c = 41.8°), have no silver coating to tarnish or peel, and do not form multiple faint ghost images like thick silvered mirrors (5).
(b) Air layers near the hot tarred road are hotter and optically less dense than cooler air above. Rays from the sky bend progressively away from the normal as they travel downward until i > c, undergoing upward Total Internal Reflection so the observer sees an inverted reflection of the sky looking like a pool of water (5).
