Model answers unlocks after you try
  1. Snell’s law states that the ratio of the sines of the angles is constant (sin i = n sin r), whereas i ÷ r is not constant (except as an approximation at very small angles).
  2. Since i + r = 56.3° + 33.7° = 90.0°, the angle between the reflected and refracted rays is 180° − 90° = 90° (perpendicular), and n = tan i = tan 56.3° = 1.50.

Laws of Refraction, Wavefront Speed & Angle Shortcuts

Let’s connect your 3D laser bench observations to the exact equations tested in WAEC, NECO, and JAMB Physics.

1 · The Two Laws of Refraction (Snell’s Law)

Refraction of light is the change in direction (bending) of a light ray as it passes obliquely from one transparent medium into another of different optical density, caused by a change in wave speed.

  • First Law: The incident ray, the refracted ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
  • Second Law (Snell’s Law): The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence i to the sine of the angle of refraction r is a constant for a given pair of media and wavelength:
1n2 = sin i ÷ sin r = n2 ÷ n1 = v1 ÷ v2 = λ1 ÷ λ2   (while frequency f = constant!)

2 · Absolute Refractive Index, Reversibility & Special Cases

For light travelling from air or vacuum (c = 3.0 × 108 m s−1) into a medium of refractive index n:

n = c ÷ v = λair ÷ λmedium = sin i ÷ sin r     and by reversibility:     gna = 1 ÷ ang
When Reflected Ray ⊥ Refracted Ray (90° apart):   i + r = 90°  ⇒  sin r = cos i  ⇒  n = tan i
Single-Boundary Deviation:   D = |i − r|   ·   Glancing Angle Conversion:   i = 90° − g

EXAMPLE 1

Glancing angle trap & Snell’s law

A ray of light in air strikes the flat surface of a rectangular glass block at a glancing angle of 35° to the surface. If the refractive index of the glass is 1.52, calculate: (a) the angle of incidence i, (b) the angle of refraction r inside the glass, and (c) the angle of deviation at this surface.

  1. Convert glancing angle g to angle of incidence i: i = 90° − g = 90° − 35° = 55.0°.
  2. Apply Snell’s law for r: sin r = sin i ÷ n = sin 55.0° ÷ 1.52 = 0.8192 ÷ 1.52 = 0.5389 ⇒ r = sin⁻¹(0.5389) = 32.6°.
  3. Angular deviation at single boundary: D = i − r = 55.0° − 32.6° = 22.4° towards the normal.
(a) i = 55.0°  ·  (b) r = 32.6°  ·  (c) D = 22.4°
EXAMPLE 2

Wave speed, wavelength & constant frequency in glass

Monochromatic yellow light of wavelength 5.89 × 10⁻⁷ m (589 nm) in air enters a rectangular glass block of refractive index 1.50. Taking the speed of light in air as 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, calculate: (a) the frequency of the light in air and in glass, (b) the speed of the light in the glass, and (c) the wavelength inside the glass.

  1. Frequency f (constant in both media): f = c ÷ λ_air = (3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ (5.89 × 10⁻⁷) = 5.09 × 10¹⁴ Hz.
  2. Wave speed v in glass: v = c ÷ n = (3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 1.50 = 2.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.
  3. Wavelength λ_glass: λ_glass = λ_air ÷ n = 589 nm ÷ 1.50 = 393 nm = 3.93 × 10⁻⁷ m.
(a) f = 5.09 × 1014 Hz  ·  (b) v = 2.00 × 108 m s−1  ·  (c) λg = 3.93 × 10−7 m
EXAMPLE 3

Perpendicular (90°) reflected and refracted rays

A ray of light in air strikes a horizontal glass block of refractive index n = 1.60. At what angle of incidence i (and glancing angle g) will the partially reflected ray and the refracted ray be perpendicular (90° apart)?

  1. Geometry along the normal: Since angle of reflection equals i and the angle between reflected and refracted rays is 90°, we have i + 90° + r = 180° ⇒ r = 90° − i.
  2. Substitute into Snell’s law: n = sin i ÷ sin(90° − i) = sin i ÷ cos i = tan i.
  3. Solve for i and g: i = tan⁻¹(1.60) = 58.0°; glancing angle g = 90° − 58.0° = 32.0°.
Angle of incidence i = 58.0°  ·  Glancing angle g = 32.0°

Exit ticket

In two sentences: why does the frequency of light stay constant when it enters a glass block while its wavelength decreases, and why is n = tan i when the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular?

2 marks
Model answer unlocks after you try

Frequency f is fixed by the source of oscillations, so when wave speed drops to v = c/n in glass, wavelength λ = v/f must decrease by the same factor n (1). When the reflected and refracted rays are 90° apart, r = 90° − i so sin r = cos i and n = sin i / cos i = tan i (1).

Assignment: Snell’s Law & Wavefront Refraction

Part A · Home Observation: Straight vs Tilted Pencil in Water

Place a straight pencil vertically (i = 0°) into a clear glass of water and view it from above/side, then tilt the pencil at 45°. Why does the tilted pencil look sharply bent at the water surface while the vertical one does not?

Practical task
Sample answer unlocks after you try

Light rays from the submerged part of the tilted pencil cross the water–air boundary obliquely and refract away from the normal (since n_water > n_air), making the submerged section appear bent upward. When held vertically along the normal (i = 0°), rays along the normal have r = 0° (zero angular deviation).

Part B · Structured Written Questions (30 marks)

1. (a) State the two laws of refraction of light. (b) Explain using wavefronts why a ray bends towards the normal when entering glass obliquely, and state what happens to speed, wavelength, and frequency. (c) Monochromatic light of wavelength 600 nm in air enters crown glass (n = 1.50). Calculate its frequency, speed in glass, and wavelength in glass. (c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹)

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) (i) The incident ray, refracted ray, and normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane (1). (ii) sin i / sin r = constant (n) for a given pair of media (1).
(b) The edge of the wavefront entering glass first slows down (v < c) while the outer edge in air continues at speed c, pivoting the wavefront towards the normal (2). Speed and wavelength decrease, while frequency remains constant (2).
(c) f = c / λ_air = (3.0 × 10⁸) / (6.0 × 10⁻⁷) = 5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz (2); v = c / n = 2.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ (1); λ_g = 600 / 1.50 = 400 nm (4.0 × 10⁻⁷ m) (1).

2. (a) A ray in air strikes a flat glass block at a glancing angle g = 35° to the surface and refracts at r = 32.6°. Calculate the angle of incidence i, the refractive index n, and the angular deviation at this surface. (b) If the absolute refractive indices of water and flint glass are 4/3 and 5/3 respectively, find the refractive index of flint glass with respect to water (w_n_g).

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) i = 90° − 35° = 55.0° (2). n = sin 55.0° / sin 32.6° = 0.8192 / 0.5388 = 1.52 (3). Angular deviation D = i − r = 55.0° − 32.6° = 22.4° (2).
(b) w_n_g = n_g / n_w = (5/3) ÷ (4/3) = 5/4 = 1.25 (3).

3. A ray of light in air is incident on a plane glass block at an angle of incidence i. The partially reflected ray and the refracted ray are perpendicular (90°) to each other. (a) Prove that n = tan i. (b) If i = 57.5°, calculate the angle of refraction r, the refractive index n, and the speed of light inside the glass block.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Along the straight normal, i_refl + 90° + r = 180°. Since i_refl = i, r = 90° − i (2). By Snell’s law, n = sin i / sin(90° − i) = sin i / cos i = tan i (2).
(b) r = 90° − 57.5° = 32.5° (2); n = tan 57.5° = 1.57 (2); v = c / n = (3.0 × 10⁸) / 1.57 = 1.91 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ (2).
