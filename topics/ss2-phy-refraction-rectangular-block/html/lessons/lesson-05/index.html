WAEC/NECO Paper 3 Practical & The 7 Graph Variants

Why do WAEC examiners award marks for aligning the bases (feet) of optical pins spaced at least 4 cm – 5 cm apart rather than their tops — and how does the internal right-angled triangle LN₀E unlock all 7 Paper 3 graph variants?

  • 01Execute the four-pin ray-tracing practical and state the mandatory WAEC precautions (aligning pin bases, spacing pins ≥ 4 cm apart, vertical pins, sharp pencil).
  • 02Use the internal right-angled triangle LN0E (width w = LN₀) to derive LE = w tan r, LN = w sec r, and LN² = LE² + w².
  • 03Determine refractive index n from linear graphs of sin i vs sin r (slope = n) and sin r vs sin i (slope = 1/n), and block width w from LE vs tan r and LN vs sec r.
  • 04Use the ratio LE ÷ LN = sin r to interpret graphs of LE vs LN (slope = sin r) and sin i vs LE/LN (slope = n).
  • 05Evaluate refractive index n = √[(1 − x₁²) ÷ (1 − y₁²)] from a coordinate point (x₁, y₁) = (cos i, cos r) on the curved cos i vs cos r graph.

Internal Triangle LN₀E & The 7 WAEC Paper 3 Graph Variants

Every WAEC or NECO Paper 3 rectangular glass block graph question comes from combining Snell’s law with the internal right-angled triangle LN₀E.

1 · Internal Right-Angled Triangle LN₀E (Width w = LN₀)

Inside a rectangular glass block of perpendicular width w = LN0, the normal LN₀ (adjacent), the offset along the back face N₀E = LE (opposite), and the internal refracted ray path LN (hypotenuse) form a right-angled triangle at N₀:

tan r = LE ÷ w  ⇒  LE = w tan r   ·   cos r = w ÷ LN  ⇒  LN = w sec r = w ÷ cos r
Pythagoras Check:   LN2 = LE2 + w2   ·   Ratio of Opposite to Hypotenuse:   LE ÷ LN = sin r

2 · The Cosine Curve Trap (Variant C: cos i vs cos r)

Using sin i = √(1 − cos²i) and sin r = √(1 − cos²r) in Snell’s law gives a non-linear curve. For any coordinate pair (x₁, y₁) = (cos i, cos r) read directly from the curve:

n = sin i ÷ sin r = √[(1 − cos2i) ÷ (1 − cos2r)] = √[(1 − x12) ÷ (1 − y12)]
Variant F (LE vs LN at fixed i):   Slope S = sin r  ⇒  n = sin i ÷ S   ·   Variant G (sin i vs LE/LN):   Slope S = n

Active 5-Minute Movement Break (The One-Eye Pin-Base Sighting Drill): Stand up and hold two pens upright at arm’s length, one 15 cm behind the other. Tilt the back pen slightly sideways while keeping its bottom tip still. Close your left eye and line up the bottom tips — notice how the bottom tips stay aligned while the tilted top cap is way off to the side! That is why WAEC examiners always insist on aligning the bases (feet) of optical pins.

EXAMPLE 1

Variants D & E · Finding block width w from LE = w tan r and LN = w sec r

In a WAEC Paper 3 practical with a rectangular glass block, a student records at i = 40° that r = 25.4°, the back-face displacement is LE = 2.85 cm, and the refracted path length inside the block is LN = 6.64 cm. Calculate the width w of the glass block from (a) LE = w tan r, (b) LN = w sec r, and (c) verify Pythagoras' theorem LN² = LE² + w².

  1. From LE = w tan r: w = LE ÷ tan 25.4° = 2.85 ÷ 0.4748 = 6.00 cm.
  2. From LN = w sec r = w ÷ cos r: w = LN cos 25.4° = 6.64 × 0.9033 = 6.00 cm.
  3. Pythagoras Check: √(LE² + w²) = √(2.85² + 6.00²) = √(8.1225 + 36.00) = √44.1225 = 6.64 cm = LN.
(a) w = 6.00 cm  ·  (b) w = 6.00 cm  ·  (c) √(LE² + w²) = 6.64 cm = LN
EXAMPLE 2

Variant C · Evaluating n from a point on the curved cos i vs cos r graph

A student plots cos r (vertical axis y) against cos i (horizontal axis x) and obtains a smooth curve. The student reads a point on the curve with coordinates (x₁, y₁) = (0.60, 0.85). Calculate the refractive index n of the glass block.

  1. Compute sin i from x₁ = cos i = 0.60: sin i = √(1 − x₁²) = √(1 − 0.60²) = √(1 − 0.36) = √0.64 = 0.800.
  2. Compute sin r from y₁ = cos r = 0.85: sin r = √(1 − y₁²) = √(1 − 0.85²) = √(1 − 0.7225) = √0.2775 = 0.5268.
  3. Substitute into Snell's law: n = sin i ÷ sin r = √[(1 − x₁²) ÷ (1 − y₁²)] = 0.800 ÷ 0.5268 = 1.52.
n = √[(1 − 0.60²) ÷ (1 − 0.85²)] = 1.52
EXAMPLE 3

Variants F & G · Using the ratio LE / LN = sin r

In a rectangular glass block experiment at a fixed angle of incidence i = 30° across blocks of different widths, a student plots LE against LN and obtains a straight line of gradient S = 0.333. Determine (a) the angle of refraction r, and (b) the refractive index n of the glass.

  1. Identify the meaning of the slope S = LE / LN: sin r = LE ÷ LN = S = 0.333 ⇒ r = sin⁻¹(0.333) = 19.5°.
  2. Compute the refractive index n at i = 30°: n = sin i ÷ sin r = sin 30° ÷ 0.333 = 0.500 ÷ 0.333 = 1.50.
(a) r = 19.5°  ·  (b) n = 1.50

Part A · Practical Drill: Why Pin Bases Beat Pin Tops

Stand two pens or toothpicks in a piece of cardboard 5 cm apart, tilt one by 15° while keeping its base fixed, and sight along them with one eye closed. Explain why joining the bases gives the true line while sighting the tops introduces an angular error.

Practical task
Sample answer unlocks after you try

The pencil line on the drawing sheet is drawn through the pin-prick holes at the bases of the pins. If a student aligns the top of a tilted pin with the line of sight, the base of that pin pierces the paper to one side of the true ray path, introducing a large angular error when the pin-pricks are joined.

Part B · Structured Written & Practical Questions (30 marks)

1. (a) Explain why a student must align the bases (feet) of optical pins rather than their tops during a rectangular glass block experiment (4 marks). (b) State four mandatory precautions to be taken in a WAEC Paper 3 rectangular glass block ray-tracing experiment (6 marks).

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) If an optical pin tilts slightly from the vertical, its top moves sideways off the true optical line of sight whereas its base (where it pierces the paper) remains accurately on the true ray path (4).
(b) (i) Ensure all optical pins are pushed strictly vertical into the board; (ii) Space consecutive pins at least 4 cm – 5 cm apart; (iii) View and align the bases (feet) of the pins rather than their tops; (iv) Use a sharp pencil to draw fine ray lines and small circles around pin-pricks (6).

2. A rectangular glass block has width w = 6.0 cm and refractive index n = 1.50. (a) For a ray incident at i = 30°, calculate r, LE = w tan r, and LN = w sec r, and verify LN² = LE² + w² (6 marks). (b) On a graph of cos i (x-axis) against cos r (y-axis), a student reads (x₁, y₁) = (0.60, 0.85); calculate n using n = √[(1 − x₁²)/(1 − y₁²)] (4 marks).

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) sin r = sin 30° / 1.50 = 0.500 / 1.50 = 0.3333 ⇒ r = 19.47° (2). LE = 6.0 tan 19.47° = 2.12 cm (1.5). LN = 6.0 / cos 19.47° = 6.36 cm (1.5). √(2.12² + 6.00²) = √40.49 = 6.36 cm = LN (1).
(b) n = √[(1 − 0.60²) / (1 − 0.85²)] = √(0.64 / 0.2775) = √2.3063 = 1.52 (4).

3. State the graph shape, the physical meaning of the gradient S, and how to determine refractive index n or block width w for each of the following WAEC Paper 3 plots: (a) sin r vs sin i, (b) LE vs tan r, (c) LN vs sec r, (d) LE vs LN at constant i, and (e) sin i vs LE / LN.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) sin r vs sin i: linear through origin, slope S = 1/n ⇒ n = 1/S (2).
(b) LE vs tan r: linear through origin, slope S = w (block width) (2).
(c) LN vs sec r: linear through origin, slope S = w (block width) (2).
(d) LE vs LN at constant i: linear through origin, slope S = sin r ⇒ n = sin i / S (2).
(e) sin i vs LE/LN: since LE/LN = sin r, linear through origin with slope S = n (2).
