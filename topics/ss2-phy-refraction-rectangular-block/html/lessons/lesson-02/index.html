Parallel Emergence, Lateral Displacement & Dispersion

Let’s derive the exact geometric equations governing a parallel-sided rectangular glass block.

1 · Proof of Parallel Emergence (e = i) & Zero Net Angular Deviation

When a light ray in air enters the top face AB of a rectangular glass block at angle of incidence i and refracts at r1, it travels to the opposite parallel face DC and strikes it at internal angle of incidence i2:

  • Because faces AB and DC are parallel, the two normals are parallel lines cut by the internal ray transversal, so alternate interior angles are equal: r1 = i2 = r.
  • Applying Snell’s law at entry (air → glass) and exit (glass → air): sin i = n sin r and n sin r = sin e.
sin e = sin i   ⇒   e = i     ·     Net Angular Deviation Dnet = (i − r) − (e − r) = 0°

2 · Deriving Lateral Displacement d & Far-Face Shift EF

Lateral displacement (d) is the perpendicular distance between the original extended path of the incident ray and the emergent ray leaving a parallel-sided refracting slab of thickness t:

Internal path length:   ON = t ÷ cos r   ·   Lateral displacement:   d = ON sin(i − r) = t sin(i − r) ÷ cos r
Far-face shift along opposite face:   EF = t tan r   ·   sin r = EF ÷ √(t2 + EF2)
Small-angle approximation:   d ≈ t i (1 − 1/n)   ·   Grazing limit (i → 90°):   dmax = t

Pivot 17° to your right and take two diagonal steps ("refracted ray inside the block"). Now pivot 17° back to your left so you face the exact original direction again ("emergent ray e = i") — notice you are facing the exact same direction as before, but shifted one tile sideways ("lateral displacement d")!

Worked examples, step by step

EXAMPLE 1

Lateral displacement & internal path length

A ray of light strikes a rectangular crown-glass block of thickness t = 6.0 cm and refractive index n = 1.50 at an angle of incidence i = 45°. Calculate: (a) the angle of refraction r, (b) the distance ON travelled by the ray inside the glass, (c) the lateral displacement d of the emergent ray, and (d) the angle of emergence e.

  1. Angle of refraction r: sin r = sin 45° ÷ 1.50 = 0.7071 ÷ 1.50 = 0.4714 ⇒ r = 28.1°.
  2. Internal path length ON: ON = t ÷ cos r = 6.0 ÷ cos 28.1° = 6.0 ÷ 0.8821 = 6.80 cm.
  3. Lateral displacement d: d = ON sin(i − r) = 6.80 × sin(45° − 28.1°) = 6.80 × sin 16.9° = 6.80 × 0.2907 = 1.98 cm.
  4. Angle of emergence e: Since opposite faces are parallel, e = i = 45.0°.
(a) r = 28.1°  ·  (b) ON = 6.80 cm  ·  (c) d = 1.98 cm  ·  (d) e = 45.0°
EXAMPLE 2

Finding refractive index n from far-face shift EF and thickness t

A ray of light is incident at i = 30° on the top face of a parallel-sided glass block of thickness t = 6.0 cm. The refracted ray strikes the opposite parallel face at a point E located EF = 2.0 cm from the foot F of the normal. Calculate the refractive index n of the glass.

  1. Find hypotenuse ON inside the block: ON = √(t² + EF²) = √(6.0² + 2.0²) = √(36 + 4) = √40 = 6.325 cm.
  2. Find sin r from the right-angled triangle: sin r = opposite ÷ hypotenuse = EF ÷ ON = 2.0 ÷ 6.325 = 0.3162.
  3. Apply Snell’s law: n = sin i ÷ sin r = sin 30° ÷ 0.3162 = 0.500 ÷ 0.3162 = 1.58.
Refractive index n = 1.58
EXAMPLE 3

Comparing red and violet lateral shifts in a glass slab

A narrow beam of white light strikes a 10.0 cm thick flint-glass slab at i = 60°. In this glass, red light has refractive index n_r = 1.62 and violet light has n_v = 1.66. Calculate the lateral displacement for each colour and their sideways separation upon emergence.

  1. Red light (n_r = 1.62): sin r_r = sin 60° ÷ 1.62 = 0.5346 ⇒ r_r = 32.3°. Shift d_r = 10.0 × sin(60° − 32.3°) ÷ cos 32.3° = 10.0 × 0.4648 ÷ 0.8453 = 5.50 cm.
  2. Violet light (n_v = 1.66): sin r_v = sin 60° ÷ 1.66 = 0.5217 ⇒ r_v = 31.4°. Shift d_v = 10.0 × sin(60° − 31.4°) ÷ cos 31.4° = 10.0 × 0.4787 ÷ 0.8536 = 5.61 cm.
  3. Parallel sideways separation: Δd = d_v − d_r = 5.61 − 5.50 = 0.11 cm (1.1 mm), with both rays emerging parallel at e = 60°.
dr = 5.50 cm  ·  dv = 5.61 cm  ·  Parallel separation Δd = 0.11 cm

Exit ticket

In two sentences: why is the net angular deviation through a rectangular glass block zero, and what three factors increase its lateral displacement d?

2 marks
Model answer unlocks after you try

Because opposite faces are parallel, the inward bending at the first face (i − r) is exactly cancelled by the outward bending at the second face (e − r, with e = i) (1). Lateral displacement d increases with greater block thickness t, larger angle of incidence i, and higher refractive index n (or shorter wavelength) (1).

Part A · Home Observation: Looking Obliquely Through a Thick Glass

Draw a straight ink line on paper and place a thick flat glass slab (or the flat thick base of a drinking glass) across part of the line. View the line from straight above (i = 0°) and then at a 45° angle. Describe and explain what happens to the segment seen through the glass.

Practical task
Sample answer unlocks after you try

Viewed straight from above (i = 0°), there is zero sideways shift (d = 0) so the line looks continuous. Viewed obliquely at 45°, the segment seen through the glass appears shifted sideways (laterally displaced by d = t sin(i − r)/cos r) while remaining strictly parallel to the outer parts of the line (e = i).

Part B · Structured Written Questions (30 marks)

1. (a) Define lateral displacement. (b) Prove using Snell's law and parallel normals that a ray entering a parallel-sided glass block at angle i emerges into air at e = i. (c) Calculate the lateral displacement of a ray incident at 50° on a glass block of thickness 7.5 cm and refractive index 1.50.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Lateral displacement is the perpendicular distance between the original extended path of an incident ray and the emergent ray from a parallel-sided refracting medium (2).
(b) At face 1, sin i = n sin r₁. Since opposite faces are parallel, normals are parallel and alternate angles i₂ = r₁. At face 2, n sin i₂ = sin e ⇒ sin e = sin i ⇒ e = i (4).
(c) sin r = sin 50° / 1.50 = 0.7660 / 1.50 = 0.5107 ⇒ r = 30.7° (2). d = 7.5 × sin(50° − 30.7°) / cos 30.7° = 7.5 × 0.3305 / 0.8599 = 2.88 cm (2).

2. A ray of light is incident at i = 40° on a rectangular glass block of thickness t = 8.0 cm. The refracted ray hits the opposite parallel face at a distance EF = 4.0 cm from the foot of the normal. Calculate: (a) the length of the path inside the glass, (b) the angle of refraction r and refractive index n, and (c) the lateral displacement d.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Path length ON = √(8.0² + 4.0²) = √80 = 8.94 cm (3).
(b) sin r = 4.0 / 8.944 = 0.4472 ⇒ r = 26.6° (2); n = sin 40° / 0.4472 = 0.6428 / 0.4472 = 1.44 (2).
(c) d = ON sin(40° − 26.6°) = 8.944 × sin 13.4° = 8.944 × 0.2317 = 2.07 cm (3).

3. (a) Explain why a 60° triangular glass prism produces a wide angular spectrum from white light whereas a rectangular glass block does not. (b) State what happens to the red and violet rays inside and outside the rectangular glass block.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) In a rectangular block, opposite faces are parallel so the second refraction cancels the angular deviation of the first (D_net = 0°). In a triangular prism, the inclined faces bend rays towards the base at both surfaces, adding angular dispersion (5).
(b) Inside the rectangular block, violet refracts closer to the normal than red (n_v > n_r, so r_v < r_r). Outside in air, both emerge at e = i parallel to the incident ray, with violet laterally shifted slightly more than red (d_v > d_r) (5).
