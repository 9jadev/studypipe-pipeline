Real Depth, Apparent Depth & Multi-Layer Slabs

Let’s derive the paraxial depth equations and master the wording distinction that decides WAEC and JAMB marks.

1 · Refractive Index from Real & Apparent Depth

When a small object O at the bottom of a transparent medium of real thickness R and refractive index n is viewed nearly vertically from air above, rays from O refract away from the normal into air (sin e = n sin r). For small paraxial angles where sin θ ≈ tan θ:

n = sin e ÷ sin r ≈ tan e ÷ tan r = Real Depth (R) ÷ Apparent Depth (A)   ⇒   A = R ÷ n

2 · Upward Vertical Shift (dv) & Multi-Layer Summation

The distance through which the object appears lifted or raised upward towards the top surface is the vertical displacement (dv):

dv = R − A = R − (R ÷ n) = R (1 − 1 ÷ n)   (also equals travelling microscope raise Δh!)
Multi-layer immiscible slabs:   Atotal = (t1 ÷ n1) + (t2 ÷ n2) + (t3 ÷ n3) + …
Total upward shift across layers:   dv,total = Rtotal − Atotal = Σ ti(1 − 1 ÷ ni)

Active 5-Minute Movement Break (The No-Parallax Finger Test): Hold your left index finger at arm’s length ("object image I") and your right index finger 15 cm closer to your nose ("misaligned search pin"). Sway your head left and right — see how your two fingers slide apart (parallax)! Now bring your right finger to touch the tip of your left finger and sway your head again — they stay locked together with zero relative motion (no parallax)!

The #1 WAEC/JAMB Wording Trap: “Appears at Depth” vs “Appears Raised By”

Worked examples, step by step

Attempt each problem on paper first, then check the step-by-step WAEC/JAMB working.

EXAMPLE 1

Finding real depth from upward vertical shift d_v

A printed letter underneath a rectangular glass block of refractive index 1.50 appears to be raised by 2.4 cm when viewed vertically from above. Calculate: (a) the real thickness R of the glass block, and (b) the apparent depth A of the letter.

  1. Identify given quantity: “Appears raised by 2.4 cm” gives upward shift d_v = 2.4 cm.
  2. Use d_v = R(1 − 1/n) to find R: 2.4 = R × (1 − 1/1.50) = R × (1 − 2/3) = R ÷ 3 ⇒ R = 3 × 2.4 = 7.2 cm.
  3. Apparent depth A: A = R − d_v = 7.2 − 2.4 = 4.8 cm (check: 7.2 ÷ 4.8 = 1.50).
(a) Real thickness R = 7.2 cm  ·  (b) Apparent depth A = 4.8 cm
EXAMPLE 2

Multi-layer immiscible media (glass + water)

A rectangular glass block of thickness 6.0 cm and refractive index 1.50 rests at the bottom of a flat-bottomed beaker. Water of refractive index 4/3 is poured into the beaker to a depth of 8.0 cm above the top of the glass block. Calculate: (a) the total apparent depth of a coin under the glass block when viewed vertically from above, and (b) the total upward vertical shift of the coin.

  1. Apparent depth through glass layer: A₁ = t₁ ÷ n₁ = 6.0 ÷ 1.50 = 4.0 cm.
  2. Apparent depth through water layer: A₂ = t₂ ÷ n₂ = 8.0 ÷ (4/3) = 8.0 × 3/4 = 6.0 cm.
  3. Total apparent depth & upward shift: A_total = A₁ + A₂ = 4.0 + 6.0 = 10.0 cm. Since R_total = 6.0 + 8.0 = 14.0 cm, upward shift d_v = 14.0 − 10.0 = 4.0 cm.
(a) Atotal = 10.0 cm  ·  (b) Total upward shift dv = 4.0 cm
EXAMPLE 3

Travelling microscope 3-reading method

In a travelling microscope experiment: (1) focused on an ink cross on the bench, the vertical scale reads h₁ = 1.40 cm; (2) with a glass block placed over the cross and refocused on the image of the cross, it reads h₂ = 3.50 cm; (3) focused on lycopodium powder sprinkled on the top surface of the glass block, it reads h₃ = 7.70 cm. Calculate the real thickness R, apparent depth A, and refractive index n of the glass block.

  1. Real thickness R of block: R = h₃ − h₁ = 7.70 − 1.40 = 6.30 cm.
  2. Apparent depth A (from top surface to image): A = h₃ − h₂ = 7.70 − 3.50 = 4.20 cm (and upward shift d_v = h₂ − h₁ = 2.10 cm).
  3. Refractive index n: n = R ÷ A = 6.30 ÷ 4.20 = 1.50.
R = 6.30 cm  ·  A = 4.20 cm  ·  n = 1.50

Assignment: Real & Apparent Depth

Part A · Home Practical: Coin in a Bowl of Water

Place a coin at the bottom of a bowl or bucket filled with 12 cm of water (n = 4/3). Hold a second coin in the air beside the bowl and adjust its height while looking from above until both coins appear at the same level (no parallax). Calculate the expected apparent depth A and upward rise d_v.

Practical task
Sample answer unlocks after you try

For R = 12.0 cm and n = 4/3: apparent depth A = R ÷ n = 12.0 ÷ (4/3) = 9.0 cm below the water surface, so the submerged coin appears raised by d_v = 12.0 − 9.0 = 3.0 cm from the bottom.

Part B · Structured Written Questions (30 marks)

1. (a) Distinguish between real depth and apparent depth. (b) Prove that the upward vertical displacement d_v of an object viewed vertically through a parallel-sided block of thickness R and refractive index n is d_v = R(1 − 1/n). (c) A glass block of refractive index 1.50 makes an ink dot on paper appear raised by 1.8 cm. Find the thickness of the block and the apparent depth of the dot.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Real depth R is the actual physical distance of an object below the surface of a medium, while apparent depth A is the distance of its virtual image below the surface when viewed from outside (2).
(b) Since n = R / A ⇒ A = R / n, upward displacement d_v = R − A = R − R/n = R(1 − 1/n) (4).
(c) 1.8 = R(1 − 1/1.50) = R/3 ⇒ R = 5.4 cm (2); A = 5.4 − 1.8 = 3.6 cm (2).

2. A rectangular tank has a 9.0 cm thick glass slab (n = 1.50) at its base, covered by 12.0 cm of water (n = 4/3) and 7.0 cm of kerosene (n = 1.40). Calculate: (a) the total real depth of the bottom, (b) the total apparent depth when viewed vertically from above, and (c) the total apparent upward shift of the bottom.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) R_total = 9.0 + 12.0 + 7.0 = 28.0 cm (2).
(b) A_total = (9.0 / 1.50) + (12.0 / (4/3)) + (7.0 / 1.40) = 6.0 + 9.0 + 5.0 = 20.0 cm (5).
(c) Total upward shift d_v = R_total − A_total = 28.0 − 20.0 = 8.0 cm (3).

3. Describe with the aid of equations how you would determine the refractive index of a rectangular glass block in the school laboratory using: (a) the no-parallax method with two optical pins, and (b) a travelling microscope and lycopodium powder.

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Place object pin P₁ beneath the glass block of measured thickness R. Clamp search pin P₂ on a retort stand beside the block and adjust its height while viewing vertically from above until there is no parallax between P₂ and the virtual image of P₁. Measure height d_v of P₂ above the table; compute A = R − d_v and n = R / A (5).
(b) Record microscope vertical scale readings when focused on (i) an ink mark on the bench (h₁), (ii) the virtual image of the mark through the block (h₂), and (iii) lycopodium powder on the top surface of the block (h₃). Compute R = h₃ − h₁, A = h₃ − h₂, and n = (h₃ − h₁) / (h₃ − h₂) (5).
