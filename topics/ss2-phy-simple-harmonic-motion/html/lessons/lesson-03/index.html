3Make sense · 15 min

SHM is the shadow of a circle

Now connect what the shadow did to the equations WAEC, NECO and JAMB expect.

The reference circle: a point moving round a circle of radius A at a steady angular speed ω casts a shadow on a diameter. That shadow moves in SHM with amplitude A and the same ω. Timing from the centre, after time t the radius has turned through ωt, so the shadow's displacement is x = A sin ωt. One turn of the circle is one complete oscillation, so they share the same period T = 2π/ω.

Equations

x = A sin ωt · v = ω√(A² − x²) · a = −ω²x
vmax = ωA (at x = 0) · amax = ω²A (at x = ±A)

Always work out ωt in radians before taking the sine (set your calculator to RAD).

The lift-off rule: the carrier can push the crate up, but it cannot pull it down. Only gravity pulls the crate down, so the crate can never accelerate downwards faster than g. At the top of the bounce the carrier accelerates downwards at ω²A. If ω²A > g, the carrier drops away faster than the crate can fall, and the crate leaves it. So the crates stay on only while A < g/ω².

4Apply · Worked examples · 10 min

Worked examples, step by step

Try each on paper first, then check the working. Calculator in radians.

EXAMPLE 1

Speed and acceleration at a point

A body moves in SHM with A = 0.10 m and ω = 5.0 rad s⁻¹. Find its speed and acceleration when x = 0.060 m, and its maximum speed.

  1. Speed formula: v = ω√(A² − x²) = 5.0 × √(0.10² − 0.060²) = 5.0 × √(0.010 − 0.0036)
  2. Evaluate the speed: v = 5.0 × √0.0064 = 5.0 × 0.080 = 0.40 m s⁻¹
  3. Acceleration: a = −ω²x = −25 × 0.060 = −1.5 m s⁻² (the minus sign means towards the centre)
  4. Maximum speed: v_max = ωA = 5.0 × 0.10 = 0.50 m s⁻¹
v = 0.40 m s⁻¹ · a = 1.5 m s⁻² towards O · v_max = 0.50 m s⁻¹
EXAMPLE 2

Displacement at a time

The okada's carrier bounces with A = 5.0 cm and f = 2.0 Hz, timed from the centre. Find its displacement at t = 0.0625 s.

  1. Angular frequency: ω = 2πf = 2π × 2.0 = 4π rad s⁻¹
  2. Phase angle in radians: ωt = 4π × 0.0625 = π/4 = 0.785 rad
  3. Displacement: x = A sin ωt = 5.0 × sin(π/4) = 5.0 × 0.707 = 3.5 cm
x ≈ 3.5 cm

Story numbers are a teaching model, not real measurements.

EXAMPLE 3

A gentle bounce

At f = 2.0 Hz the carrier bounces with A = 3.0 cm. Find vmax and amax. Do the crates stay on? (g = 9.8 m s⁻²)

  1. Maximum speed: v_max = ωA = 12.6 × 0.030 = 0.38 m s⁻¹ (ω = 2π × 2.0 = 12.6 rad s⁻¹)
  2. Maximum acceleration: a_max = ω²A = 158 × 0.030 = 4.7 m s⁻²
  3. Compare with g: 4.7 m s⁻² < 9.8 m s⁻², so the carrier never accelerates downwards faster than g.
v_max ≈ 0.38 m s⁻¹ · a_max ≈ 4.7 m s⁻² · crates stay on
EXAMPLE 4

The lift-off threshold

Find the smallest amplitude at which the crates leave the carrier at f = 2.0 Hz. (g = 9.8 m s⁻²)

  1. Condition: lift-off starts when ω²A = g, so A = g/ω²
  2. Square ω: ω² = 12.57² = 158 s⁻²
  3. Threshold: A = g ÷ ω² = 9.8 ÷ 158 = 0.062 m = 6.2 cm
A ≥ 6.2 cm (the same as the okada's static sag mg/k)

Assignment: Part A and Part B

A home investigation and a 30-mark written paper. Type your attempt first to unlock each model solution.

Part A · Home investigation: the shadow of a turning wheel

Where does the shadow move fastest, and where does it turn round?

You need: a bicycle (or a basin lid or round tray that can turn on a nail), a bottle cap and tape, a torch, a plain wall, a pencil and paper. Work with an adult; keep fingers away from the spokes.

  1. Turn the bicycle upside down so the front wheel spins freely, or fix the lid on a nail so it can turn. Tape the bottle cap to the rim.
  2. In a dim room, shine the torch from the side so the cap throws a shadow on the wall.
  3. Turn the wheel slowly and as steadily as you can. Watch the shadow, not the cap.
  4. Mark on the wall (with tape) the two ends of the shadow's path and its middle.
  5. Turn the wheel through quarter turns, pausing each time, and note where the shadow is.
  6. Describe where the shadow moves fastest and where it slows down and turns round.

A good answer shows that the shadow's path is as long as the wheel's diameter, so its amplitude is the radius; the shadow is fastest at the middle and momentarily still at the ends, exactly like the bouncing carrier; and one turn of the wheel is one complete oscillation of the shadow, so they share the same period. A strong answer links this to the reference circle, explains that the shadow must accelerate hardest at the ends (it stops and reverses there), and names a source of error, for example the wheel was not turned at a steady rate, or the torch was so close that the shadow was enlarged unevenly.

Part B · Written paper (30 marks)

1. Definitions

3 marks

(a) Define simple harmonic motion in terms of acceleration. (b) What is meant by the reference circle for an SHM? (c) Define angular frequency, and give its unit.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) SHM is motion in which a body's acceleration is directly proportional to how far it is displaced from a fixed point and always points back towards that point. (1) (b) A circle of radius equal to the amplitude, round which a point moves at constant angular speed ω; the projection (shadow) of that point on a diameter moves with the SHM. (1) (c) Angular frequency is 2π times the frequency (ω = 2πf = 2π/T), the angle in radians swept per second by the point on the reference circle; unit rad s⁻¹. (1)

Mark scheme note: In (b) both "radius = amplitude" (or "point moving uniformly") and "projection on a diameter" are needed. In (c) the unit is required.

3. From the Circle to the Formulae

4 marks

A point P moves round a circle of radius A at constant angular speed ω, starting on the horizontal diameter at t = 0. N is the projection of P on the vertical diameter. (a) Show that the displacement of N from the centre is x = A sin ωt. (b) Given that the velocity of N is v = ωA cos ωt, show that v = ω√(A² − x²). (c) State the maximum speed and where it occurs.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) After time t the radius OP has turned through angle θ = ωt; the vertical component of OP is A sin θ, so x = A sin ωt. (1) (b) cos ωt = √(1 − sin² ωt) = √(1 − x²/A²) (1); so v = ωA√(1 − x²/A²) = ω√(A² − x²). (1) (c) vmax = ωA, when x = 0 (at the equilibrium position). (1)

Mark scheme note: In (a) the angle ωt must be identified. In (b) the identity sin² + cos² = 1 must be used or clearly implied; quoting the result without working earns no mark. In (c) the position is required.

4. A Bouncing Load

5 marks

A load on a spring oscillates in SHM with amplitude 0.12 m and period 0.80 s, timed from the equilibrium position. Calculate: (a) its angular frequency; (b) its maximum speed; (c) its maximum acceleration; (d) its speed when it is 0.060 m from equilibrium; (e) its displacement at t = 0.10 s.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) ω = 2π/T = 6.283 ÷ 0.80 = 7.85 rad s⁻¹ (1) (b) v_max = ωA = 7.85 × 0.12 = 0.94 m s⁻¹ (1) (c) a_max = ω²A = 61.7 × 0.12 = 7.4 m s⁻² (1) (d) v = ω√(A² − x²) = 7.85 × √(0.0144 − 0.0036) = 7.85 × 0.104 = 0.82 m s⁻¹ (1) (e) ωt = 7.85 × 0.10 = 0.785 rad (π/4); x = 0.12 × sin(π/4) = 0.12 × 0.707 = 0.085 m (1)

Mark scheme note: Error carried forward from (a). In (e) a calculator in degree mode gives 0.0016 m and loses the mark. Units required.

5. The Lift-off Threshold

4 marks

The okada's rear bounces at its natural frequency f₀. (Take g = 9.8 m s⁻².) (a) Explain why a crate resting loosely on the carrier leaves it when ω²A > g. (b) With 200 kg on the carrier, ω = 12.57 rad s⁻¹. Find the smallest amplitude for lift-off. (c) With 250 kg, ω = 11.24 rad s⁻¹. Find the new threshold and say whether a 7.0 cm bounce would now throw the crates.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) At the top of the bounce the carrier accelerates downwards at ω²A; only gravity pulls the crate down, so it can only fall at g. If ω²A exceeds g the carrier moves down faster than the crate and they separate. (1) (b) A = g/ω² = 9.8 ÷ 158 = 0.062 m = 6.2 cm (1) (c) A = 9.8 ÷ 126.4 = 0.078 m = 7.8 cm (1); 7.0 cm is below 7.8 cm, so the crates would stay on (just). (1)

Mark scheme note: In (a) the comparison of the carrier's downward acceleration with g is essential; "the bike is going fast" scores zero. In (c) the conclusion must follow from the comparison, error carried forward allowed.

7. Sketching x, v and a

3 marks

On one time axis, describe (or sketch) the graphs of x, v and a against t for one cycle of SHM starting at equilibrium, and state the phase relationship of v and of a to x.

Model solution unlocks after you try

x is a sine curve starting at zero and rising. (1) v is a cosine curve starting at its maximum: it is a quarter of a cycle (90°) ahead of x. (1) a is an inverted sine curve, exactly opposite to x (half a cycle, 180°, out of phase), with peaks of size ω²A where x = ±A. (1)

Mark scheme note: Sketches must be correctly aligned in time. "a is the same as x" (missing the inversion) loses the third mark.

8. The Bridge: Correcting Tunde

2 marks

Tunde says the crates jump off "where the bike is moving fastest". Correct him in two sentences, using the ideas of this lesson.

Model solution unlocks after you try

The carrier is fastest at the middle of its bounce, where its acceleration is zero, so it is not throwing anything off there. (1) The crates leave at the top of the bounce, where the carrier is momentarily still but has its greatest downward acceleration ω²A; when that exceeds g the carrier drops away from under them, because only gravity pulls the crates down. (1)

Mark scheme note: Both "speed greatest at the middle" and "acceleration greatest at the top, compared with g" are needed for full marks.
