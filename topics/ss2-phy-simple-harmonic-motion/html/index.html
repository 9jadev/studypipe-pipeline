The Unit Mystery · Yaba Market, Lagos

Why does Mama Ngozi’s antique grandfather clock lose 14 minutes every day?

Mama Ngozi just imported a brass-pendulum grandfather clock that kept exact time in London (T = 2.000 s per complete tick-tock swing). Inside her Yaba Market shop in Lagos, however, the clock steadily falls behind by 14 minutes every 24 hours!

Her apprentice Chidi tried to fix it by taping three heavy brass washers onto the pendulum bob — reasoning that a heavier bob should fall faster. Yet the clock still lost the exact same 14 minutes! Meanwhile, outside the shop, a petrol generator vibrates rapidly while its cooling fan spins in one direction at 1500 rpm.

Why does the fan’s rotation fail the test for Simple Harmonic Motion while the pendulum obeys it? Why did Chidi’s extra mass make zero difference? And by how many millimetres must Tunde adjust the brass screw under the bob so the clock keeps accurate time in Lagos (g = 9.78 m s⁻²)?

The 6 calculation archetypes

Every WAEC, NECO, or JAMB numerical problem on Simple Harmonic Motion is one of these six patterns:

Type 1 · Lesson 1 & 2

Period, Frequency & ω

Given N oscillations in time t, find T = t/N, f = N/t, and ω = 2πf.

Type 2 · Lesson 2

Instantaneous & Max Speed

Find v_max = ωA at equilibrium or speed v = ω√(A² − x²) at any displacement x.

Type 3 · Lesson 2

Restoring Acceleration

Compute acceleration magnitude |a| = ω²x at position x or peak acceleration a_max = ω²A.

Type 4 · Lesson 3

Pendulum Scaling Ratios

Use T₁/T₂ = √(L₁/L₂) to find the new period when string length L is doubled or quadrupled.

Type 5 · Lesson 4

Gravity g from L–T² Slope

From slope S = ΔL/ΔT², evaluate g = 4π²S (or g = 4π²/S' for T² vs L).

Type 6 · Lesson 5

Energy & Spiral Springs

Use E = ½kA² = ½mω²A² and spring period T = 2π√(m/k) to find mass, force constant, or speed.
