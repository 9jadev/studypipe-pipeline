01 / The Phenomenon

Slow is safe, fast is safe, but one speed breaks the eggs.

Every lesson in this unit closes one piece of the same case file, from a bouncing okada outside Ogbomoso to a pendulum on Mrs Okafor’s lab bench.

The Unit Mystery · Ridge Road, Ogbomoso

The Cracked Crates of Ridge Road

Outside Ogbomoso, Hajia Ramatu packs trays of eggs into crates every dawn, and Dayo rides them to the town market on his okada. Halfway there, the laterite road crosses a run of low concrete drainage ridges laid by the council, all about the same distance apart.

Her daughter Zainab, an SS2 student, keeps a notebook of the losses, and the pattern is strange: slow trips are fine, fast trips are fine, but trips at one middle speed end in broken eggs. Her classmate Tunde is sure that faster always means worse. Their physics teacher, Mrs Okafor, says the bike is behaving like a mass on a spring, and that physics can name the dangerous speed before another tray is lost.

Why does one in-between speed throw the crates off, and what must Dayo and Mallam Sule change?

  • Clue 1Crates arrive whole at 10 km/h and at 30 km/h.
  • Clue 2Crates crack at about 18 km/h.
  • Clue 3With two extra crates, the bad speed falls to about 16 km/h.
  • Clue 4Mallam Sule finds the rear shock absorber weeping oil.

Realism note: story numbers are a teaching model: chosen to be realistic and easy to work with, not measured on a real okada or road. (Teachers: 200 kg on the rear spring, a 2 Hz bounce, ridges 2.5 m apart; real suspensions and roads vary.)

The 6 calculation archetypes

Almost every number question on simple harmonic motion in WAEC, NECO and JAMB is one of these six. Each step has a note under it.

Type 1 · Lesson 1

Timing an oscillation

  1. T = t/n = 10.0 ÷ 20 = 0.50 sZainab counts 20 bounces of the parked okada in 10.0 s.
  2. f = 1/T = 1 ÷ 0.50 = 2.0 HzFrequency is the reciprocal of the period.
  3. ω = 2πf = 2 × 3.142 × 2.0 = 12.6 rad s⁻¹Angular frequency is 2π times the frequency.
Type 2 · Lesson 3

SHM kinematics

  1. v = ω√(A² − x²) = 5.0√(0.010 − 0.0036)A = 0.10 m, ω = 5.0 rad s⁻¹, at x = 0.060 m.
  2. v = 5.0 × 0.080 = 0.40 m s⁻¹The square root of 0.0064 is 0.080.
  3. a = −ω²x = −25 × 0.060 = −1.5 m s⁻²The minus sign: the acceleration points back to the centre.
Type 3 · Lesson 2

The loaded spring

  1. k = F/x = (0.30 × 9.8) ÷ 0.060 = 49 N m⁻¹A 0.30 kg load stretches the spring by 6.0 cm.
  2. T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(0.30 ÷ 49) = 0.49 sMore mass gives a longer period; a stiffer spring a shorter one.
  3. k = 4π²/slope = 39.48 ÷ 1.61 = 24.5 N m⁻¹From the gradient of a T² against m graph.
Type 4 · Lesson 4

The simple pendulum

  1. T = 2π√(l/g) = 2π√(0.90 ÷ 9.8) = 1.90 sOnly the length and gravity count, for small swings.
  2. T₂/T₁ = √(l₂/l₁) = √(0.90 ÷ 0.40) = 1.5A ratio problem: the 1.27 s pendulum becomes 1.90 s.
  3. l = gT²/4π² = (9.8 × 2.0²) ÷ 39.5 = 0.99 mThe seconds pendulum: a period of 2.0 s.
Type 5 · Lesson 5

Practical g from a graph

  1. s = (4.032 − 1.613) ÷ (1.000 − 0.400) = 4.03 s² m⁻¹Slope of the T² against l line from a large triangle.
  2. g = 4π²/s = 39.48 ÷ 4.03 = 9.79 m s⁻²Our class value for g.
  3. H = 1.80 + 4.028 ÷ 4.03 = 2.80 mCeiling variant: the T² against y line meets the axis at the ceiling height.
Type 6 · Lessons 6–7

Energy and resonance

  1. E = ½kA² = ½ × 50 × 0.10² = 0.25 JTotal energy depends on the amplitude squared.
  2. KE = E − ½kx² = 0.25 − 0.090 = 0.16 JAt x = 0.060 m, the rest of the energy is kinetic.
  3. v = d f₀ = 2.5 × 2.0 = 5.0 m s⁻¹ = 18 km h⁻¹The ridges push at the natural frequency: resonance.
