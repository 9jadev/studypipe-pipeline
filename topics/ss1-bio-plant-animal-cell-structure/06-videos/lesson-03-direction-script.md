# Direction Script: Lesson 03 · One cell or billions? Forms of cells, levels and SA : V

## Overview
The video answers the lesson's guiding question, why an *Amoeba* can live alone in drain water while Chinedu needs a heart, lungs
and blood. It walks through the forms of cells (free-living, colony, filament, tissue), cell nutrition, the levels of organisation,
then SA : V = 6/L with the lesson's three worked examples. It opens the lesson's **Make Sense** phase, after the pond survey and
the cube bench. The 12-question quiz and the exit ticket (5 mm cube) stay in Apply / Reflect and are not used here.

**Accuracy notes:**
- Numbers are the lesson's exactly: 2 mm cube → 24 mm², 8 mm³, 3.0 mm⁻¹; 1 mm → 6.0, 6 mm → 1.0, ratio 6; *Amoeba* 0.1 mm → 60 mm⁻¹,
  frog 30 mm → 0.2 mm⁻¹, ratio 300; bench table 1/2/3/4/6 mm → 6.0/3.0/2.0/1.5/1.0 mm⁻¹.
- The *Amoeba* and frog are **modelled** as cubes (the lesson's wording). Narration says "model … as a cube"; real *Amoeba* sizes vary.
- *Volvox*: "hundreds, even thousands of cells" (true range roughly 500 to tens of thousands). Narration keeps the lesson's honest
  wording: jobs are **hardly** shared (a few *Volvox* cells are reproductive), not "never".
- *Euglena*: one emergent flagellum, eyespot, chloroplasts, no cellulose wall; **saprophytic** in darkness (not "holozoic").
  *Chlamydomonas*: **two** flagella, one cup-shaped chloroplast, cellulose wall.
- *Hydra*: ectoderm and endoderm (with non-cellular mesoglea between, not named in the video). Stinging cells catch food, endoderm
  cells digest, outer cells cover.
- Ladder example uses Chinedu's body (mitochondrion → heart muscle cell → heart muscle → heart → circulatory system → Chinedu), so it
  does not give away quiz Q8 (the ugu-plant ladder). The lesson's own examples (heart as an organ; *Euglena* as a cell) agree.
- Mistake 1 uses the 2 mm cube (8/24 = 0.33) rather than quiz Q9's 8 mm cube, and the your-turn uses a new 12 mm cube, so no quiz
  or exit-ticket answer is revealed.
- Dye model: dye soaks the same depth (0.5 mm in the kit) into every face in the same time; the pale centre has side L − 2 × 0.5 mm.
  This is shown, never put as a number on the board.

## Chapter 3: Colony, filament or tissue? (about 1 min 30 s)
Many cells is not the same as a tissue: division of labour is.

| # | Narration | On screen | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | Some cells live together. Volvox is a hollow green ball of hundreds, even thousands, of cells, each very like a Chlamydomonas. | Board (clear): **Living together** · Kit scene compare: Volvox, Spirogyra, Hydra, labels on · 📷 WIDE → Volvox | structure |
| 3.2 | The cells are joined by thin strands of cytoplasm. A group of similar cells living together like this is a colony. | Board: Volvox = colony | term |
| 3.3 | Spirogyra is a green thread. Its identical cells are joined end to end, each with a spiral ribbon chloroplast. That is a filament. | Board: Spirogyra = filament · 📷 WIDE → Spirogyra | term |
| 3.4 | Now Hydra, a tiny pond animal. Its body has two layers of cells, an outer ectoderm and an inner endoderm. | Board: Hydra: ectoderm + endoderm · 📷 WIDE → Hydra | structure |
| 3.5 | And its cells do different jobs. Some sting and catch food, some digest it, and some cover and protect the body. | Kit: jobs on (Hydra cells coloured by job; Volvox cells all one colour) · Board: cells do different jobs | key contrast |
| 3.6 | Coach Danjuma says Volvox is a tissue because it has many cells. Is he right? Watch as we take one cell out of each. | Board: Danjuma: Volvox = tissue? · 📷 WIDE | misconception |
| 3.7 | The Volvox cell swims off and keeps living. The Hydra stinging cell cannot survive alone. It can only do its one job. | Kit: separate on: Volvox cell swims away ✓, Hydra cell fades ✗ | evidence |
| 3.8 | So the difference is division of labour. In a tissue, cells are specialised and depend on each other. In a colony, they are alike and nearly self-sufficient. | Board (boxed): tissue = specialised cells sharing jobs · note: not just "many cells" | key definition |

## Chapter 5: The answer is in a cube (SA : V = 6/L, about 1 min 20 s)
Why size matters: surface takes in, volume uses up.

| # | Narration | On screen | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 | Now the big question. Why can Amoeba live on diffusion alone, while Chinedu needs a heart and blood? The answer is in a cube. | Board (clear): **Size and surface** · Kit `sa-volume-cubes`: one cube, L = 2 mm · 📷 WIDE | question |
| 5.2 | Oxygen and food enter through the surface, but every bit of the volume inside needs them. | Board: in through the surface · needed by the volume | idea |
| 5.3 | Take a cube of side L. It has six faces, and each face is L by L. So the surface area is six L squared. | Kit: faces on (six faces, each L²) · Board: SA = 6L² · 📷 WIDE → cube | formula |
| 5.4 | The volume is L times L times L, which is L cubed. | Kit: fill on (unit cubes fill the cube) · Board: V = L³ | formula |
| 5.5 | Surface area to volume ratio is surface area divided by volume. Six L squared over L cubed simplifies to six over L. | Board: SA:V = 6L²/L³ · then = 6/L (boxed) · 📷 WIDE | key formula |
| 5.6 | Six over L. So as a cube gets bigger, its surface area to volume ratio gets smaller. | Kit: L grows 2 → 6 mm · Board: note bigger L, smaller SA:V | key idea |
