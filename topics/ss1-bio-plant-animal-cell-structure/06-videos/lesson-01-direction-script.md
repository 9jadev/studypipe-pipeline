# Direction Script: Lesson 01 · Seeing the cell: microscopes, cell size and the cell theory

## Overview
The video answers the lesson's guiding question ("a ugu leaf and a drop of blood look nothing alike, so what do they share, and
how do we see it?"): it tells the story from Hooke's dead cork to the cell theory, shows why a compound microscope's powers
**multiply**, turns a field of view into a cell width in µm, works back from a photo to a real cell, and separates prokaryotes
from eukaryotes. It opens the lesson's **Make Sense** phase, after the microscope bench. The Bridge (which slide is the ugu leaf,
which is the blood, and the 8 µm red cell) and the quiz stay in Apply; the video never says which cells have walls, so the
Bridge keeps its reveal.

**Accuracy notes:**
- Dates as taught in the lesson: Hooke 1665 (dead cork, named "cells"), Dujardin 1835 (living jelly, "sarcode"), Schleiden 1838
  (plants), Schwann 1839 (animals), Virchow 1855 (new cells from existing cells; "useful though not required").
  Dujardin is "about a hundred and seventy years later" (1835 − 1665 = 170).
- Cell-theory wording follows `02-lesson.md` § Make Sense exactly; the cell and organelle definitions follow it too.
- The field-of-view numbers are the lesson's idealised bench values (onion peel): ×40 → 4.0 mm, 80 cells; ×100 → 1.6 mm,
  32 cells; ×400 → 0.40 mm, 8 cells; each gives 0.050 mm = 50 µm. They are internally consistent (field diameter × total
  magnification = 160 mm at every power), so the video can say "the window shrinks, the cell does not".
- Onion cells are long boxes; "width" is measured across the short side, with cells counted side by side along one diameter.
- A wall does not separate prokaryotes from eukaryotes (bacteria and plant cells both have walls). This mirrors the b1/b7 mark
  scheme notes in `03-questions.md`. Prokaryotes DO have ribosomes (smaller); the video says so (quiz Q12 trap).
- The blood smear is shown as cells (round red cells, no nucleus) only to say "blood is made of cells too"; the narration never
  says which slide has walls (that is the Bridge's job).
- The your-turn question uses **new numbers** (eyepiece ×5, objective ×40, 0.80 mm field, 20 cells → ×200; 40 µm), so it does
  not give away the Reflect exit ticket (×10 / ×10 / 2.0 mm / 20 cells) or any quiz or paper item.

## Chapter 5: Same cell, smaller window (field of view, about 1 min 40 s)
The field shrinks as magnification rises; the cell does not change.

| # | Narration | On screen | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 | Now let's look down the eyepiece at Chinedu's onion peel. The bright circle you see is called the field of view. | Board: new board "Field of view" · Kit: `field`, slide onion, mag ×40, field 4.0 mm, ruler on · 📷 WIDE | setup |
| 5.2 | At times forty, the field is four point zero millimetres across, and eighty cells lie side by side across it. | Board: ×40: 4.0 mm, 80 cells · Kit: count on (row highlighted, counter 80) · 📷 WIDE → row | observation |
| 5.3 | Switch to times one hundred. The field shrinks to one point six millimetres, and only thirty-two cells fit across. | Board: ×100: 1.6 mm, 32 cells · Kit: mag ×100, field 1.6 mm (cells grow on screen, counter 32) | observation |
| 5.4 | At times four hundred, the field is only zero point four zero millimetres, with eight cells across. | Board: ×400: 0.40 mm, 8 cells · Kit: mag ×400, field 0.40 mm (counter 8) | observation |
| 5.5 | Did the onion cells grow? No. The picture grew and the window got smaller. The real size of each cell never changes. | Board: real cell size: unchanged · 📷 WIDE (back out) | misconception |
| 5.6 | So the width of one cell is the field diameter divided by the number of cells across it. | Board: new board "Cell width from the field" · line: width = field ÷ number of cells (boxed) | formula |
| 5.7 | Check it at every power. Four point zero over eighty, one point six over thirty-two, and zero point four zero over eight. | Board: 4.0 ÷ 80 = 1.6 ÷ 32 = 0.40 ÷ 8 | check |
| 5.8 | All three give the same answer, zero point zero five zero millimetres. Magnifying changes the image, not the cell. | Board: = 0.050 mm (boxed) | key insight |
