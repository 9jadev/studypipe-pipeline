  3. Oxidative phosphorylation on the cristae stops, so ATP yield collapses from 38 ATP per glucose down to only 2 ATP per glucose from cytoplasmic glycolysis.

Complete Guide to Eukaryotic Organelles & Plant vs Animal Cells

Master the structure, membranes, and exact WAEC/JAMB functions of every component inside plant and animal cells.

1 · Boundaries, Cytoplasm & The Nucleus

  • Cell Wall (Plants, Fungi, Bacteria — absent in Animals): In plants, a rigid, non-living outer layer composed of cellulose microfibrils cemented by pectin and hemicellulose (middle lamella). It is freely permeable, provides mechanical support, gives fixed polygonal shape, and prevents osmotic bursting. Cytoplasmic bridges called plasmodesmata pass through pits to link adjacent cells.
  • Plasma (Cell Surface) Membrane: Living, thin (~7.5 nm), flexible phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins and cholesterol (Fluid-Mosaic Model). It is selectively (differentially) permeable, regulating the entry and exit of ions, water, nutrients, and wastes.
  • Cytoplasm (Cytosol): Translucent, aqueous colloidal jelly (70–90% water, proteins, salts, glucose, amino acids) where metabolic reactions such as glycolysis occur and organelles are suspended.
  • Nucleus (“Control Centre”): Bounded by a perforated double nuclear envelope (nuclear pores regulate mRNA and ribosome traffic). Contains nucleoplasm, chromatin (linear DNA wrapped around histone proteins, condensing into chromosomes during division), and a dense non-membranous nucleolus that synthesises ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and assembles ribosomal subunits.

2 · Semi-Autonomous Energy Organelles: Mitochondria & Plastids

  • Mitochondrion (“Powerhouse of the Cell”): Rod-shaped or spherical double-membrane organelle (0.5–2 µm wide). The outer membrane is smooth; the inner membrane is folded into shelf-like cristae studded with stalked ATP synthase ($F_0F_1$) particles and electron-transport chains. The inner fluid matrix contains Krebs cycle enzymes, circular naked DNA, and 70S ribosomes. Site of aerobic respiration (36–38 ATP per glucose).
  • Plastids (Plant & Algal Cells Only):
    • Chloroplasts: Green double-membrane plastids containing chlorophyll. Coin-like thylakoids stack into grana (connected by intergranal lamellae) where the light-dependent stage (photolysis of water + photophosphorylation) occurs. The colourless fluid stroma contains RuBisCO for the Calvin cycle (dark stage), starch grains, circular DNA, and 70S ribosomes.
    • Chromoplasts: Pigmented plastids containing red, orange, or yellow carotenoids (carotene, xanthophyll) in flower petals, ripe tomato fruits, and carrot roots to attract pollinators and seed dispersers.
    • Leucoplasts: Colourless storage plastids in roots, tubers, and seeds — including amyloplasts (store starch in yam/cassava tubers), elaioplasts (store lipids/oils), and aleuroplasts / proteinoplasts (store proteins).

3 · Endomembrane System, Ribosomes, Lysosomes, Centrioles & Vacuoles

  • Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER): Flattened membrane sacs (cisternae) continuous with the outer nuclear membrane and studded with 80S ribosomes. Synthesises, folds, and transports secretory and membrane proteins.
  • Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER): Tubular membranes lacking ribosomes. Synthesises lipids, phospholipids, and steroid hormones (testosterone, oestrogen), detoxifies drugs and poisons in liver hepatocytes, and stores $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions in muscle cells (sarcoplasmic reticulum).
  • Golgi Apparatus (Dictyosomes in Plants): Stack of curved membrane cisternae with a receiving cis face and shipping trans face. Modifies proteins (glycosylation into glycoproteins), packages secretory vesicles, forms lysosomes, synthesises cell-wall polysaccharides (pectin/hemicellulose), and forms the acrosome of spermatozoa.
  • Ribosomes (Non-Membrane-Bound): Tiny ribonucleoprotein particles (rRNA + protein; 80S in eukaryotic cytoplasm, 70S in prokaryotes, mitochondria, and chloroplasts). Site of translation (polypeptide synthesis).
  • Lysosomes (“Suicide Bags”): Single-membrane vesicles budded from the Golgi containing ~50 acidic hydrolytic enzymes (proteases, lipases, nucleases at $\text{pH} \approx 4.8$). Digest engulfed bacteria (phagocytosis), recycle worn-out organelles (autophagy), and self-digest damaged or developmental cells (autolysis, e.g., tadpole tail regression during metamorphosis).
  • Centrosome & Centrioles (Animal Cells & Lower Plants): Pair of perpendicular hollow cylinders made of 9 triplets of microtubules ($9+0$ arrangement). Organise the spindle fibres that pull chromosomes apart during mitosis and meiosis, and form the basal bodies of cilia and flagella ($9+2$ arrangement).
  • Vacuoles: In mature plant cells, a large permanent central sap vacuole bounded by a selectively permeable single membrane called the tonoplast stores water, sugars, amino acids, mineral salts, organic wastes, and anthocyanin pigments, while generating turgor pressure. Animal cells have only small, temporary vacuoles (food or pinocytic vacuoles); freshwater protists have contractile vacuoles for osmoregulation.

4 · Cellular Bioenergetics & Vacuolar Volume Equations

R_ATP = ATP_aerobic ÷ ATP_anaerobic = 38 ÷ 2 = 19
ΔATP = N_glucose × (38 − 2) = 15 × 36 = 540 ATP
%V_vacuole = (V_vacuole ÷ V_cell) × 100 = (160000 ÷ 200000) × 100 = 80%

EXAMPLE 1

Aerobic Mitochondrial ATP Yield vs Anaerobic Cytoplasmic Glycolysis

An active flight muscle cell oxidises 15 molecules of glucose completely inside its mitochondria (yielding 38 ATP per glucose molecule). Calculate: (a) the total ATP produced aerobically, (b) the ATP that would be produced if the 15 glucose molecules underwent anaerobic glycolysis in the cytoplasm (2 ATP per glucose), and (c) the net additional ATP gained due to the presence of mitochondria.

  1. Part (a) — Total aerobic ATP produced inside mitochondria: ATP_aerobic = 15 × 38 = 570 ATP.
  2. Part (b) — Total anaerobic ATP from cytoplasmic glycolysis alone: ATP_anaerobic = 15 × 2 = 30 ATP.
  3. Part (c) — Net additional ATP gained from mitochondrial cristae: ΔATP = 570 − 30 = 540 ATP.
(a) 570 ATP  ·  (b) 30 ATP  ·  (c) Net gain = 540 ATP
EXAMPLE 2

Calculating Central Sap Vacuole Volume Percentage in a Mature Plant Cell

A mature palisade mesophyll cell has a total internal volume of 200000 µm³. Its central sap vacuole occupies 160000 µm³ while the nucleus occupies 10000 µm³. Calculate the percentage of the cell's volume occupied by: (a) the central sap vacuole, and (b) the remaining cytoplasm and organelles.

  1. Part (a) — Percentage volume occupied by the central sap vacuole: %V_vacuole = (160000 ÷ 200000) × 100 = 0.80 × 100 = 80%.
  2. Part (b) Step 1 — Volume of remaining cytoplasm and organelles (excluding vacuole and nucleus): V_cyto = 200000 − 160000 − 10000 = 30000 µm³.
  3. Part (b) Step 2 — Percentage volume of remaining cytoplasm and organelles: %V_cyto = (30000 ÷ 200000) × 100 = 0.15 × 100 = 15%.
(a) Sap Vacuole = 80%  ·  (b) Remaining Cytoplasm & Organelles = 15%
EXAMPLE 3

Actual Chloroplast Diameter from Electron Photomicrograph

In a transmission electron micrograph of a palisade cell taken at ×12000 magnification, a chloroplast measures 60 mm in length. Calculate the actual length A of the chloroplast in micrometres (µm).

  1. Step 1 — Convert image length I from millimetres to micrometres: I = 60 × 1000 = 60000 µm.
  2. Step 2 — Divide image length I by magnification M to find actual chloroplast length A: A = I ÷ M = 60000 ÷ 12000 = 5.0 µm.
Actual length of chloroplast A = 5.0 µm

Exit ticket

In two sentences: why is it wrong to say “plant cells have chloroplasts instead of mitochondria”, and why are mitochondria and chloroplasts called semi-autonomous?

2 marks
Model answer unlocks after you try

Green plant cells possess both chloroplasts (to synthesise glucose using sunlight) and mitochondria (to respire glucose into ATP day and night) (1). Both organelles are called semi-autonomous because they contain their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes, enabling them to synthesise some of their own proteins and divide by binary fission (1).
