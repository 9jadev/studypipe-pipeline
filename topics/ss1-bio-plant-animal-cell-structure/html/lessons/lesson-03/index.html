Practical analysis questions

Answer these five questions, then compare with the model answers.

  1. What happens to SA : V as the side L doubles?
  2. Why does the centre of a large cube stay undyed?
  3. Why can Amoeba rely on diffusion?
  4. Why does Euglena puzzle classifiers?

Model answers unlocks after you try
  1. SA : V halves. In the table, 6.0 mm⁻¹ for the 1 mm cube becomes 3.0 mm⁻¹ for the 2 mm cube, because SA : V = 6 ÷ L.
  2. Dye reaches only a short distance in the time allowed. In a big cube the middle is far from every face, so the dye does not get there and a pale core remains.
  3. Amoeba is tiny, so it has a very large SA : V and every part of it is close to the surface. Diffusion across the membrane is fast enough for its needs.
  4. It has chloroplasts like a plant cell, and a gullet and flagellum like an animal-like protozoan. In light it feeds autotrophically; in darkness it absorbs food saprophytically.
  5. The cells of Volvox are copies of one another and each can feed, respire and move by itself. A tissue needs cells that share out the jobs and cannot live apart.

Colony or tissue?

The cells of Volvox are copies of one another: each can feed, respire and move by itself, so jobs are hardly shared. Spirogyra's filament works the same way. In Hydra the cells have taken on different jobs (catching food, digesting, covering) and could not survive apart. That sharing of jobs is what makes a tissue.

    4Apply · Worked examples

    Worked examples, step by step

    Try each problem on paper first, then check the working.

    EXAMPLE 1

    SA : V of a 2 mm cube

    A cube has sides of 2 mm. Find its surface area to volume ratio.

    1. Surface area: SA = 6 × 22 = 6 × 4 = 24 mm2.
    2. Volume: V = 23 = 8 mm3.
    3. Ratio: SA ÷ V = 24 ÷ 8 = 3.0 mm−1. Check with the shortcut: 6 ÷ 2 = 3.0 mm−1.
    3.0 mm−1
    EXAMPLE 2

    Comparing a 1 mm cube with a 6 mm cube

    Compare the SA : V of cubes with sides of 1 mm and 6 mm.

    1. Small cube: 6 ÷ 1 = 6.0 mm−1.
    2. Large cube: 6 ÷ 6 = 1.0 mm−1.
    3. Compare: 6.0 ÷ 1.0 = 6. The small cube has six times as much surface for each mm³.
    6.0 ÷ 1.0 = 6
    EXAMPLE 3

    Amoeba against a small frog

    Model an Amoeba as a 0.1 mm cube and a small frog's body as a 30 mm cube. How many times larger is the Amoeba's SA : V?

    1. Amoeba: 6 ÷ 0.1 = 60 mm−1.
    2. Frog: 6 ÷ 30 = 0.2 mm−1.
    3. Compare: 60 ÷ 0.2 = 300. Diffusion alone serves the Amoeba; the frog needs blood and a heart.
    60 ÷ 0.2 = 300
    4Apply · The big idea

    Back to the lab: why does Dr Amaka's patient need blood?

    Scale a cube up from an Amoeba to a small frog and watch SA : V and the distance to the centre.

    The big idea of Lesson 3: large many-celled organisms divide labour among specialised cells and must move materials around, because SA ÷ V = 6 ÷ L falls as the body grows.
