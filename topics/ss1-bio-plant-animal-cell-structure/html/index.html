The Unit Mystery · Mile 12 Market & LUTH

The Wilting Ugu and the Saline Drip

At Mile 12 Market, Mama Ngozi keeps her ugu leaves crisp with a sprinkle of clean water. One hot afternoon her nephew washes a basket in a bowl of salty water “to keep flies off”. Within an hour, the leaves hang limp.

The same evening at LUTH, Dr Amaka refuses to drip plain water into a dehydrated patient’s vein. She asks for 0.9% saline instead. Chinedu, the SS1 student who helps at the stall, wants to know what links a limp leaf to a hospital drip. Coach Danjuma is sure: “The salt soaks into the leaves and makes them heavy. And water can’t hurt anybody.”

Is Coach Danjuma right? Why would salty water wilt a leaf, why could plain water harm a patient who needs water, and why must the saline be exactly 0.9%?

  • Clue 1The leaf wilts, but it does not burst.
  • Clue 2The patient needs water, yet plain water is refused.
  • Clue 3The saline is exactly 0.9%, no more and no less.
  • Clue 4Under a microscope, both the leaf and the blood are made of cells.

The image grows; the cell does not

Turning to a stronger objective makes the image bigger, but the onion cell itself stays 50 µm wide. That is why actual size = image size ÷ magnification always gives the same answer.

The 6 calculation archetypes

Almost every number question on cells in WAEC, NECO and JAMB is one of these six. Each step has a note under it.

Type 1 · Lesson 1

Total magnification

  1. M = M(eyepiece) × M(objective)The two lenses work one after the other, so their powers multiply. Never add them.
  2. M = 10 × 40 = ×400A ×10 eyepiece with the ×40 objective gives ×400.
Type 2 · Lesson 1

Cell width from the field of view

  1. width = field diameter ÷ cells acrossCount how many cells sit side by side across the circle of light.
  2. width = 0.40 mm ÷ 8 = 0.050 mm8 onion cells fill a 0.40 mm field at ×400.
  3. 0.050 mm × 1000 = 50 µmMultiply by 1000 to change millimetres into micrometres.
Type 3 · Lesson 1

Actual size from an image

  1. actual = image ÷ MDivide the size in the picture by how many times it was magnified.
  2. actual = 24 mm ÷ 400 = 0.060 mmA cheek cell 24 mm long in a ×400 photograph.
  3. 0.060 mm × 1000 = 60 µmCells are usually quoted in micrometres.
Type 4 · Lessons 1 & 5

Drawing magnification

  1. M = drawing length ÷ actual lengthPut both lengths in the same unit before you divide.
  2. M = 50 mm ÷ 0.25 mm = ×200The units cancel, so a magnification has no unit: write ×200.
Type 5 · Lesson 3

Surface area : volume of a cube

  1. SA ÷ V = 6L² ÷ L³ = 6/LA cube has 6 square faces of area L²; its volume is L³.
  2. 6/L = 6 ÷ 2 mm = 3.0 mm⁻¹Double the side and the ratio halves: big bodies have little surface for their size.
Type 6 · Lesson 4

Percentage change in mass

  1. % change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100Always divide by the starting mass, because strips start at different masses.
  2. (2.85 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = +14.0%A gain means water entered the potato by osmosis (pure water, hypotonic).

05 / How Every Lesson Works

See it first, then explain it.

After a short Do Now warm-up, every lesson follows the same five-step inquiry loop, with a movement break at 45 minutes, so you test an idea before you memorise it.

  1. 1 · Wonder

    Watch the 3D story and pin your prediction

    Mama Ngozi, Chinedu, Coach Danjuma and Dr Amaka meet a real puzzle. Post your guess on the Wonder Wall.

  2. 2 · Investigate

    Work the 3D practical bench

    Change objectives, tap organelles, cut cubes or weigh potato strips yourself and record the results.

  3. 3 · Make sense

    Zoom inside the cell

    See membranes, organelles and water molecules up close, then build the rules and tables from what you saw.

  4. 4 · Apply

    Worked examples, the Bridge and a quiz

    Three step-by-step calculations, a case-solving challenge and 12 exam-style questions every lesson.

  5. 5 · Reflect

    Rate yourself and unlock solutions

    Rate each goal 1–4, do the blank-page recall, then try the 30-mark paper before opening the model answers.
