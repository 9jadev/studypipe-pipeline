The Unit Mystery · Mile 12 Market & LUTH Idi-Araba

Why does pure water rescue a wilted Nigerian ugu leaf, yet shatter every human red blood cell on a slide?

At Mile 12 Market in Lagos, Mama Nkechi sprinkles cool borehole water over wilted fluted pumpkin (Telfairia occidentalis / ugu) leaves that have been sitting in the hot afternoon sun. Within thirty minutes, every leaf blade stands crisp, green, and firm again — and not a single plant cell bursts.

Yet across town at the LUTH Diagnostic Lab in Idi-Araba, a student intern accidentally dilutes a patient’s blood smear with pure distilled water instead of 0.9% saline. Under the ×400 microscope, Zainab and Kelechi watch in shock as every red blood cell swells into a sphere and bursts open (haemolysis). And when Kelechi later draws an iodine-stained onion bulb epidermis for his WAEC Paper 3 mock exam and labels “chloroplasts” and “mitochondria”, Chief Examiner Dr Halima deducts marks immediately.

What structural armour protects a plant cell in pure water? How do double-membrane, single-membrane, and non-membranous organelles divide cellular work? Why can microscopic Amoeba breathe by simple diffusion while humans need lungs and a heart? And why does an underground onion bulb have zero chloroplasts?

Key Exam Rule
Convert I and A to the SAME unit first (1 cm = 10 mm = 10,000 μm)

For any specimen of fixed actual length A, the apparent image length I grows linearly with magnification M (I = M × A). Always write magnification with a multiplication sign (e.g. ×400) and never attach length units (cm or mm) to M.

The 6 calculation types

Every numerical problem in WAEC, NECO, and JAMB Cell Biology follows one of these six patterns:

Type 1 · Lesson 1 & 5

Drawing Magnification ($M = I \div A$)

Convert drawing size $I$ and actual specimen size $A$ to the same unit, then divide: M = I ÷ A = 9.0 cm ÷ 1.5 cm = ×6.

Type 2 · Lesson 1 & 5

Actual Cell Size in Micrometres ($\mu\text{m}$)

Convert image size in $\text{mm}$ to $\mu\text{m}$ ($\times 1000$) and divide by $M$: A = I ÷ M = 24000 μm ÷ 400 = 60 μm.

Type 3 · Lesson 1 & 2

Compound Microscope & ATP Scaling

Multiply lens powers Mtotal = Me × Mo = 10 × 40 = ×400; scale cristae surface area and ATP output per second.

Type 4 · Lesson 3

Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio ($\text{SA/V}$)

For a cube of side $L$, $\text{SA} = 6L^2$ and $V = L^3$, so SA/V = 6 ÷ L = 6 ÷ 0.1 mm = 60 mm⁻¹.

Type 5 · Lesson 4

Tissue Osmometry Percentage Mass Change

Use initial mass $m_i$ and final mass $m_f$: %Δm = ((mf − mi) ÷ mi) × 100 = ((4.60 − 4.00) ÷ 4.00) × 100 = +15.0%.

Type 6 · Lesson 4

Plant Cell Water Potential ($\Psi_w$)

Combine negative solute potential $\Psi_s$ and positive turgor pressure $\Psi_p$: Ψw = Ψs + Ψp = −650 + 450 = −200 kPa.
