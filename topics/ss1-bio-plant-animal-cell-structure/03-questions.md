## Lesson 01 · Discovery of the Cell, Cell Theory & Prokaryotes vs Eukaryotes

### Part 1 · 12 Interactive MCQs (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** When Robert Hooke examined thin slices of oak cork under his compound microscope in 1665, what were the honey-comb chambers he named *"cells"* actually composed of?
   - **A.** Living protoplasm containing nuclei and active chloroplasts
   - **B.** Empty non-living cellulose cell walls left behind after the plant cells died ✓
   - **C.** Selectively permeable lipoprotein membranes of living phloem tissue
   - **D.** Dividing bacterial colonies trapped inside tree bark
   - *Explanation:* Cork is dead outer bark tissue. Hooke observed only the rigid, empty **cellulose cell walls** surrounding spaces where living cells once existed. The living substance inside cells was discovered later by Félix Dujardin (1835).

2. **[WAEC-style · Remember]** Which scientist studied the living, gelatinous substance inside active cells in 1835 and named it *"sarcode"* (later called **protoplasm**)?
   - **A.** Matthias Schleiden
   - **B.** Theodor Schwann
   - **C.** Félix Dujardin ✓
   - **D.** Rudolf Virchow
   - *Explanation:* **Félix Dujardin (1835)** described the living jelly inside protozoan cells as *sarcode*, demonstrating that cells are not hollow empty chambers. Purkinje (1839) and von Mohl (1846) later introduced the term *protoplasm*.

3. **[JAMB-style · Understand]** Which pair of nineteenth-century German scientists jointly established that both plant tissues and animal tissues are composed of cells, laying the foundation of the classical Cell Theory?
   - **A.** Robert Hooke and Antonie van Leeuwenhoek
   - **B.** Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann ✓
   - **C.** Félix Dujardin and Louis Pasteur
   - **D.** Rudolf Virchow and Robert Brown
   - *Explanation:* Botanist **Matthias Schleiden (1838)** stated that all plants are aggregates of cells, and zoologist **Theodor Schwann (1839)** extended this principle to animals, uniting botany and zoology under the Cell Theory.

4. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** Rudolf Virchow's 1855 dictum *"Omnis cellula e cellula"* directly refuted the theory of spontaneous generation by stating that:
   - **A.** all cells contain a double-membrane nucleus
   - **B.** every new cell arises only by the division of a pre-existing living cell ✓
   - **C.** plant cells are structurally identical to bacterial cells
   - **D.** non-living organic broth can assemble into prokaryotic cells in warm air
   - *Explanation:* Virchow (1855) completed the modern Cell Theory by showing that cells never form spontaneously from non-living matter; **all cells come from pre-existing cells** via cell division.

5. **[JAMB-style · Analyse]** Why are **viruses** regarded as an exception at the boundary between living and non-living matter rather than true cells under the Cell Theory?
   - **A.** They possess 70S ribosomes and a cellulose cell wall but lack mitochondria
   - **B.** They lack a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and ribosomes, existing as inert nucleoprotein crystals outside a living host cell ✓
   - **C.** They carry out aerobic respiration independently in soil water
   - **D.** They contain both DNA and RNA enclosed inside a double nuclear envelope
   - *Explanation:* A virus consists only of a nucleic acid core (either DNA or RNA, never both) enclosed in a protein coat (**capsid**). It has no protoplasm, no cell membrane, and no metabolic machinery of its own, so it can only replicate inside a living host cell.

6. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** Which of the following structures is present in a **prokaryotic cell** (such as *Escherichia coli* or a blue-green alga) as well as in a **eukaryotic plant cell**?
   - **A.** Nuclear envelope with nuclear pores
   - **B.** Mitochondrion with folded cristae
   - **C.** Ribosomes for protein synthesis ✓
   - **D.** Chloroplast with stacked thylakoid grana
   - *Explanation:* Both prokaryotes and eukaryotes synthesise proteins using **ribosomes** (70S ribosomes in prokaryotes; 80S cytoplasmic ribosomes in eukaryotes). Prokaryotes have **no nuclear envelope** and **no membrane-bound organelles** (no mitochondria, chloroplasts, ER, or Golgi bodies).

7. **[NECO-style · Analyse]** How does the genetic material of a bacterium differ structurally from the genetic material inside the nucleus of a human cheek cell?
   - **A.** Bacterial DNA is a single circular molecule lying naked in the cytoplasm (nucleoid), whereas human nuclear DNA consists of linear chromosomes bound to histone proteins inside a double membrane ✓
   - **B.** Bacterial DNA is linear and enclosed in a single-membrane tonoplast, whereas human DNA is circular
   - **C.** Bacteria use RNA only as their hereditary material, whereas humans use DNA
   - **D.** Bacterial chromosomes are attached to centrioles during mitosis, whereas human chromosomes are not
   - *Explanation:* Prokaryotic cells lack a nuclear membrane; their main chromosome is a **closed circular loop of naked DNA** located in the cytoplasmic **nucleoid** region (often with small circular **plasmids**). Eukaryotic cells package **linear DNA** with histone proteins inside a **double-membrane nucleus**.

8. **[WAEC Practical · Apply]** A student views a stained *Spirogyra* cell using a compound microscope fitted with a $\times 15$ eyepiece lens and a $\times 40$ objective lens. What is the total magnification ($M_{\text{total}}$) of the image?
   - **A.** $\times 55$
   - **B.** $\times 300$
   - **C.** $\times 600$ ✓
   - **D.** $\times 1500$
   - *Explanation:* Total microscope magnification is the product of the eyepiece magnification and the objective magnification: `M_total = M_e × M_o = 15 × 40 = 600` ($\times 600$).

9. **[WAEC Practical · Apply]** In a WAEC Paper 3 practical, a candidate makes a biological drawing of a leaf specimen. The actual length of the leaf is $A = 3.5\text{ cm}$ and the length of the candidate's drawing is $I = 14.0\text{ cm}$. What is the magnification of the drawing?
   - **A.** $\times 0.25$
   - **B.** $\times 4$ ✓
   - **C.** $\times 10.5$
   - **D.** $\times 49$
   - *Explanation:* Drawing magnification equals drawing length divided by actual specimen length in the same unit: `M = I / A = 14.0 / 3.5 = 4` ($\times 4$). Remember that magnification has a `×` sign and **no unit**.

10. **[JAMB-style · Apply]** Under a compound microscope at $\times 400$ magnification, the image of a single cheek epithelial cell measures $I = 20\text{ mm}$ across. What is the actual diameter ($A$) of the cheek cell in micrometres ($\mu\text{m}$)?
    - **A.** $5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$
    - **B.** $20\text{ }\mu\text{m}$
    - **C.** $50\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ ✓
    - **D.** $80\text{ }\mu\text{m}$
    - *Explanation:* First convert image size $I$ from millimetres to micrometres ($1\text{ mm} = 1000\text{ }\mu\text{m}$): `I = 20 × 1000 = 20000 μm`. Then divide by magnification $M = 400$: `A = I / M = 20000 / 400 = 50 μm`.

11. **[WAEC-style · Apply]** An electron micrograph shows a mitochondrion magnified $\times 25\,000$. If the actual length of the mitochondrion is $A = 2.4\text{ }\mu\text{m}$, what is the length ($I$) of the mitochondrion in the photograph in millimetres ($\text{mm}$)?
    - **A.** $6.0\text{ mm}$
    - **B.** $24.0\text{ mm}$
    - **C.** $60.0\text{ mm}$ ✓
    - **D.** $104.0\text{ mm}$
    - *Explanation:* First find image length in micrometres: `I = M × A = 25000 × 2.4 = 60000 μm`. Convert micrometres to millimetres by dividing by $1000$: `I_mm = 60000 / 1000 = 60.0 mm`.

12. **[JAMB-style · Analyse]** Which feature is shared by a **multinucleate striated muscle fibre**, a **coenocytic *Rhizopus* hypha**, and a **mature mammalian red blood cell**?
    - **A.** They are all prokaryotic organisms lacking membrane-bound organelles
    - **B.** Each represents a specialised structural exception to the simple textbook model of one nucleus per discrete cell ✓
    - **C.** They all possess a rigid cellulose cell wall for osmotic support
    - **D.** They all photosynthesise using cup-shaped chloroplasts
    - *Explanation:* Striated muscle fibres and non-septate (*coenocytic*) fungal hyphae contain many nuclei within one continuous cytoplasm without cross-walls, while mature mammalian erythrocytes lose their nucleus entirely to maximise haemoglobin space — making all three classic exceptions/modifications to the "one cell, one nucleus" rule.

## Lesson 02 · Inside the Eukaryotic Cell — Organelles & Plant vs Animal Cells

### Part 1 · 12 Interactive MCQs (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **[WAEC-style · Remember]** Which of the following cell organelles is bounded by a **double membrane** and has its inner membrane folded into shelf-like projections called **cristae**?
   - **A.** Golgi apparatus
   - **B.** Lysosome
   - **C.** Mitochondrion ✓
   - **D.** Endoplasmic reticulum
   - *Explanation:* The **mitochondrion** has a smooth outer membrane and an inner membrane folded into **cristae** surrounding a fluid **matrix**. The cristae provide a large surface area for the enzymes of aerobic cellular respiration (ATP synthesis).

2. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** All of the following cellular structures possess a biological lipoprotein membrane **EXCEPT**:
   - **A.** vacuole (tonoplast)
   - **B.** chloroplast
   - **C.** ribosome ✓
   - **D.** lysosome
   - *Explanation:* **Ribosomes** (and centrioles) are **non-membranous organelles** — each ribosome is a compact granule of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and protein with zero surrounding membrane. Vacuoles and lysosomes have a **single membrane**, while chloroplasts, mitochondria, and the nucleus have a **double membrane**. *(Note: the cellulose cell wall is also non-membranous).*

3. **[JAMB-style · Understand]** A green palisade mesophyll cell in a sunlit *ugu* leaf is actively carrying out both **photosynthesis** and **aerobic respiration** simultaneously. Which pair of organelles is directly responsible for these two energy processes?
   - **A.** Chloroplasts and mitochondria ✓
   - **B.** Ribosomes and Golgi bodies
   - **C.** Centrioles and lysosomes
   - **D.** Smooth endoplasmic reticulum and nucleolus
   - *Explanation:* **Chloroplasts** trap light energy in their chlorophyll-rich **grana** to synthesise glucose (photosynthesis), while **mitochondria** break down glucose in aerobic respiration to generate **ATP** for cellular metabolism. Green plant cells possess **both** organelles!

4. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** Which organelle is correctly matched with its primary physiological role in a eukaryotic cell?
   - **A.** **Golgi apparatus** — modifies, sorts, packages, and secretes proteins and glycoproteins in vesicles ✓
   - **B.** **Rough endoplasmic reticulum** — digests worn-out organelles using hydrolytic enzymes
   - **C.** **Lysosome** — synthesises lipids and steroid hormones
   - **D.** **Centrosome** — maintains turgor pressure in flowering plant cells
   - *Explanation:* The **Golgi body** (stack of flattened **cisternae**) receives proteins from the Rough ER, chemically modifies and packages them into secretory vesicles or **lysosomes**, and supplies cell-wall polysaccharides in dividing plant cells.

5. **[NECO-style · Understand]** Why are **lysosomes** frequently referred to as the *"suicide bags"* of animal cells?
   - **A.** They pump excess water out of freshwater protozoa until the cell collapses
   - **B.** They contain powerful hydrolytic (digestive) enzymes that can rupture and self-digest a damaged or dying cell (**autolysis**) ✓
   - **C.** They oxidise glucose completely to carbon dioxide and water
   - **D.** They pull chromosomes apart during anaphase of mitosis
   - *Explanation:* **Lysosomes** are single-membrane vesicles filled with acid hydrolase enzymes. In addition to digesting engulfed bacteria or food vacuoles, they break down worn-out organelles (**autophagy**) or rupture to digest dead/damaged cells (**autolysis**).

6. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** Which set of three structures is present in a typical green **plant cell** (such as a palisade cell) but **absent** in a typical **animal cell** (such as a human liver cell)?
   - **A.** Plasma membrane, mitochondria, and ribosomes
   - **B.** Cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large permanent central sap vacuole bounded by a tonoplast ✓
   - **C.** Centrioles, glycogen granules, and abundant lysosomes
   - **D.** Nucleus, rough endoplasmic reticulum, and Golgi bodies
   - *Explanation:* A mature green plant cell is distinguished from an animal cell by having a **rigid cellulose cell wall**, **chloroplasts (plastids)**, and a **large central permanent vacuole** bounded by a **tonoplast** (which pushes the nucleus to the side).

7. **[JAMB-style · Analyse]** Animal cells differ from the cells of higher flowering plants by possessing which organelle near the nucleus for spindle fibre organisation during cell division, and by storing excess carbohydrate in what chemical form?
   - **A.** Centrioles (centrosome); stored carbohydrate is **glycogen** ✓
   - **B.** Leucoplasts; stored carbohydrate is **starch**
   - **C.** Pyrenoids; stored carbohydrate is **cellulose**
   - **D.** Dictyosomes; stored carbohydrate is **chitin**
   - *Explanation:* Animal cells possess a **centrosome containing a pair of cylindrical centrioles** (absent in flowering plants) and store carbohydrate as **glycogen granules** (just as fungi do), whereas plants store carbohydrate as **starch grains** inside plastids.

8. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** Which row in the table below correctly contrasts the **plant cell wall** with the **plasma (cell) membrane**?
   - **A.** Cell wall: Lipoprotein, selectively permeable, living | Cell membrane: Cellulose, freely permeable, non-living
   - **B.** Cell wall: Cellulose, freely (fully) permeable, rigid and non-living | Cell membrane: Lipoprotein, selectively (differentially) permeable, flexible and living ✓
   - **C.** Cell wall: Found in all cells | Cell membrane: Found in plant cells only
   - **D.** Cell wall: Controls active transport of ions | Cell membrane: Prevents osmotic bursting in distilled water
   - *Explanation:* The plant **cell wall** is composed of **cellulose**, is **non-living**, **rigid**, and **fully/freely permeable** to water and dissolved solutes. The **cell membrane** is composed of **lipoprotein** (phospholipid bilayer + proteins), is **living**, **flexible**, and **selectively permeable**.

9. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** Which type of **plastid** is colourless and specialised for storing starch, oils, or proteins in underground plant organs such as yam tubers, cassava roots, and onion bulbs?
   - **A.** Chloroplast
   - **B.** Chromoplast
   - **C.** Leucoplast ✓
   - **D.** Tonoplast
   - *Explanation:* **Leucoplasts** are colourless plastids found in non-photosynthetic underground storage tissues (e.g., *amyloplasts* storing starch in yam/potato tubers). **Chloroplasts** are green (photosynthesis), **chromoplasts** are yellow/orange/red (flower petals and ripe fruits), and the **tonoplast** is the vacuolar membrane (not a plastid!).

10. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** Which of the following human cell types contains the **fewest (or zero) mitochondria**?
    - **A.** Cardiac (heart) muscle cells
    - **B.** Proximal convoluted tubule cells of the kidney
    - **C.** Dead keratinised cells of the outer cornified layer of the skin (and mature mammalian red blood cells) ✓
    - **D.** Spermatozoa middle-piece and active liver hepatocytes
    - *Explanation:* Cells of the **cornified layer (stratum corneum)** of the skin are dead, flattened, keratin-filled cells with no metabolic demand, and mature mammalian **red blood cells** extrude their mitochondria and nucleus so they do not consume the oxygen they transport. By contrast, heart muscle, kidney tubule, liver, and sperm cells are packed with mitochondria.

11. **[JAMB-style · Apply]** The outer membrane of a mitochondrion has a surface area of $15\text{ }\mu\text{m}^2$, while its folded inner cristae membrane has a surface area $5.2$ times larger. If each $1\text{ }\mu\text{m}^2$ of cristae membrane generates $2000\text{ ATP molecules per second}$, how many ATP molecules does this mitochondrion produce per second?
    - **A.** $30\,000\text{ ATP s}^{-1}$
    - **B.** $78\,000\text{ ATP s}^{-1}$
    - **C.** $156\,000\text{ ATP s}^{-1}$ ✓
    - **D.** $312\,000\text{ ATP s}^{-1}$
    - *Explanation:* First find the inner cristae surface area: `A_inner = 5.2 × 15 = 78 μm²`. Then multiply by the ATP output per $\mu\text{m}^2$: `N_ATP = 2000 × 78 = 156000 ATP/s`.

12. **[WAEC-style · Apply]** A mature plant cell has a total volume of $V_{\text{cell}} = 150\,000\text{ }\mu\text{m}^3$. Its central sap vacuole occupies $84\%$ of the total cell volume. What is the volume ($V_{\text{proto}}$) occupied by the remaining peripheral cytoplasm and nucleus?
    - **A.** $16\,000\text{ }\mu\text{m}^3$
    - **B.** $24\,000\text{ }\mu\text{m}^3$ ✓
    - **C.** $84\,000\text{ }\mu\text{m}^3$
    - **D.** $126\,000\text{ }\mu\text{m}^3$
    - *Explanation:* First find the vacuole volume: `V_vac = 0.84 × 150000 = 126000 μm³`. Subtract from total cell volume to get the peripheral protoplasm volume: `V_proto = V_cell - V_vac = 150000 - 126000 = 24000 μm³`.

## Lesson 03 · Forms of Living Cells & Levels of Biological Organisation

### Part 1 · 12 Interactive MCQs (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** Why is *Euglena viridis* frequently described in WAEC and JAMB examinations as an organism that shares both **plant** and **animal** characteristics?
   - **A.** It has a cellulose cell wall like a plant and moves by pseudopodia like an *Amoeba*
   - **B.** It possesses chloroplasts for autotrophic photosynthesis in light (plant feature), but has a flexible pellicle, flagellum, gullet, eyespot, and saprophytic nutrition in darkness (animal features) ✓
   - **C.** It forms multicellular tissues in sunlight and separates into single cells at night
   - **D.** It stores glycogen in its chloroplasts and starch in its centrioles
   - *Explanation:* *Euglena* is a **mixotrophic** flagellate protist: in sunlight it photosynthesises using **chloroplasts** (plant-like), while it lacks a cellulose cell wall (bounded instead by a flexible protein **pellicle**), swims with a **flagellum**, detects light with an **eyespot (stigma)**, osmoregulates with a **contractile vacuole**, and absorbs dissolved organic matter (**saprophytic/holozoic**) in darkness.

2. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** Which organelle enables freshwater unicellular organisms such as *Amoeba* and *Paramecium* to continuously expel excess water that enters their cytoplasm by osmosis, preventing them from bursting?
   - **A.** Food vacuole
   - **B.** Contractile vacuole ✓
   - **C.** Macronucleus
   - **D.** Pyrenoid
   - *Explanation:* Because the cytoplasm of freshwater protozoa is hypertonic to surrounding pond water, water constantly enters by osmosis. The **contractile vacuole** collects this excess water and rhythmically contracts to expel it across the cell membrane (**osmoregulation**).

3. **[JAMB-style · Analyse]** Which row correctly matches each organism with the **form in which its cells exist** and the shape of its **chloroplast** (where present)?
   - **A.** *Chlamydomonas*: Single free-living cell with a cup-shaped chloroplast | *Spirogyra*: Unbranched filament with a spiral ribbon-shaped chloroplast | *Volvox*: Hollow spherical colony ✓
   - **B.** *Chlamydomonas*: Colony with spiral chloroplast | *Spirogyra*: Single cell with cup-shaped chloroplast | *Volvox*: Filament
   - **C.** *Chlamydomonas*: Tissue level | *Spirogyra*: Organ level | *Volvox*: System level
   - **D.** *Chlamydomonas*: Holozoic | *Spirogyra*: Parasitic | *Volvox*: Saprophytic
   - *Explanation:* *Chlamydomonas* is a **unicellular** green alga with a **cup-shaped chloroplast** and pyrenoid; *Spirogyra* is an **unbranched filament** of cylindrical cells with **spiral ribbon-shaped chloroplasts**; *Volvox* is a **colonial** hollow sphere of biflagellate cells joined by cytoplasmic strands.

4. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** Why is a *Volvox* colony or a *Spirogyra* filament **NOT** classified as a biological **tissue**, whereas the body wall of *Hydra* is classified at the **tissue level** of organisation?
   - **A.** *Volvox* and *Spirogyra* contain fewer than ten cells, whereas *Hydra* contains millions
   - **B.** In a colony or filament the cells are structurally alike and carry out life processes independently with little division of labour, whereas in a tissue the cells are specialised and interdependent for a specific function ✓
   - **C.** *Hydra* cells have cellulose cell walls whereas *Spirogyra* cells do not
   - **D.** *Volvox* and *Spirogyra* are prokaryotic, whereas *Hydra* is eukaryotic
   - *Explanation:* A **tissue** is a group of similar or cooperating **specialised, interdependent cells** performing a specific function (e.g., *Hydra*'s ectoderm and endoderm layers). In a **colony** (*Volvox*) or **filament** (*Spirogyra*), the cells remain unspecialised and physiologically independent.

5. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** Arrange the following biological structures in the correct ascending order of complexity, from the **simplest** to the **most complex**:
   *I. Alimentary canal · II. Mitochondrion · III. Smooth muscle cell · IV. Stomach · V. Smooth muscle sheet · VI. Rabbit*
   - **A.** $\text{II} \rightarrow \text{III} \rightarrow \text{V} \rightarrow \text{IV} \rightarrow \text{I} \rightarrow \text{VI}$ ✓
   - **B.** $\text{III} \rightarrow \text{II} \rightarrow \text{V} \rightarrow \text{IV} \rightarrow \text{I} \rightarrow \text{VI}$
   - **C.** $\text{VI} \rightarrow \text{I} \rightarrow \text{IV} \rightarrow \text{V} \rightarrow \text{III} \rightarrow \text{II}$
   - **D.** $\text{II} \rightarrow \text{V} \rightarrow \text{III} \rightarrow \text{IV} \rightarrow \text{I} \rightarrow \text{VI}$
   - *Explanation:* Following the hierarchy $\text{Organelle} \rightarrow \text{Cell} \rightarrow \text{Tissue} \rightarrow \text{Organ} \rightarrow \text{System} \rightarrow \text{Organism}$:
     **II. Mitochondrion** (organelle) $\rightarrow$ **III. Smooth muscle cell** (cell) $\rightarrow$ **V. Smooth muscle sheet** (tissue) $\rightarrow$ **IV. Stomach** (organ) $\rightarrow$ **I. Alimentary canal** (organ system) $\rightarrow$ **VI. Rabbit** (multicellular organism).

6. **[JAMB-style · Analyse]** At what level of biological organisation are an **onion bulb**, a **ginger rhizome**, and a **hibiscus flower** classified?
   - **A.** Cellular level
   - **B.** Tissue level
   - **C.** Organ level ✓
   - **D.** System level
   - *Explanation:* An **onion bulb**, **ginger rhizome**, **leaf**, **root**, **stem**, **flower**, and **heart** are all **organs** because each is composed of several different tissues (epidermal tissue, parenchyma storage/packing tissue, xylem and phloem vascular tissues) working together as a structural unit.

7. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** Why can a microscopic *Paramecium* or *Amoeba* carry out gaseous exchange and excretion purely by simple diffusion across its cell surface without needing gills, lungs, or a circulatory system?
   - **A.** It has a very large surface-area-to-volume ratio ($\frac{SA}{V}$) and a very short diffusion distance from the outside medium to all parts of its cytoplasm ✓
   - **B.** It does not respire aerobically and produces zero metabolic waste
   - **C.** Its cellulose cell wall actively pumps oxygen molecules into the nucleus
   - **D.** Its surface-area-to-volume ratio is much smaller than that of a mammal
   - *Explanation:* As an organism's linear dimension $L$ is very small, its **surface-area-to-volume ratio ($\frac{SA}{V} = \frac{6}{L}$)** is huge, and every organelle lies within micrometres of the plasma membrane. Simple diffusion is therefore rapid enough to meet all respiratory and excretory demands.

8. **[WAEC-style · Apply]** Compare the surface-area-to-volume ratio ($\frac{SA}{V} = \frac{6}{L}$) of a unicellular organism modelled as a cube of side $L_1 = 0.05\text{ mm}$ with that of a multicellular tissue block modelled as a cube of side $L_2 = 5.0\text{ mm}$. How many times larger is the unicellular organism's $\frac{SA}{V}$ ratio?

## Lesson 04 · The Cell & Its Environment — Diffusion, Osmosis, Turgor & Haemolysis

### Part 1 · 12 Interactive MCQs (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** Which statement accurately defines **osmosis** in living systems?
   - **A.** Movement of solute ions from a dilute solution to a concentrated solution using ATP energy
   - **B.** Net movement of water molecules from a dilute solution (higher water potential) to a more concentrated solution (lower water potential) across a selectively permeable membrane ✓
   - **C.** Random movement of gas molecules across a fully permeable cellulose cell wall
   - **D.** Bulk engulfing of solid food particles by pseudopodia
   - *Explanation:* **Osmosis** is a special case of passive diffusion involving **water molecules only** moving down a water-potential gradient (from a **hypotonic / dilute** solution to a **hypertonic / concentrated** solution) through a **selectively permeable membrane** without requiring metabolic energy (ATP).

2. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** Why does a mammalian **red blood cell** swell and burst (**haemolysis**) when placed in distilled water, whereas a **plant epidermal cell** placed in the same distilled water becomes firm (**turgid**) and never bursts?
   - **A.** Water does not enter plant cells by osmosis
   - **B.** The plant cell is surrounded by a rigid, inelastic cellulose cell wall that exerts opposing turgor pressure to prevent bursting, whereas the red blood cell has only a flexible cell membrane and no cell wall ✓
   - **C.** Red blood cells actively pump water inward using mitochondria
   - **D.** The plant cell membrane is completely impermeable to water molecules
   - *Explanation:* Distilled water is strongly **hypotonic** to both cells, so water enters both by osmosis. The red blood cell lacks a cell wall, so its delicate plasma membrane stretches and ruptures (**haemolysis**). The plant cell has a tough **cellulose cell wall** that resists expansion and builds up inward **turgor pressure ($\Psi_p$)** until water potential equilibrium ($\Psi_w = 0$) is reached (**turgidity**).

3. **[WAEC-style · Understand]** When a living *Spirogyra* filament or onion epidermal strip is mounted in a concentrated ($1.0\text{ M}$) salt or sugar solution, the vacuole shrinks and the protoplasm pulls away from the cell wall. What is this phenomenon called?
   - **A.** Haemolysis
   - **B.** Turgidity
   - **C.** Plasmolysis ✓
   - **D.** Crenation
   - *Explanation:* In a **hypertonic** solution, water leaves the plant cell's central sap vacuole by osmosis. The vacuole shrinks and the cytoplasm + plasma membrane detach and pull inward away from the rigid cellulose cell wall — a process called **plasmolysis**.

4. **[JAMB-style · Analyse]** In a fully **plasmolysed** plant cell immersed in concentrated sucrose solution, what fills the space between the rigid cellulose cell wall and the shrunken protoplast?
   - **A.** A complete vacuum
   - **B.** Pure distilled water
   - **C.** The external concentrated sucrose solution, because the cellulose cell wall is freely (fully) permeable ✓
   - **D.** Air bubbles released by the mitochondria
   - *Explanation:* Because the **cellulose cell wall is fully permeable** to both water and dissolved solutes, the external hypertonic sucrose solution passes freely through the cell wall to fill the gap left as the selectively permeable plasma membrane shrinks inward!

5. **[NECO-style · Understand]** What happens to a mammalian **red blood cell** when it is immersed in a strongly **hypertonic** ($5\%\text{ NaCl}$) salt solution?
   - **A.** It loses water by osmosis, shrinks, and develops a notched, wrinkled surface (**crenation**) ✓
   - **B.** It absorbs water by osmosis and bursts (**haemolysis**)
   - **C.** Its protoplasm pulls away from its cellulose wall (**plasmolysis**)
   - **D.** It becomes fully turgid
   - *Explanation:* In a hypertonic solution, an animal cell loses water by osmosis and shrivels with a scalloped/spiky membrane margin — called **crenation** (never call it plasmolysis, because animal cells have no cell wall to pull away from!).

6. **[WAEC-style · Analyse]** Root hair cells of a maize plant continue to absorb nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^-$) and potassium ($\text{K}^+$) ions from soil water even when the concentration of those ions is ten times higher inside the cell sap than in the soil.    - **A.** simple diffusion
   - **B.** osmosis
