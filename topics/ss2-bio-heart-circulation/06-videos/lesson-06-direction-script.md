## Chapter 3: Three kinds of cell

| # | Narration | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | Most cells are red cells, or erythrocytes. Each is a dimpled disc with no nucleus, and there are about five million in every cubic millimetre. | New board: "Red cells"<br>Kit blood: rbc=1, focus=rbc<br>📷 WIDE → rbc<br>Board: red cell: biconcave disc<br>Board: no nucleus; 5 million/mm^3 |

## Chapter 5: Example 1: counting red cells

| # | Narration | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 | Worked example one. A blood sample is diluted one in two hundred. In five haemocytometer squares, each point two by point two millimetres and point one deep, four hundred and eighty red cells are counted. Find the count in whole blood. | New board: "Example 1: cell count"<br>Kit panel off<br>📷 BOARD SHOT<br>Board: 480 cells, 5 squares<br>Board: 0.2 × 0.2 × 0.1 mm<br>Board: diluted 1 in 200 |
| 5.2 | Step one. The volume of one square is point two times point two times point one. That is point zero zero four cubic millimetres. | Board: 1 square = 0.004 mm^3 |
| 5.3 | Step two. Five squares hold five times that, so point zero two zero cubic millimetres. | New board: "Example 1: steps"<br>Board: 5 × 0.004 = 0.020 mm^3 |
| 5.4 | Step three. Divide the cells by the volume. Four hundred and eighty over point zero two zero is twenty-four thousand cells per cubic millimetre, in the diluted blood. | Board: 480 / 0.020<br>Board: = 24 000 mm^-3 |
| 5.5 | Step four. Undo the dilution. The blood was diluted two hundred times, so multiply by two hundred. Twenty-four thousand times two hundred is four point eight million. | Board: 24 000 × 200<br>Board: = 4.8 × 10^6 mm^-3<br>Box e9 |
| 5.6 | That is close to the normal five million, so the answer makes sense. The two classic slips are forgetting to multiply by the dilution, and dividing by one square instead of five. | New board: "Count: sense check"<br>Board: ≈ 5 million: normal<br>Board: keep the × 200<br>Board: divide by all 5 squares |
| 5.7 | And remember, cells touching the top and left lines are counted, but cells on the bottom and right lines are left out. Counting all four lines counts some twice, so the answer comes out too high. | Board: top, left: count<br>Board: bottom, right: skip<br>Board: all four: count too high |
