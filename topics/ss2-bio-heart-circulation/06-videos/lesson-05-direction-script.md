## Chapter 3: Why capillary blood is slow

| # | Narration | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | Blood is slowest in the capillaries, and that gives time for exchange. Why? Blood leaves the aorta at thirty-five centimetres per second. | 3.2 | All the capillaries together have a cross-section of about two thousand four hundred square centimetres. Find the speed in the capillaries. | Board: capillaries: A = 2400 cm^2<br>Board: find v |
| 3.3 | Step one. The flow is area times speed. Two point four times thirty-five is eighty-four centimetres cubed per second. | New board: "Flow = area x speed"<br>Board: Q = A × v<br>Board: Q = 2.4 × 35<br>Board: Q = 84 cm^3/s |

| 3.5 | Step two. The same flow runs through the capillaries. So speed equals flow over area, eighty-four over two thousand four hundred. | New board: "Speed in capillaries"<br>Board: v = Q / A<br>Board: v = 84 / 2400<br>Board: v = 0.035 cm/s<br>Box g3 |
