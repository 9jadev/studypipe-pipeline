The Unit Mystery · Surulere Stadium & LUTH Idi-Araba

How does a fist-sized pump triple its output in seconds, drive blood uphill from your ankles, and never mix a single drop of oxygen-poor and oxygen-rich blood?

During the Lagos State Inter-House Sports 400 m final at Surulere National Stadium, 16-year-old sprinter Tunde explodes off the blocks. Within twenty seconds, his heart rate climbs from 72 bpm to 160 bpm and his cardiac output more than triples from 5.0 L/min to over 17.6 L/min. On the echocardiogram at the LUTH Sports Medicine & Cardiac Clinic, his classmate Amaka and Consultant Cardiologist Dr Bamidele notice a striking asymmetry: Tunde’s left ventricular wall is 15 mm thick — three times thicker than his 5 mm right ventricular wall — even though both ventricles pump the exact same 70 mL stroke volume on every beat!

Even more puzzling, while pressure in Tunde’s aorta surges to 120/80 mmHg, pressure in his ankle veins drops below 10 mmHg — yet blood still climbs a full metre uphill against gravity back to his right atrium without pooling. Meanwhile, across the corridor at the LUTH Blood Bank, an emergency road-traffic patient with Blood Group B negative needs an urgent transfusion and a WAEC Paper 3 candidate loses marks for drawing the right and left ventricular walls equal in thickness.

Why can a cockroach survive with low-pressure open circulation while Tunde needs high-pressure double circulation? What makes the heart beat on its own without a brain nerve impulse? What produces the “Lub-Dub” sounds? How do capillaries form tissue fluid and lymph? And how do platelets, fibrin, and ABO antigens protect human life?

The 6 calculation types

Every numerical problem in WAEC, NECO, and JAMB Heart & Circulation follows one of these six patterns:

Type 1 · Lesson 1

Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio ($\text{SA/V} = 6 \div L$)

For a cube of side $L$, $\text{SA} = 6L^2$ and $V = L^3$, so SA/V = 6 ÷ L. Compare microscopic vs macroscopic organisms.

Type 2 · Lesson 1 & 3

Cardiac Output & Full Circulation Time

Multiply heart rate by stroke volume: CO = HR × SV = 72 × 70 = 5,040 mL/min = 5.04 L/min, and t = Vblood ÷ CO.

Type 3 · Lesson 2 & 3

Cardiac Cycle Duration & Ejection Fraction

Total beat time is T = 60 ÷ HR = 0.80 s ($0.1\text{ s} + 0.3\text{ s} + 0.4\text{ s}$); SV = EDV − ESV and EF% = (SV ÷ EDV) × 100%.

Type 4 · Lesson 3

Pulse Pressure & Mean Arterial Pressure ($\text{MAP}$)

From $120/80\text{ mmHg}$: PP = Psys − Pdia = 40 mmHg and MAP = Pdia + PP/3 = 93.3 mmHg.

Type 5 · Lesson 4

Starling Net Filtration Pressure ($\text{NFP}$)

Compare hydrostatic $P_c$ and oncotic $\pi_c$: arterial end NFP = 35 − 25 = +10 mmHg (out); venous end 15 − 25 = −10 mmHg (in).

Type 6 · Lesson 2 & 5

Haemoglobin $\text{O}_2$ Capacity & Magnification ($M = I \div A$)

Compute oxygen carriage (1.34 mL O2/g Hb), $\text{RBC}:\text{WBC}$ ratios, and Paper 3 drawing magnification M = I ÷ A.
