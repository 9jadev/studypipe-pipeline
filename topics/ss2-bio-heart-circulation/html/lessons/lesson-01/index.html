Need for Transport: Open vs Closed & Single vs Double Circulation

Why can a microscopic pond Amoeba breathe by simple diffusion, a cockroach survive with colourless haemolymph, and a human sprinter require a high-pressure four-chambered double pump?

  • 01Calculate the surface-area-to-volume ratio (SA/V = 6 ÷ L) of cubic organisms and explain why large multicellular animals cannot rely on simple diffusion alone.
  • 02List the materials transported in animals (oxygen, CO2, digested food, urea, hormones, antibodies, heat) and identify the media of transport (cytoplasm, haemolymph, blood, tissue fluid, lymph).
  • 03Contrast open circulation (arthropods: cockroach 13-chambered dorsal heart, ostia, haemocoel; tracheae carry O2) with closed circulation (earthworm, vertebrates).
  • 04Compare single circulation in bony fish (2 chambers: 1 atrium, 1 ventricle) with incomplete double circulation in amphibians/lizards (3 chambers) and complete double circulation in birds, mammals, and crocodiles (4 chambers).
  • 05Calculate cardiac output (CO = HR × SV) and full-body blood circulation time (t = Vblood ÷ CO).

3D SA/V Cube & Pump Bench: Verifying SA/V = 6 ÷ L

Test five organism cube sizes (L = 0.2, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0 mm) on Dr Bamidele’s 3D bench. Measure Surface Area SA = 6L² (mm²), Volume V = L³ (mm³), and SA/V = 6 ÷ L (mm⁻¹) to see why organisms larger than 1 mm require a vascular network and muscular heart.

Biophysical Diffusion Law (Fick’s Principle): Diffusion time increases with the square of distance ($t \propto x^2$). While oxygen diffuses across a 0.01 mm capillary wall in milliseconds, diffusing across a 10 mm unvascularised tissue block would take hours — causing central cells to suffocate without a mass-flow circulatory system.

Model answers unlocks after you try
  1. They are microscopic unicellular organisms with a very large SA/V ratio and a diffusion distance of less than 0.05 mm, so simple diffusion and cyclosis are fast enough.

1 · Materials Transported & Media of Transport in Animals

Every metabolising animal cell requires continuous delivery of raw materials and removal of toxic metabolic wastes across four transport media (cytoplasm, haemolymph, blood, and lymph/tissue fluid):

2 · Open vs Closed Circulatory Systems Compared

FeatureOpen Circulatory SystemClosed Circulatory System
Vessel ContinuityCirculating fluid leaves the dorsal vessel and empties into open body cavities (haemocoel / sinuses).Blood remains strictly confined inside a continuous network of arteries, capillaries, and veins.
Circulating FluidHaemolymph (no distinction between blood and interstitial fluid).Blood is distinct from tissue fluid and lymph.
Direct Contact with CellsHaemolymph bathes internal organs and tissues directly.Blood never touches tissue cells directly; exchange occurs across capillary walls via tissue fluid.
Hydrostatic Pressure & SpeedVery low pressure and slow, sluggish circulation.High pressure and rapid, precisely regulated circulation.
Control of DistributionCannot selectively redirect flow to specific active organs.Arterioles constrict or dilate (vasoconstriction/vasodilation) to shunt blood to active muscles or organs.

Worked examples, step by step

Every calculation is broken down one atomic mathematical step at a time with a plain-English explainer note underneath.

EXAMPLE 1

Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA/V) of Microscopic vs Macroscopic Cubes

Compare a unicellular organism modelled as a cube of side length L1 = 0.1 mm with a small multicellular animal modelled as a cube of side length L2 = 100 mm. Calculate: (a) the surface-area-to-volume ratio (SA/V) of the unicellular cube, (b) the SA/V ratio of the multicellular cube, and (c) how many times larger the unicellular SA/V ratio is than the multicellular SA/V ratio.

  1. Part (a) — Calculate SA/V of the 0.1 mm unicellular cube using SA/V = 6 ÷ L: R_1 = 6 ÷ 0.1 = 60 mm^-1.
  2. Part (b) — Calculate SA/V of the 100 mm multicellular cube: R_2 = 6 ÷ 100 = 0.06 mm^-1.
  3. Part (c) — Divide R1 by R2 to find the scaling factor F: F = 60 ÷ 0.06 = 1000.
(a) R1 = 60 mm⁻¹  ·  (b) R2 = 0.06 mm⁻¹  ·  (c) 1,000 times larger
EXAMPLE 2

Cardiac Output (CO) & Total Blood Circulation Time at Rest

At rest before his race, Tunde has a heart rate HR = 72 beats/min and a left ventricular stroke volume SV = 70 mL/beat. Given that his total blood volume is 5,040 mL, calculate: (a) his resting cardiac output in mL/min and L/min, and (b) the time in seconds required for his heart to pump his entire blood volume once.

  1. Part (a) Step 1 — Multiply heart rate HR by stroke volume SV: CO = 72 × 70 = 5040 mL/min.
  2. Part (a) Step 2 — Convert cardiac output from mL/min to L/min (÷ 1000): CO_L = 5040 ÷ 1000 = 5.04 L/min.
  3. Part (b) — Divide total blood volume by cardiac output to find circulation time t (min): t = 5040 ÷ 5040 = 1.0 min = 60 s.
(a) Resting Cardiac Output = 5,040 mL/min (5.04 L/min)  ·  (b) Full Circulation Time = 60 seconds
EXAMPLE 3

Sprint Exercise Cardiac Output & Percentage Increase

During his 400 m sprint at Surulere National Stadium, Tunde’s heart rate rises to HR = 150 beats/min and his stroke volume increases to SV = 110 mL/beat. Calculate: (a) his sprint cardiac output in L/min, and (b) the time in seconds now needed to circulate his 5,040 mL blood volume once.

  1. Part (a) Step 1 — Calculate sprint cardiac output in mL/min: CO_sprint = 150 × 110 = 16500 mL/min.
  2. Part (a) Step 2 — Convert mL/min to L/min (÷ 1000): CO_L = 16500 ÷ 1000 = 16.5 L/min.
  3. Part (b) — Calculate full circulation time in seconds: t_s = (5040 ÷ 16500) × 60 = 18.3 s.
(a) Sprint Cardiac Output = 16.5 L/min  ·  (b) Full Circulation Time = 18.3 seconds

The big idea of Lesson 1: As an organism’s size $L$ increases, its surface-area-to-volume ratio ($\text{SA/V} = 6 \div L$) plummets and diffusion distances grow too large for simple diffusion. While insects use low-pressure open circulation (where tracheae carry $\text{O}_2$) and fish use 2-chamber single circulation, birds, mammals, and crocodiles use 4-chamber complete double circulation to keep oxygenated and deoxygenated blood 100% separate at high systemic pressure!

Exit ticket

In two sentences: why does cockroach haemolymph play no role in oxygen transport, and why is systemic arterial blood pressure much higher in a human than in a tilapia fish?

2 marks
Model answer unlocks after you try

Cockroach haemolymph lacks the respiratory pigment haemoglobin, so oxygen is delivered directly to tissues through air-filled tracheae and tracheoles (1). In a tilapia fish (single circulation), blood loses pressure as it passes through narrow gill capillaries before reaching the body, whereas in a human (double circulation), oxygenated blood returns from the lungs to the thick left ventricle to be re-pumped at high pressure (~120 mmHg) (1).

Part B · Structured Written Questions (30 marks)

1. (a) Explain why a unicellular organism such as Amoeba does not require a blood circulatory system whereas a mammal does [3 marks]. (b) Calculate the surface-area-to-volume ratio (SA/V) of a cube of side 0.5 mm and a cube of side 25 mm, and state how many times larger the first ratio is than the second [4 marks]. (c) List six substances transported by mammalian blood [3 marks].

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Amoeba is microscopic with a very large surface-area-to-volume ratio (SA/V) and a tiny diffusion distance (< 0.05 mm) so simple diffusion suffices (1.5); a mammal is large and multicellular with a very small SA/V ratio and long diffusion distances, requiring a mass-flow circulatory system (1.5).
(b) Small cube: R_1 = 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12.0 mm^-1 (1.5). Large cube: R_2 = 6 ÷ 25 = 0.24 mm^-1 (1.5). Ratio: F = 12.0 ÷ 0.24 = 50 times larger (1).
(c) Oxygen, carbon dioxide, digested food (glucose/amino acids), urea, hormones, antibodies, water/mineral salts, heat (any 6 × ½ = 3 marks).

2. (a) Construct a table contrasting an open circulatory system with a closed circulatory system under five headings [5 marks]. (b) Explain how a cockroach supplies oxygen to its flight muscles even though its haemolymph lacks haemoglobin [3 marks]. (c) Name one invertebrate that possesses a closed circulatory system [2 marks].

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) 1. Vessels: Fluid empties into open haemocoel vs Blood stays inside continuous vessels (1). 2. Contact with cells: Haemolymph bathes organs directly vs Exchange occurs across capillary walls via tissue fluid (1). 3. Pressure: Very low hydrostatic pressure vs High hydrostatic pressure (1). 4. Speed of flow: Slow/sluggish vs Rapid (1). 5. Example: Cockroach/grasshopper vs Earthworm/human (1).
(b) Air enters through paired lateral spiracles into branching chitin-lined tracheae and fluid-filled tracheoles that penetrate directly into flight muscle fibres for gaseous diffusion (3).
(c) Earthworm (Lumbricus terrestris) (or squid / octopus) (2).

3. (a) Distinguish between single circulation in a bony fish and double circulation in a mammal, stating the number of heart chambers in each [4 marks]. (b) State two advantages of complete double circulation over incomplete double circulation [2 marks]. (c) An athlete has a heart rate of 140 beats/min and a stroke volume of 105 mL/beat. Calculate his cardiac output in L/min and the time in seconds required to pump 4.90 L of blood [4 marks].

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) In a bony fish (2 chambers: 1 atrium, 1 ventricle), blood passes through the heart once per complete circuit (Heart → Gills → Body → Heart) (2). In a mammal (4 chambers: 2 atria, 2 ventricles), blood passes through the heart twice per complete circuit via separate pulmonary and systemic loops (2).
(b) 1. Prevents any mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood so tissues receive 100% oxygenated blood (1). 2. Allows high systemic arterial pressure (~120 mmHg) while keeping pulmonary capillary pressure low (~25 mmHg), supporting endothermy (1).
(c) Cardiac output: CO = 140 × 105 = 14700 mL/min (1); convert to L/min: CO_L = 14700 ÷ 1000 = 14.7 L/min (1). Time for 4.90 L: t = (4.90 ÷ 14.7) × 60 = 20.0 s (2).
