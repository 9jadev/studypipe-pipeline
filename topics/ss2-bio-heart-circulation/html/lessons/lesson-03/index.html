Practical Analysis Questions

Answer these three WAEC cardiac physiology questions, then compare with the model answers.

  1. What detects a rise in blood CO₂ concentration (drop in blood pH) during exercise, and which part of the brain responds to speed up the heart?
  2. What is a heart murmur, and what structural defect causes it?
  3. Why does cardiac muscle never suffer from muscle cramp or fatigue the way skeletal leg muscle does during a 400 m sprint?
Model answers unlocks after you try
  1. Chemoreceptors in the carotid bodies, aortic arch, and medulla detect the rise in CO₂ / H⁺ and signal the cardiovascular centre in the medulla oblongata, which sends sympathetic impulses to the SAN to increase heart rate.
  2. A heart murmur is an abnormal swishing or hissing sound heard between “Lub” and “Dub” caused by turbulent backflow (regurgitation) through a leaky, damaged valve or narrowing (stenosis) of a valve opening.
  3. Cardiac muscle has an exceptionally rich blood supply (coronary capillaries), abundant mitochondria (~40% of cell volume), myoglobin, and a long refractory period (~0.25 s) that guarantees relaxation (diastole) between every contraction.

Myogenic Conduction, The 0.80 s Cardiac Cycle, “Lub-Dub” & Blood Pressure

Let’s master the complete electrical, mechanical, acoustic, and pressure events of the mammalian heartbeat for WAEC, NECO, and JAMB Biology.

1 · Myogenic Contraction & The Electrical Conduction System

Mammalian cardiac muscle is myogenic — its rhythmic contraction originates spontaneously within the muscle tissue itself without requiring an external motor nerve impulse from the brain. If a frog or mammalian heart is removed from the body and placed in warm, oxygenated Ringer’s solution, it continues to beat rhythmically on its own!

StepConduction StructureAnatomical LocationPhysiological Role in the Heartbeat
1Sinoatrial Node (SAN) — The Natural PacemakerUpper wall of the right atrium near the opening of the superior vena cavaSpontaneously depolarises (~72–75 times/min at rest) to initiate a wave of electrical excitation that spreads across both left and right atria, causing atrial systole.
2Atrioventricular Node (AVN)Lower part of the interatrial septum at the junction of atria and ventriclesReceives the wave from the SAN and introduces a crucial ~0.10 s delay so the atria finish contracting and emptying into the ventricles before ventricular contraction begins.
3Bundle of His (Atrioventricular Bundle)Runs down the muscular interventricular septum (dividing into left and right bundle branches)Conducts the electrical impulse rapidly from the AVN down the septum to the bottom tip (apex) of the heart.
4Purkinje Fibres (Purkyne Tissue)Fine conducting fibres branching upward throughout the walls of both ventriclesSpread excitation rapidly from the apex upward so both ventricles contract simultaneously from the bottom up, squeezing blood cleanly into the aorta and pulmonary artery.

2 · The Three Phases of the Cardiac Cycle (T = 0.80 s at 75 bpm)

One complete heartbeat (cardiac cycle) consists of contraction (systole) and relaxation (diastole). At a resting heart rate of 75 beats/min, one cycle lasts T = 60 ÷ 75 = 0.80 second:

Phase of Cardiac CycleDuration (at 75 bpm)Atria & VentriclesTricuspid & Bicuspid (AV) ValvesAortic & Pulmonary Semilunar ValvesHeart Sound & Blood Flow
1 · Atrial Systole (Ventricular Diastole)0.10 sAtria contract; Ventricles are relaxedOPENCLOSEDAtria squeeze the final ~20–30% of blood through open AV valves to top up the ventricles (reaching EDV ≈ 120 mL).
2 · Ventricular Systole (Atrial Diastole)0.30 sAtria relax; Ventricles contract powerfully from apex upwardSNAP SHUT (held taut by chordae tendineae)FORCED OPENFirst sound “LUB” (S1) as AV valves slam shut; blood is ejected at high pressure into the aorta (120 mmHg) and pulmonary artery (25 mmHg).
3 · General (Joint) Diastole0.40 sBoth Atria and Ventricles are relaxedOPEN (after ventricular pressure drops below atrial pressure)SNAP SHUT at start of phaseSecond sound “DUB” (S2) as arterial back-pressure snaps semilunar valves shut; blood flows passively from venae cavae and pulmonary veins into atria and ventricles.

3 · Governing Equations: Cycle Time, Cardiac Output, Pulse Pressure & MAP

T = 60 ÷ HR = 60 ÷ 75 = 0.80 s = 0.10 s + 0.30 s + 0.40 s
PP = P_sys − P_dia = 120 − 80 = 40 mmHg
MAP = P_dia + (PP ÷ 3) = 80 + (40 ÷ 3) = 93.3 mmHg

4 · Heart Sounds (“Lub-Dub”), Arterial Pulse, Blood Pressure & Nervous/Hormonal Control

  • First Heart Sound (“LUB” / S1): Lower-pitched, louder, and slightly longer sound caused by the simultaneous closure of the bicuspid and tricuspid (AV) valves and vibration of the chordae tendineae at the onset of ventricular systole.
  • Second Heart Sound (“DUB” / S2): Higher-pitched, sharper, and shorter sound caused by the simultaneous closure of the aortic and pulmonary semilunar valves at the onset of ventricular diastole.
  • Arterial Pulse: The rhythmic wave of expansion and elastic recoil that travels along arterial walls following each left ventricular systole. It is easily felt where an artery runs close to the skin over a bone — such as the radial artery at the wrist, the carotid artery in the neck, or the temporal artery at the temple. Pulse rate equals heart rate (70–75 bpm in a resting adult; higher in infants and children).
  • Blood Pressure (120/80 mmHg): The hydrostatic lateral force exerted by circulating blood against the walls of the main arteries, measured over the brachial artery using a sphygmomanometer and stethoscope. Systolic pressure (~120 mmHg) occurs during ventricular contraction; diastolic pressure (~80 mmHg) occurs during ventricular relaxation (maintained by elastic recoil of the aorta). Persistent blood pressure above 140/90 mmHg at rest is hypertension.
  • Nervous & Hormonal Modulation (Cardiovascular Centre in the Medulla Oblongata): Although the SAN is myogenic, the medulla oblongata adjusts heart rate to match metabolic demand:
    • Accelerator (Sympathetic) Nerve & Adrenaline: High blood $\text{CO}_2$ (low pH during exercise), low blood pressure, fear, or excitement stimulates the sympathetic cardiac nerve (releasing noradrenaline) and the adrenal medulla (secreting adrenaline), which speed up the SAN and increase stroke volume.
    • Inhibitory (Parasympathetic Vagus) Nerve: High blood pressure detected by baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch stimulates the vagus nerve (Cranial Nerve X), which releases acetylcholine onto the SAN to slow down the heart rate during rest.

Worked examples, step by step

Every calculation is broken down one atomic mathematical step at a time with a plain-English explainer note underneath.

EXAMPLE 1

Cardiac Cycle Duration & Diastolic Rest Time per Minute

A resting student has a heart rate of HR = 75 beats/min. In each cardiac cycle, atrial systole lasts 0.10 s and ventricular systole lasts 0.30 s, while the remaining time is general diastole. Calculate: (a) the total duration T of one cardiac cycle in seconds, (b) the duration of general diastole in each cycle, and (c) the total number of seconds in every minute (60 s) that all four chambers spend resting in general diastole.

  1. Part (a) — Divide 60 seconds by heart rate HR = 75 bpm: T = 60 ÷ 75 = 0.80 s.
  2. Part (b) — Subtract atrial (0.10 s) and ventricular (0.30 s) systole from total cycle T: t_dia = 0.80 − 0.40 = 0.40 s.
  3. Part (c) — Multiply general diastole per beat (0.40 s) by 75 beats/min: T_rest = 0.40 × 75 = 30.0 s.
(a) Cycle Duration T = 0.80 s  ·  (b) General Diastole = 0.40 s/beat  ·  (c) 30.0 seconds of rest every minute!
EXAMPLE 2

Pulse Pressure (PP) & Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)

Using a sphygmomanometer at the LUTH clinic, Dr Bamidele records a patient’s brachial blood pressure as 135/90 mmHg. Calculate: (a) the patient’s pulse pressure PP, and (b) the patient’s mean arterial pressure MAP using MAP = Pdia + (PP ÷ 3).

  1. Part (a) — Subtract diastolic pressure (90 mmHg) from systolic pressure (135 mmHg): PP = 135 − 90 = 45 mmHg.
  2. Part (b) Step 1 — Divide pulse pressure PP by 3: ΔP = 45 ÷ 3 = 15 mmHg.
  3. Part (b) Step 2 — Add ΔP to diastolic pressure Pdia: MAP = 90 + 15 = 105 mmHg.
(a) Pulse Pressure PP = 45 mmHg  ·  (b) Mean Arterial Pressure MAP = 105 mmHg
EXAMPLE 3

Finding Stroke Volume (SV) from Cardiac Output & Cycle Duration

During moderate exercise, an athlete’s cardiac cycle duration shortens to T = 0.50 s and her cardiac output is measured as CO = 10.8 L/min (10,800 mL/min). Calculate: (a) her heart rate HR in beats/min, and (b) her stroke volume SV in mL/beat.

  1. Part (a) — Divide 60 seconds by cycle duration T = 0.50 s: HR = 60 ÷ 0.50 = 120 bpm.
  2. Part (b) — Divide cardiac output (10,800 mL/min) by heart rate (120 bpm): SV = 10800 ÷ 120 = 90 mL/beat.
(a) Heart Rate HR = 120 beats/min  ·  (b) Stroke Volume SV = 90 mL/beat

2. (a) Describe the events that occur during (i) Atrial Systole, (ii) Ventricular Systole, and (iii) General Diastole, stating the position of the atrioventricular and semilunar valves in each phase [6 marks]. (b) Account for the production of the “Lub” and “Dub” sounds of the heart [4 marks].

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) (i) Atrial Systole (0.10 s): Both atria contract while ventricles relax; tricuspid and bicuspid (AV) valves are open and semilunar valves are closed (2). (ii) Ventricular Systole (0.30 s): Both ventricles contract while atria relax; AV valves snap shut and semilunar valves are forced open to eject blood into the aorta and pulmonary artery (2). (iii) General Diastole (0.40 s): Both atria and ventricles relax; semilunar valves snap shut and AV valves open as chambers refill passively (2).
(b) “LUB” (first sound): Produced by the simultaneous closure of the bicuspid and tricuspid (AV) valves at the start of ventricular systole (2). “DUB” (second sound): Produced by the simultaneous closure of the aortic and pulmonary semilunar valves at the start of ventricular diastole (2).

3. (a) Distinguish between systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure, naming the instrument used to measure them in humans [3 marks]. (b) A patient has a blood pressure of 144/90 mmHg and a heart rate of 80 beats/min with a stroke volume of 75 mL. Calculate: (i) the cardiac cycle duration T in seconds, (ii) the cardiac output in L/min, (iii) the pulse pressure PP, and (iv) the mean arterial pressure MAP [7 marks].

Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Systolic pressure is the peak arterial pressure during left ventricular contraction (~120 mmHg), whereas diastolic pressure is the minimum arterial pressure during ventricular relaxation (~80 mmHg) (2). Measured with a sphygmomanometer (1).
(b) (i) Cycle duration: T = 60 ÷ 80 = 0.75 s (1.5). (ii) Cardiac output: CO = 80 × 75 = 6000 mL/min = 6.00 L/min (2). (iii) Pulse pressure: PP = 144 − 90 = 54 mmHg (1.5). (iv) Mean arterial pressure: MAP = 90 + (54 ÷ 3) = 90 + 18 = 108 mmHg (2).
