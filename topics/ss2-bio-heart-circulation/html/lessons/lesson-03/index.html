How does the rider's donated blood reach his brain, and why does food from the gut go to the liver first?

Practical analysis questions

Answer these five questions, then compare with the model answers.

  1. Why must every body-to-body route pass through the lungs?
  2. What changes in the blood in the lung capillaries?
  3. Why does gut blood go to the liver first?
  4. What makes the hepatic portal vein different from other veins?
  5. Why can't heart muscle feed on the blood inside its chambers?
Model answers unlocks after you try
  1. The septum splits the heart in two. Blood can only get from the right side to the left side by going round through the lungs, so there is no shortcut.
  2. It loses carbon dioxide and gains oxygen, turning dark oxygen-poor blood into bright oxygen-rich blood.
  3. Gut blood carries newly absorbed food and any swallowed poisons. The liver stores extra glucose as glycogen, breaks down surplus amino acids and makes poisons harmless before the blood reaches the rest of the body.
  4. It begins in capillaries (of the gut) and ends in capillaries (of the liver), instead of draining a capillary bed straight to the heart.
  5. The heart wall is too thick for oxygen to diffuse from the chambers to every cell fast enough, and the right side holds oxygen-poor blood anyway. It needs its own supply, the coronary arteries.
3Make sense · 15 min

Two loops, two pumps

Now we connect the route you traced to the names and jobs WAEC, NECO and JAMB expect.

Movement break (45 min mark, 5 minutes): stand up and trace the double circuit on your own body. Point to your right ventricle in the middle of your chest, then sweep your right hand to your lungs (the small loop) and back to the left side of your chest. Now push your arm out to your head, then your toes (the big loop), and bring both hands back to your heart. Do two full circuits, breathing in as the blood passes the lungs. Shake out your hands and sit down.

Two loops (one complete circuit passes through the heart twice): the pulmonary circulation runs right ventricle → pulmonary artery → capillaries of the lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium, and swaps carbon dioxide for oxygen. The systemic circulation runs left ventricle → aorta → every body organ → venae cavae → right atrium, and delivers oxygen and food while collecting wastes.

Within the body

heart → arteries → arterioles → capillaries → venules → veins → heart

The hepatic portal system: blood leaving the stomach and intestines is rich in absorbed food and any swallowed poisons. Instead of returning straight to the heart, it travels in the hepatic portal vein to the liver. There, extra glucose is stored as glycogen, surplus amino acids are broken down (making urea) and poisons are made harmless. Only then does blood leave by the hepatic vein for the inferior vena cava. Any vein that links two capillary beds is called a portal vein.

The coronary circulation: the coronary arteries branch off the aorta just beyond its valve and spread over the heart surface; the coronary veins return blood to the right atrium. If a coronary artery is blocked, the muscle beyond it is starved of oxygen and dies: a heart attack.

4Apply · Worked examples · 15 min

Worked examples, step by step

Try each route on paper first, then check the working. Every route starts at a named organ and names every valve.

EXAMPLE 1

The drip: arm to brain

List the path of blood from a forearm vein to the brain, naming the valves.

  1. Into the heart: forearm vein → subclavian vein → superior vena cava (SVC) → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle.
  2. To the lungs and back: pulmonary semilunar valve → pulmonary artery → lung capillaries (CO₂ out, O₂ in) → pulmonary veins → left atrium.
  3. Out to the brain: bicuspid valve → left ventricle → aortic semilunar valve → aorta → carotid artery → brain.
  4. Count the valves: 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4 (tricuspid, pulmonary semilunar, bicuspid, aortic semilunar).
Four heart valves are passed, and the lungs cannot be skipped.
EXAMPLE 2

Glucose from the ileum to a leg muscle

Trace glucose from the capillaries of the ileum wall to a muscle in the leg.

  1. Gut to liver: capillaries of the ileum wall → hepatic portal vein → liver (sugar stored) → hepatic vein → IVC.
  2. Right side and lungs: right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle → pulmonary semilunar valve → pulmonary artery → lung capillaries → pulmonary veins.
  3. Left side and out: left atrium → bicuspid valve → left ventricle → aortic semilunar valve → aorta → femoral artery → leg muscle.
  4. Count the valves: 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4.
The liver comes first, the lungs second.
EXAMPLE 3

Urea from the liver to the kidney

Trace urea made in the liver to the kidney, where it will be removed.

  1. Liver to the heart: liver → hepatic vein → IVC (inferior vena cava) → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle.
  2. Through the lungs: pulmonary semilunar valve → pulmonary artery → lung capillaries → pulmonary veins → left atrium → bicuspid valve → left ventricle.
  3. Out to the kidney: aortic semilunar valve → aorta → renal artery → kidney.
  4. Count the valves: 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4.
Four valves again: the lungs are always on the way.
EXAMPLE 4

Oxygen from the lungs to the heart muscle

Trace newly oxygenated blood from the lung capillaries to the muscle of the heart wall.

  1. Lungs to the left heart: lung capillaries → pulmonary vein → left atrium → bicuspid valve → left ventricle.
  2. Out and round: aortic semilunar valve → aorta → coronary artery → heart muscle.
  3. Count the valves: 1 + 1 = 2 (bicuspid and aortic semilunar).
Only two valves: the right side of the heart is not involved.

Part B · Written paper (30 marks)

1. Definitions

3 marks

Define (a) pulmonary circulation; (b) systemic circulation; (c) portal vein.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) The pulmonary circulation is the loop that carries blood from the right ventricle to the lungs and back to the left atrium, where carbon dioxide is lost and oxygen gained. (1) (b) The systemic circulation is the loop that carries blood from the left ventricle to all the body organs and back to the right atrium. (1) (c) A portal vein is a vein that begins in one capillary bed and ends in another capillary bed, instead of returning blood directly to the heart. (1)

Mark scheme note: Equivalent wordings that keep the key ideas are credited. The start and end chambers are needed in (a) and (b); "blood going to the lungs" alone scores zero. "A vein that carries food" in (c) is not accepted; the mark is for linking two capillary beds.

2. The Full Course of Blood

6 marks

Starting with blood entering the heart from the venae cavae, describe its course through the heart, lungs and body until it returns to the right atrium. Name every chamber, valve and main vessel.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(1) Blood low in oxygen arrives in the right atrium, which squeezes it across the tricuspid valve to fill the right ventricle. (1) (2) The right ventricle contracts, forcing the pulmonary semilunar valve open, and the blood is sent along the pulmonary artery. (1) (3) In the capillaries round the air sacs of the lungs, carbon dioxide leaves the blood and oxygen enters it. (1) (4) Now rich in oxygen, it returns along the pulmonary veins into the left atrium, and from there crosses the bicuspid (mitral) valve to fill the left ventricle. (1) (5) The left ventricle contracts strongly, forcing the aortic semilunar valve open; the aorta divides into arteries and arterioles that lead to capillaries in every organ, where the cells take oxygen and nutrients from the blood. (1) (6) The capillaries merge into venules and then veins, which empty into the superior and inferior venae cavae, and these return the blood to the right atrium. (1)

Mark scheme note: Each mark point needs its named valve or vessel; a point with a wrong valve (e.g. "bicuspid" on the right) scores zero. Skipping the lungs loses mark (3) and breaks the sequence for (4). Answers in a clear arrow chain are fully acceptable if every named part is present.

3. The Hepatic Portal System

4 marks

(a) State where the hepatic portal vein begins and where it ends. (b) State three things the liver does to the blood it receives through this vein.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) It begins in the capillaries of the stomach and intestine walls and ends in the capillaries of the liver. (1) (b) Extra glucose is stored as glycogen. (1) Surplus amino acids are broken down (deaminated), forming urea. (1) Poisons and drugs absorbed from the gut are made harmless (detoxified). (1)

Mark scheme note: "It goes to the heart" in (a) scores zero. In (b) the three marks are for three different jobs; "removes glucose" and "stores sugar" count as one point. "Makes bile from the blood" is not accepted here, since the question is about changes to the blood's absorbed contents.

4. Feeding the Heart Itself

3 marks

(a) From which vessel do the coronary arteries branch? (b) Explain why heart muscle cannot rely on the blood inside its own chambers for oxygen. (c) State what happens if a coronary artery is blocked.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) From the aorta, just beyond the aortic semilunar valve. (1) (b) The heart wall is too thick for oxygen to diffuse from the chambers to all its cells fast enough (and the right-side chambers hold oxygen-poor blood). (1) (c) The heart muscle beyond the blockage is starved of oxygen and dies: a heart attack. (1)

Mark scheme note: "From the heart" in (a) is too vague. In (b), the key idea is diffusion distance through a thick wall, the same reason from Lesson 01 that large animals need transport. "The person dies" in (c) without the idea of starved heart muscle earns nothing.

5. Vessels In and Out

4 marks

For each organ, name the artery that brings blood to it and the vein that takes blood away: (a) kidney; (b) arm; (c) leg; (d) brain.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) Renal artery; renal vein. (1) (b) Subclavian artery; subclavian vein. (1) (c) Iliac (or femoral) artery; iliac (or femoral) vein. (1) (d) Carotid artery; jugular vein. (1)

Mark scheme note: Each mark needs both vessels correct and in the right order (artery in, vein out). Swapping the artery and vein for an organ scores zero for that organ.

6. From the Ileum to an Arm Muscle

5 marks

Trace the route taken by an amino acid absorbed in the ileum until it reaches a muscle in the arm. Name every vessel, chamber and valve.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(1) The ileum capillaries drain into the hepatic portal vein, which carries the amino acid into the liver. (1) (2) Blood leaves the liver by the hepatic vein, which empties into the inferior vena cava. (1) (3) It reaches the right atrium, crosses the tricuspid valve to the right ventricle, and is pumped past the pulmonary semilunar valve along the pulmonary artery. (1) (4) After the lung capillaries, the pulmonary veins return it to the left atrium, and it crosses the bicuspid valve to the left ventricle. (1) (5) The left ventricle pumps it past the aortic semilunar valve and out along the aorta, which hands it to the subclavian artery supplying the arm muscle. (1)

Mark scheme note: Sending the amino acid from the gut straight to the heart loses marks (1) and (2). Any route that leaves out the lungs loses mark (4). "Vena cava" without "inferior" is accepted only if the hepatic vein is named before it.

7. Data Analysis: A Faulty Route

3 marks

On the route-tracer bench, a student recorded this journey for urea leaving the kidney and reaching the brain: renal vein → inferior vena cava → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle → left atrium → bicuspid valve → left ventricle → aortic semilunar valve → aorta → carotid artery → brain.<br>(a) Identify the stage the student left out. (b) Explain why this stage cannot be skipped. (c) State how many heart valves the correct route passes.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) The trip to the lungs: right ventricle → pulmonary semilunar valve → pulmonary artery → lung capillaries → pulmonary veins → left atrium. (1) (b) The septum separates the two halves of the heart, so blood can only get from the right side to the left side by going round through the lungs. (1) (c) Four (tricuspid, pulmonary semilunar, bicuspid, aortic semilunar). (1)

Mark scheme note: "The lungs" alone earns (a) only if the answer makes clear the stage lies between the right ventricle and left atrium. In (b), "to get oxygen" alone is not enough; the mark is for the halves being separated, so there is no other path. Answers of "2" in (c) come from counting only one side.

8. Practical: Setting Up the Rider's Drip

2 marks

At the screening tent, a nurse set up a drip in the okada rider's forearm vein. (a) Give one reason the drip was put into a vein and not an artery. (b) State one precaution the nurse took.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) Any one: blood pressure in a vein is low, so fluid can run in and there is little bleeding at the needle site (1); veins lie near the surface and are easy to find (1); a vein carries the fluid to the heart, which then pumps it to the whole body (1). (Maximum 1.) (b) Any one, in the past tense: hands were washed and gloves worn (1); a new, sterile needle was used once only (1); air bubbles were run out of the line before it was connected (1); the label on the bag was checked against the rider's card (1). (Maximum 1.)

Mark scheme note: In (a), "arteries carry oxygenated blood" is not a reason and scores zero. A precaution written as an instruction or in the future tense loses the mark in (b).
