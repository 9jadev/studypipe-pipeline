Model answers unlocks after you try
  1. The atria only need to pump blood a very short distance downward through the open AV valves into the adjacent ventricles just below them.
  2. The left ventricle has a circular cavity surrounded by a very thick muscular wall (~15 mm), whereas the right ventricle has a crescent-shaped cavity with a much thinner wall (~5 mm).
  3. Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix across the septal defect, reducing systemic oxygen delivery and causing breathlessness, fatigue, and cyanosis (“blue baby” syndrome).

Heart Membranes, Four Chambers, Four One-Way Valves & Pathway

Let’s assemble the complete anatomical and physiological blueprint of the mammalian heart required for WAEC, NECO, and JAMB Biology.

1 · Pericardium, Heart Wall Layers & The Four Chambers

The human heart is a hollow, cone-shaped muscular organ about the size of a clenched fist (~300 g), located in the mediastinum of the thoracic cavity between the two lungs, slightly tilted to the left. It is enclosed by a tough, double-walled fibrous sac called the pericardium. Between the inner visceral and outer parietal pericardial membranes lies pericardial fluid, which:

  • Reduces friction as the heart contracts and relaxes ~100,000 times every day, and
  • Cushions mechanical shocks and prevents over-expansion (over-filling) of the heart chambers.

The heart wall itself consists of three layers: the outer epicardium, the thick middle myocardium (branched, striated, involuntary cardiac muscle rich in mitochondria and linked by intercalated discs that never fatigues), and the smooth inner endothelial lining called the endocardium.

4 · Complete Double-Circulation Pathway, Coronary Vessels & Portal Systems

Trace one complete lap of a red blood cell through the mammalian cardiovascular system:

Venae Cavae → Right Atrium → Tricuspid Valve → Right Ventricle → Pulmonary Semilunar Valve → Pulmonary Artery (deoxygenated!) → Lung Alveolar Capillaries (O₂ in, CO₂ out) → Pulmonary Veins (oxygenated!) → Left Atrium → Bicuspid (Mitral) Valve → Left Ventricle → Aortic Semilunar Valve → Aorta → Systemic Arteries → Organ Capillaries → Systemic Veins → Venae Cavae.

  • The Great Vessel Exception: Every artery carries blood away from the heart and every vein carries blood towards the heart. Therefore, the pulmonary artery is the only artery in an adult mammal that carries deoxygenated blood, and the pulmonary veins are the only veins that carry oxygenated blood. (In a mammalian foetus, the umbilical artery also carries deoxygenated blood to the placenta, while the umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood from the placenta to the foetus.)
  • Coronary Circulation: The thick myocardium cannot absorb oxygen quickly enough from the blood rushing through its chambers. Instead, the left and right coronary arteries branch directly off the base of the ascending aorta (just above the aortic semilunar valve) to deliver oxygenated blood and glucose to cardiac muscle cells, draining via cardiac veins and the coronary sinus into the right atrium. Blockage of a coronary artery by a blood clot (thrombus) or atheroma causes a myocardial infarction (heart attack).
  • Hepatic Portal System: A portal vein begins and ends in capillaries. The hepatic portal vein collects blood rich in newly absorbed glucose and amino acids from the capillaries of the small intestine (ileum villi) and delivers it directly to the sinusoids of the liver before the hepatic vein drains into the inferior vena cava. This allows the liver to regulate blood glucose (converting excess glucose to glycogen under insulin), deaminate excess amino acids into urea, and detoxify poisons before blood reaches the rest of the body.

EXAMPLE 1

Stroke Volume (SV), Ejection Fraction (EF%) & Ventricular Wall Ratio

On Tunde’s echocardiogram at LUTH, his left ventricular wall thickness is wLV = 15.0 mm and his right ventricular wall thickness is wRV = 5.0 mm. At the end of diastole, his left ventricle holds EDV = 120 mL of blood, and at the end of systole ESV = 50 mL remains. Calculate: (a) the ratio of left-to-right ventricular wall thickness, (b) his stroke volume SV, and (c) his left ventricular ejection fraction EF%.

  1. Part (a) — Divide left ventricular wall thickness by right ventricular wall thickness: R_w = 15.0 ÷ 5.0 = 3.0.
  2. Part (b) — Subtract ESV from EDV to find stroke volume SV: SV = 120 − 50 = 70 mL.
  3. Part (c) — Divide SV by EDV and multiply by 100% to find ejection fraction EF%: EF = (70 ÷ 120) × 100 = 58.3%.
(a) Wall Thickness Ratio = 3 : 1  ·  (b) Stroke Volume SV = 70 mL  ·  (c) Ejection Fraction = 58.3%
EXAMPLE 2

WAEC Paper 3 Dissected Heart Drawing Magnification (M = I ÷ A)

During a WAEC Paper 3 practical exam, Amaka examines a dissected longitudinal section of a goat’s heart of actual length A = 85 mm. She makes a biological drawing of length I = 11.9 cm. Calculate the linear magnification M of her drawing.

  1. Step 1 — Convert drawing length I from centimetres to millimetres (× 10): I = 11.9 × 10 = 119 mm.
  2. Step 2 — Divide drawing length I by actual specimen length A: M = 119 ÷ 85 = 1.4×.
Linear Magnification of Heart Drawing M = ×1.4
EXAMPLE 3

Comparing Systemic vs Pulmonary Pressure & Ventricular Work

During a single heartbeat, both the left and right ventricles eject the same stroke volume SV = 70 mL. However, the left ventricle ejects blood at a peak systolic pressure of PLV = 120 mmHg whereas the right ventricle ejects blood at PRV = 24 mmHg. Calculate how many times greater the peak pressure (and mechanical stroke work per beat) of the left ventricle is compared to the right ventricle.

  1. Step 1 — Write the ratio of peak systolic pressures PLV ÷ PRV: F_P = 120 ÷ 24 = 5.0.
  2. Step 2 — Multiply equal stroke volumes (70 mL ÷ 70 mL = 1) by the pressure ratio: W_ratio = 5.0 × 1 = 5.0.
The Left Ventricle generates 5.0 times higher peak pressure and mechanical work per beat

The big idea of Lesson 2: The mammalian heart is a dual four-chambered pump separated by an impermeable septum. Both ventricles eject the exact same stroke volume (~70 mL), but the left ventricular wall (~15 mm) is 3× thicker than the right (~5 mm) to generate ~120 mmHg for systemic circulation, while four one-way valves (tricuspid, bicuspid, and two semilunar valves) anchored by chordae tendineae enforce strict one-way flow!

In two sentences: state the exact function of the chordae tendineae, and explain why the pulmonary artery is classified as an artery even though it carries deoxygenated blood.

2 marks
Model answer unlocks after you try

The chordae tendineae are inelastic tendons attached to papillary muscles that hold the bicuspid and tricuspid valve flaps taut during ventricular systole so they cannot invert into the atria (1). The pulmonary artery is classified as an artery because it carries blood away from the heart (from the right ventricle to the lungs), and vessel classification depends on direction of flow rather than oxygen content (1).

Part B · Structured Written Questions (30 marks)

1. (a) State two functions of the pericardium and pericardial fluid [2 marks]. (b) Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is much thicker than the wall of the right ventricle, and why the walls of the atria are thinner than both [4 marks]. (c) State the exact location and function of (i) the tricuspid valve, (ii) the bicuspid valve, and (iii) the chordae tendineae [4 marks].

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) 1. Pericardial fluid lubricates the heart surfaces to reduce friction during contraction (1). 2. The fibrous pericardium protects the heart from mechanical shock and prevents over-expansion (1).
(b) The left ventricle must generate high pressure (~120 mmHg) to pump blood through the long systemic circuit around the entire body, whereas the right ventricle pumps blood only a short distance to the lungs at low pressure (~25 mmHg) (2). The atria have thin walls (~2 mm) because they only push blood into the immediately adjacent ventricles (2).
(c) (i) Tricuspid valve: Between right atrium and right ventricle; prevents backflow into RA (1). (ii) Bicuspid valve: Between left atrium and left ventricle; prevents backflow into LA (1). (iii) Chordae tendineae: Link AV valve cusps to papillary muscles in ventricles; prevent the valve flaps from turning inside out into the atria during ventricular systole (2).

(b) Explain the roles of (i) the coronary arteries and (ii) the hepatic portal vein [4 marks].

Solution unlocks after you try

(a) Hepatic vein → Inferior vena cava → Right atrium → Tricuspid valve → Right ventricle → Pulmonary semilunar valve → Pulmonary artery → Lung capillaries → Pulmonary vein → Left atrium → Bicuspid (mitral) valve → Left ventricle → Aortic semilunar valve → Aorta → Renal artery (6 marks).

3. (a) During a cardiac scan, a patient’s left ventricle has an end-diastolic volume (EDV) of 140 mL and an end-systolic volume (ESV) of 63 mL. Calculate (i) the stroke volume SV and (ii) the ejection fraction EF% [4 marks]. (b) If the patient’s heart rate is 80 beats/min, calculate the cardiac output in L/min [3 marks]. (c) A student draws a dissected heart specimen 13.5 cm long. If the actual heart length is 90 mm, calculate the linear magnification of the drawing [3 marks].

10 marks
Solution unlocks after you try

(a) (i) Stroke volume: SV = 140 − 63 = 77 mL (2). (ii) Ejection fraction: EF = (77 ÷ 140) × 100 = 55.0% (2).
(b) Cardiac output: CO = 80 × 77 = 6160 mL/min (1.5); in L/min: CO_L = 6160 ÷ 1000 = 6.16 L/min (1.5).
