2Investigate · 3D Practical · 25 min

The goat-heart dissection: measure every wall

Dr Aisha's bench holds a fresh goat heart, a scalpel and a 30 cm ruler. Cut the heart open, then measure each chamber wall and the septum twice at mid-height. The heart faces you, so the animal's right side is on your left.

Safety: wear gloves, keep the blade facing away from you, cut on the dissecting board and wash your hands afterwards.

STEP 01

Rinse, lay flat, find the vessels

Wearing gloves, rinse the heart and lay it front side up (the side with the diagonal groove). Feel for the thick, rubbery aorta and the thinner pulmonary artery.

STEP 02

Cut from apex to base

With the blade facing away from you, cut from the apex upwards through both ventricles to expose all four chambers. Press Cut section.

STEP 03

Find the inside parts

Find the septum, both atrioventricular valves, the chordae tendineae and papillary muscles, and the semilunar pockets at the exit vessels. Press Show parts.

STEP 04

Measure each wall twice

At mid-height, measure the wall of each chamber and the septum twice with the ruler at eye level, and record the mean. Tap the Measure buttons.

Precautions and sources of error

Precautions (past tense): gloves were worn and hands were washed afterwards · the scalpel was used on a dissecting board and cut away from the body · every wall was measured at the same height, midway between base and apex · the ruler was read with the eye level with the mark · each wall was measured twice and the mean taken.

Sources of error (with direction): a slanting cut exposed the wall at an angle, so its thickness read too high · measuring where a papillary muscle joined the wall added muscle that is not wall, so the reading was too high · a specimen stored in a fridge for days had shrunk and lost fluid, so walls read too low · reading the ruler from above or below the mark (parallax) made each reading too high or too low at random. 3Make sense · 15 min

Chambers, walls, valves and vessels

Now we connect what your hands and eyes found on the bench to the names and jobs WAEC, NECO and JAMB expect.

Movement break (45 min mark, 5 minutes): stand up. Make a fist, the size of your own heart, and hold it in the middle of your chest, tilted a little to the left. Squeeze it gently twice for the right ventricle: a short push to the lungs beside it. Now squeeze hard and stretch your arm out to your toes and then your head for the left ventricle: a long push round the whole body. Shake out your hands and sit down.

1 · Position and coverings

The heart sits in the chest between the two lungs, tilted a little to the left, shielded by the breastbone and ribs. It is wrapped in the pericardium, a two-layered sac with slippery fluid between the layers so the beating heart does not rub. Its wall has a thick central layer of heart muscle, the myocardium, which beats rhythmically, never tires and starts each beat itself. Inside, the smooth endocardium lets blood slide past.

2 · The four chambers

ChamberReceives blood fromSends blood toWall
Right atrium (RA)both venae cavae (oxygen-poor)right ventriclethin
Right ventricle (RV)right atriumlungs, by the pulmonary arterythicker than the atria
Left atrium (LA)four pulmonary veins (oxygen-rich)left ventriclethin
Left ventricle (LV)left atriumwhole body, by the aortathickest, about three times the RV

Why the walls differ: a chamber's wall matches its job. The atria only squeeze blood a few centimetres down into the ventricles. The right ventricle pushes blood a short way to the nearby lungs. The left ventricle must drive blood through every organ from scalp to toes, so it needs the most muscle.

3 · Valves and supports

PartWhereDetail
Tricuspid valvebetween RA and RVthree flaps
Bicuspid (mitral) valvebetween LA and LVtwo flaps
Pulmonary semilunar valvebase of the pulmonary arterythree half-moon pockets
Aortic semilunar valvebase of the aortathree pockets

Chordae tendineae are tough cords anchoring the AV flaps to small papillary muscles that jut from the ventricle wall. When the ventricles squeeze, they hold the flaps so they cannot be blown back into the atria. The septum is the muscle partition that keeps right-side and left-side blood apart.

4 · The great vessels

VesselCarries blood
Superior vena cavafrom head, neck and arms, into the RA
Inferior vena cavafrom trunk and legs, into the RA
Pulmonary arteryRV to the lungs; the one artery with oxygen-poor blood
Pulmonary veins (four)lungs to the LA; the veins with oxygen-rich blood
AortaLV to the whole body
Coronary arteriesthe earliest branches off the aorta, feeding the heart muscle itself

Drawing rules (WAEC): draw the heart facing you, so the patient's right side is on your left · septum down the middle, AV boundary across each side · LV wall clearly thicker than RV · valves and chordae in place · flow arrows inside chambers · title in capitals below, e.g. LONGITUDINAL SECTION OF A MAMMALIAN HEART · ruled horizontal label lines with no arrowheads · magnification stated.

4Apply · Worked examples · 15 min

Worked examples, step by step

Try each problem on paper first, then check the working.

EXAMPLE 1

Wall ratio from readings

LV readings are 12.4 mm and 11.6 mm; RV readings are 4.2 mm and 3.8 mm. How many times thicker is the LV wall?

  1. Mean LV: (12.4 + 11.6) ÷ 2 = 12.0 mm.
  2. Mean RV: (4.2 + 3.8) ÷ 2 = 4.0 mm.
  3. Ratio: 12.0 ÷ 4.0 = 3.0.
The LV wall is 3.0 times thicker, matching the “about three times” rule.
EXAMPLE 2

Drawing magnification

The goat heart is 8.0 cm from base to apex. Chidi draws it 120 mm long. Find the magnification.

  1. Same unit: 120 mm = 12.0 cm.
  2. Magnification: M = 12.0 cm ÷ 8.0 cm.
  3. Result: M = 1.5, written ×1.5 with no unit.
Magnification = ×1.5
EXAMPLE 3

Actual size from a drawing

On that ×1.5 drawing the LV wall is 18 mm thick. Find its real thickness in cm.

  1. Rearrange: actual = drawing ÷ M.
  2. Substitute: 18 mm ÷ 1.5 = 12 mm.
  3. Convert: 12 mm ÷ 10 = 1.2 cm.
Real thickness = 1.2 cm, consistent with the 12.0 mm bench reading.
EXAMPLE 4

Functions of three parts

State the function of the septum, the tricuspid valve and the aorta.

  1. Septum: it keeps oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood apart.
  2. Tricuspid valve: it stops blood flowing back from the RV into the RA.
  3. Aorta: it carries oxygen-rich blood from the LV to the body.
Septum separates the halves · tricuspid stops backflow · aorta carries blood to the body

Part A · Home investigation: kitchen heart check

Measure a real heart and draw it to scale.

You need: a goat heart bought fresh from a market butcher by an adult (if none can be bought, use the 3D bench in this lesson and measure the screen model instead), disposable gloves or clean nylon bags over your hands, a chopping board, a sharp kitchen knife (the adult does all the cutting), a 30 cm ruler and plain paper.

  1. Rinse the heart in clean water and lay it on the board with the front (the side with the slanting groove) facing up.
  2. Measure its length from the top, where the vessels leave, to the pointed tip (apex), in cm.
  3. Make a large outline drawing of the heart, at least half a page tall, and measure the drawing's length in mm.
  4. Ask the adult to cut across both ventricles midway between top and tip. Measure the left and right ventricle walls twice each, away from any bulging papillary muscle, and find the means.
  5. Calculate the magnification of your drawing, and the ratio of left to right ventricle wall thickness.
  6. Wrap the heart for cooking or disposal, scrub the board and knife with soap, and wash your hands well.

Part B · Written paper (30 marks)

1. Position and Coverings

3 marks

(a) Describe the position of the heart in the body. (b) Name the double-layered sac around the heart, and state the job of the fluid between its layers.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) It lies in the chest (thoracic cavity) between the two lungs, tilted a little to the left, protected by the breastbone and ribs. (1) (b) The pericardium. (1) Its fluid reduces friction, so the beating heart does not rub against the sac. (1)

Mark scheme note: Equivalent wordings that keep the key ideas are credited. "On the left side of the chest" alone is not accepted in (a); the heart lies near the middle, between the lungs, only tilted to the left. Writing "pericardium" for the inner lining or "endocardium" for the sac loses the mark.

2. What Each Chamber Receives and Sends

4 marks

Copy and complete the table.

ChamberBlood comes in fromBlood goes out to
Left atrium(i) ______(ii) ______
Right ventricle(iii) ______(iv) ______
Model solution unlocks after you try

(i) The lungs, through the pulmonary veins. (1) (ii) The left ventricle, through the bicuspid valve. (1) (iii) The right atrium, through the tricuspid valve. (1) (iv) The lungs, through the pulmonary artery. (1)

Mark scheme note: Naming the vessel alone is accepted where it identifies the source correctly (e.g. "pulmonary veins" for (i)). "From the body" in (i) or "to the body" in (iv) is the commonest swap and scores zero.

3. Settling Baba Sule's Argument

3 marks

Baba Sule says, "The thick side of the heart is the right side, because right is strong." (a) Explain why the left ventricle has a thicker wall than the right ventricle. (b) Explain why both atria have thin walls.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) The left ventricle must drive its blood, under high pressure, along the aorta and out to every organ from head to feet, a long journey with much resistance. (1) The right ventricle has a short trip to make, to the lungs lying beside the heart, so it needs less muscle. (1) (b) Each atrium needs only to squeeze its blood a few centimetres down into the ventricle directly beneath it. (1)

Mark scheme note: The two marks in (a) are for two separate ideas: the long whole-body route for the left, and the short lung route for the right. "The left ventricle works harder" with no reason earns nothing. "The left ventricle holds more blood" is wrong and is penalised.

4. The Four Valves

4 marks

Name the four valves of the mammalian heart and state where each is found.

Model solution unlocks after you try

Tricuspid valve: between the right atrium and the right ventricle. (1) Bicuspid (mitral) valve: between the left atrium and the left ventricle. (1) Pulmonary semilunar valve: at the base of the pulmonary artery, where it leaves the right ventricle. (1) Aortic semilunar valve: at the base of the aorta, where it leaves the left ventricle. (1)

Mark scheme note: Each mark needs the name and the correct position. Placing the tricuspid on the left or the bicuspid on the right is the commonest lost mark ("tRi = Right"). "Semilunar valves" written once without saying which vessel scores one mark at most.

5. Supports and Partitions

3 marks

(a) Describe the chordae tendineae and explain why they are needed. (b) State the function of the septum.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) They are tough cords joining the flaps of the atrioventricular valves to the papillary muscles on the ventricle wall. (1) When the ventricles contract, they hold the flaps so they shut but cannot be blown back into the atria, so blood does not leak backwards. (1) (b) The septum separates the right and left halves of the heart, keeping oxygen-poor and oxygen-rich blood apart. (1)

Mark scheme note: The first mark in (a) is for structure and attachment, the second for function; "they prevent backflow" alone earns only the second. "The septum pumps blood" is wrong and scores zero.

6. Wall Ratio and Drawing Magnification

5 marks

In a dissection, a student measured the left ventricle wall as 13.1 mm and 12.7 mm, and the right ventricle wall as 4.4 mm and 4.2 mm. The goat heart was 8.6 cm long and his drawing of it was 172 mm long.
(a) Calculate the mean thickness of each ventricle wall. (b) Calculate how many times thicker the left ventricle wall is. (c) Calculate the magnification of the drawing.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) Mean LV = (13.1 + 12.7) ÷ 2 = 12.9 mm. (1) Mean RV = (4.4 + 4.2) ÷ 2 = 4.3 mm. (1) (b) Ratio = 12.9 ÷ 4.3 = 3.0. (1) (c) Same units: 172 mm = 17.2 cm (1); magnification = 17.2 ÷ 8.6 = ×2.0. (1)

Mark scheme note: Units are needed on the means in (a). In (b), the difference (8.6 mm) is not a ratio and scores zero. In (c), dividing 172 by 8.6 without converting (×20) loses the final mark, and so does writing "2.0 cm": magnification has no unit.

7. Practical: The Goat-Heart Dissection

4 marks

(a) State two precautions that were taken during the dissection and measurement. (b) Give two sources of error in the wall measurements, stating for each whether the reading was too high or too low.

Model solution unlocks after you try

(a) Any two, in the past tense: gloves were worn and hands were washed afterwards (1); the scalpel was used on a dissecting board and cut away from the body (1); every wall was measured at the same height, midway between base and apex (1); the eye was level with the ruler mark when reading (1); each wall was measured twice and the mean taken (1). (Maximum 2.) (b) Any two: a slanting cut exposed the wall at an angle, so the reading was too high (1); measuring where a papillary muscle joined the wall added extra muscle, so the reading was too high (1); a specimen stored for days had shrunk and lost fluid, so the walls read too low (1). (Maximum 2.)

Mark scheme note: Precautions written as instructions or in the future tense lose the marks. In (b) the error and its direction must both be right; "human error" or "the ruler was faulty" with no direction scores zero. Parallax is accepted only if the answer says it can make readings too high or too low at random.

8. Drawing the Longitudinal Section

4 marks

State four rules a candidate should follow when drawing a labelled longitudinal section of the mammalian heart for WAEC.

Model solution unlocks after you try

Any four: draw the heart in front view, so the right side of the heart is on the reader's left (1); make the left ventricle wall clearly thicker than the right (1); draw it large, at least half a page, with clean, unshaded lines (1); use ruled horizontal label lines with no arrowheads (1); add arrows showing the direction of blood flow inside the chambers (1); write a title in capitals below the drawing, e.g. LONGITUDINAL SECTION OF A MAMMALIAN HEART, and state the magnification (1). (Maximum 4.)

Mark scheme note: Each rule must be different; "label clearly" and "label neatly" count as one vague point and earn nothing. A rule that puts the left ventricle on the reader's left is wrong and is penalised.
