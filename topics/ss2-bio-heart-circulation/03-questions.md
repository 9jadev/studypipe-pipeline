## LESSON 01: Need for Transport — Open vs Closed & Single vs Double Circulation

### Part 1: 12 Interactive Quiz Questions (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **Q1 (JAMB-style · Understand):** Why can a microscopic freshwater *Amoeba* obtain all the oxygen it requires by simple diffusion across its cell membrane, whereas an adult human cannot?
   - **A.** *Amoeba* has a very large surface-area-to-volume ratio and tiny diffusion distances, whereas a human has a very small surface-area-to-volume ratio and large internal distances
   - **B.** *Amoeba* does not respire aerobically, whereas human cells respire only anaerobically
   - **C.** Human cell membranes are completely impermeable to oxygen and carbon dioxide
   - **D.** *Amoeba* possesses a high-pressure four-chambered heart inside its cytoplasm
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** As an organism's linear dimension $L$ increases, its surface area grows as $L^2$ while its metabolising volume grows as $L^3$, so $\frac{\text{SA}}{V} = \frac{6}{L}$ falls sharply and diffusion alone becomes far too slow over distances greater than $1\text{ mm}$.

2. **Q2 (WAEC-style · Apply / Calculation):** Two tissue models are shaped as cubes of side lengths $L_1 = 1.5\text{ mm}$ and $L_2 = 6.0\text{ mm}$. What is the surface-area-to-volume ratio ($\text{SA}/\text{V}$) of each cube?
   - **A.** $1.50\text{ mm}^{-1}$ and $6.00\text{ mm}^{-1}$
   - **B.** $4.00\text{ mm}^{-1}$ and $1.00\text{ mm}^{-1}$
   - **C.** $6.00\text{ mm}^{-1}$ and $2.00\text{ mm}^{-1}$
   - **D.** $13.5\text{ mm}^{-1}$ and $216\text{ mm}^{-1}$
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** For a cube of side $L$, $\frac{\text{SA}}{V} = \frac{6L^2}{L^3} = \frac{6}{L}$. For $L_1 = 1.5\text{ mm}$: $\frac{\text{SA}_1}{V_1} = \frac{6}{1.5\text{ mm}} = 4.00\text{ mm}^{-1}$. For $L_2 = 6.0\text{ mm}$: $\frac{\text{SA}_2}{V_2} = \frac{6}{6.0\text{ mm}} = 1.00\text{ mm}^{-1}$.

3. **Q3 (JAMB-style · Remember):** Which of the following pairs of organisms possesses an **open circulatory system** in which haemolymph flows directly into a body cavity called the haemocoel?
   - **A.** Earthworm (*Lumbricus*) and *Tilapia*
   - **B.** Cockroach (*Periplaneta*) and Giant African land snail (*Archachatina*)
   - **C.** Toad (*Bufo*) and Agama lizard
   - **D.** Domestic pigeon and laboratory rat
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** Arthropods (such as the cockroach) and most molluscs (such as the land snail) have an open circulatory system where colourless haemolymph bathes organs directly inside the haemocoel at low pressure. Earthworms and all vertebrates have closed circulatory systems.

4. **Q4 (WAEC-style · Understand):** In a bony fish such as *Tilapia*, blood circulation is described as **single circulation** because:
   - **A.** the fish has only one blood vessel in its entire body
   - **B.** blood passes through the two-chambered heart only once during each complete circuit around the body
   - **C.** oxygenated blood returns from the gills directly to the left atrium before going to the muscles
   - **D.** blood flows in one direction during the day and reverses direction at night
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** A bony fish has a 2-chambered heart (1 atrium, 1 ventricle). Blood is pumped $\text{Heart} \rightarrow \text{Gill capillaries} \rightarrow \text{Systemic body capillaries} \rightarrow \text{Heart}$, passing through the heart only once per complete tour.

5. **Q5 (NECO-style · Analyse):** What is the two-fold physiological advantage of **complete double circulation** in a mammal compared with single circulation in a fish?
   - **A.** Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are kept completely separate, and oxygenated blood is re-pumped by the left ventricle at high pressure to the systemic organs
   - **B.** Blood moves much more slowly through mammalian arteries, and red blood cells are destroyed in the lungs
   - **C.** Blood bypasses the lungs during exercise, and the right ventricle pumps blood directly to the brain
   - **D.** Mammalian blood does not require haemoglobin because pressure alone dissolves oxygen
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** In a 4-chambered mammalian heart, the interventricular septum prevents any mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, and returning pulmonary blood is re-pressurised by the thick left ventricle ($120\text{ mmHg}$) for rapid delivery to active tissues.

6. **Q6 (JAMB-style · Understand):** Which of the following vertebrates has a **three-chambered heart** (two atria and one ventricle) resulting in incomplete double circulation?
   - **A.** *Tilapia* (bony fish)
   - **B.** Adult toad (*Bufo regularis*)
   - **C.** Domestic fowl (*Gallus*)
   - **D.** Man (*Homo sapiens*)
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** Amphibians (such as the toad and frog) have two atria (right and left) opening into a single undivided ventricle, producing incomplete double circulation. Fish have 2 chambers; birds and mammals have 4 chambers.

7. **Q7 (WAEC-style · Apply / Calculation):** During a warm-up jog at Surulere Stadium, an athlete has a heart rate $\text{HR} = 110\text{ beats/min}$ and a stroke volume $\text{SV} = 90\text{ mL/beat}$. What is the athlete's cardiac output ($\text{CO}$) in $\text{L/min}$?
   - **A.** $4.95\text{ L/min}$
   - **B.** $9.90\text{ L/min}$
   - **C.** $12.2\text{ L/min}$
   - **D.** $20.0\text{ L/min}$
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** Cardiac output is the volume of blood pumped by one ventricle per minute: $\text{CO} = \text{HR} \times \text{SV} = 110\text{ bpm} \times 90\text{ mL} = 9900\text{ mL/min} = \frac{9900}{1000}\text{ L/min} = 9.90\text{ L/min}$.

8. **Q8 (NECO-style · Understand):** Which of the following lists correctly identifies the three fundamental components of the mammalian circulatory system?
   - **A.** Heart (pump), blood vessels (channels), and blood (transport medium)
   - **B.** Lungs (pump), trachea (channel), and air (medium)
   - **C.** Kidney (pump), ureter (channel), and urine (medium)
   - **D.** Haemocoel (pump), malpighian tubules (channels), and haemolymph (medium)
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** Every closed circulatory system requires a muscular pump (the heart), a network of tubular channels (arteries, capillaries, veins), and a fluid connective tissue medium (blood).

9. **Q9 (JAMB-style · Analyse):** Why does a cockroach remain an active terrestrial insect despite having a low-pressure open circulatory system whose haemolymph lacks haemoglobin?
   - **A.** Its tracheal system pipes gaseous oxygen directly to body tissues through spiracles and tracheoles, so haemolymph only needs to transport digested food and wastes
   - **B.** Its exoskeleton conducts oxygen electrically from the antenna to the legs
   - **C.** Cockroaches absorb all their oxygen through their compound eyes
   - **D.** Cockroach muscles do not require oxygen for flight or running
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** In insects, respiratory gas exchange is decoupled from the circulatory system: branched air tubes (tracheae and tracheoles) deliver $\text{O}_2$ directly to cells, while haemolymph transports nutrients, hormones, and uric acid.

10. **Q10 (WAEC Practical · Apply / Calculation):** An adult mammal has a total blood volume $V_{\text{blood}} = 5.40\text{ L}$. At rest, its heart rate is $72\text{ bpm}$ and its stroke volume is $75\text{ mL}$. How many seconds does it take for the entire blood volume to complete one full circulation?
    - **A.** $30.0\text{ s}$
    - **B.** $45.0\text{ s}$
    - **C.** $60.0\text{ s}$
    - **D.** $90.0\text{ s}$
    - **Correct:** `2` (`C`)
    - **Explanation:** First find Cardiac Output: $\text{CO} = \text{HR} \times \text{SV} = 72 \times 75\text{ mL} = 5400\text{ mL/min} = 5.40\text{ L/min}$. Then circuit time $t = \frac{V_{\text{blood}}}{\text{CO}} = \frac{5.40\text{ L}}{5.40\text{ L/min}} = 1.00\text{ min} = 60.0\text{ s}$.

11. **Q11 (NECO-style · Understand):** Which of the following substances is **NOT** transported by the mammalian blood circulatory system?
    - **A.** Urea from the liver to the kidneys
    - **B.** Insulin hormone from the pancreas to the liver
    - **C.** Undigested cellulose roughage from the ileum to the colon
    - **D.** Carbon dioxide as hydrogencarbonate ions ($\text{HCO}_3^-$) from tissues to the lungs
    - **Correct:** `2` (`C`)
    - **Explanation:** Undigested dietary fibre (cellulose) remains inside the lumen of the alimentary canal and is egested as faeces; it never enters the bloodstream.

12. **Q12 (WAEC-style · Analyse):** On a graph of Surface-Area-to-Volume ratio ($y = \text{SA}/\text{V}$) plotted against cube side length ($x = L$), what happens to $y$ when $L$ is multiplied by $3$?
    - **A.** $y$ increases by a factor of $3$
    - **B.** $y$ increases by a factor of $9$
    - **C.** $y$ decreases to one-third ($\frac{1}{3}$) of its original value
    - **D.** $y$ remains constant regardless of $L$
    - **Correct:** `2` (`C`)
    - **Explanation:** Because $\frac{\text{SA}}{V} = \frac{6}{L}$ is inversely proportional to $L$, tripling $L$ reduces $\frac{\text{SA}}{V}$ to $\frac{6}{3L} = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{6}{L}\right)$.

## LESSON 02: Structure of the Mammalian Heart & The Complete Double-Circulation Pathway

### Part 1: 12 Interactive Quiz Questions (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **Q1 (WAEC-style · Understand):** Which protective double-walled sac surrounds the mammalian heart and secretes a lubricating fluid that prevents friction as the heart beats against the lungs and sternum?
   - **A.** Pleural membrane
   - **B.** Pericardium
   - **C.** Peritoneum
   - **D.** Meninges
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** The pericardium is the tough double membrane enclosing the heart; pericardial fluid between its inner visceral and outer parietal layers reduces friction during contraction.

2. **Q2 (JAMB-style · Analyse):** Why is the muscular wall (myocardium) of the **left ventricle** approximately three times thicker than the wall of the **right ventricle**?
   - **A.** The left ventricle stores three times more blood volume than the right ventricle
   - **B.** The left ventricle must generate high pressure ($\approx 120\text{ mmHg}$) to pump oxygenated blood through the aorta to the entire body, whereas the right ventricle pumps blood only a short distance to the nearby lungs
   - **C.** Deoxygenated blood in the right ventricle is thinner and less viscous than oxygenated blood
   - **D.** The left ventricle beats three times faster than the right ventricle
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** Both ventricles pump the exact same stroke volume ($\approx 70\text{ mL}$) at the exact same rate, but the systemic circuit offers much greater resistance and distance than the nearby pulmonary circuit, requiring a $3\times$ thicker left ventricular wall.

3. **Q3 (WAEC-style · Remember):** Which valve guards the opening between the **left atrium** and the **left ventricle**, and how many flaps (cusps) does it possess?
   - **A.** Tricuspid valve with 3 cusps
   - **B.** Bicuspid (mitral) valve with 2 cusps
   - **C.** Pulmonary semilunar valve with 3 half-moon pockets
   - **D.** Pyloric sphincter valve with 1 ring
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** Remember **"Right = tRi"**: the tricuspid valve (3 cusps) is on the right side ($\text{RA} \rightarrow \text{RV}$), while the bicuspid or mitral valve (2 cusps) is on the left side ($\text{LA} \rightarrow \text{LV}$).

4. **Q4 (JAMB-style · Understand):** What is the specific function of the **chordae tendineae** (heart strings) and **papillary muscles** inside the ventricles?
   - **A.** They pull the semilunar valves open during atrial diastole
   - **B.** They anchor the cusps of the tricuspid and bicuspid valves to the ventricular wall so the valves cannot turn inside out (prolapse) into the atria when the ventricles contract
   - **C.** They conduct electrical impulses from the brain to the vena cava
   - **D.** They secrete heparin into the ventricular chambers
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** During powerful ventricular systole, high intraventricular pressure pushes against the closed AV valve flaps; the inelastic chordae tendineae tensed by papillary muscles prevent the flaps from inverting into the atria.

5. **Q5 (WAEC-style · Analyse):** Which two blood vessels are the famous **exceptions** to the rule that arteries carry oxygenated blood and veins carry deoxygenated blood?
   - **A.** Renal artery and renal vein
   - **B.** Carotid artery and jugular vein
   - **C.** Pulmonary artery (carries deoxygenated blood from RV to lungs) and pulmonary veins (carry oxygenated blood from lungs to LA)
   - **D.** Hepatic artery and hepatic portal vein
   - **Correct:** `2` (`C`)
   - **Explanation:** Vessel identity depends on flow direction relative to the heart: the pulmonary artery leaves the right ventricle carrying deoxygenated blood to the lungs, while the four pulmonary veins return oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium.

6. **Q6 (JAMB-style · Understand):** How does the thick cardiac muscle (myocardium) of the heart wall receive its own supply of oxygen and glucose?
   - **A.** By direct diffusion from the deoxygenated blood inside the right atrium
   - **B.** Through the **coronary arteries**, which branch directly off the base of the aorta just above the aortic semilunar valve
   - **C.** Through the pulmonary artery before it reaches the lungs
   - **D.** Through the hepatic portal vein from the liver
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** The myocardium is too thick for diffusion from the chambers; it is supplied by the left and right coronary arteries branching off the ascending aorta, and drained by the coronary veins (coronary sinus) into the right atrium.

7. **Q7 (NECO-style · Apply):** A molecule of glucose is absorbed into the blood capillaries of the villi in the **ileum**. Which sequence correctly traces its path to the **brain**?
   - **A.** $\text{Ileum} \rightarrow \text{Hepatic vein} \rightarrow \text{Liver} \rightarrow \text{Hepatic portal vein} \rightarrow \text{Left atrium} \rightarrow \text{Aorta} \rightarrow \text{Brain}$
   - **B.** $\text{Ileum} \rightarrow \text{Hepatic portal vein} \rightarrow \text{Liver} \rightarrow \text{Hepatic vein} \rightarrow \text{Inferior vena cava} \rightarrow \text{Right heart} \rightarrow \text{Lungs} \rightarrow \text{Left heart} \rightarrow \text{Aorta} \rightarrow \text{Carotid artery} \rightarrow \text{Brain}$
   - **C.** $\text{Ileum} \rightarrow \text{Inferior vena cava} \rightarrow \text{Right atrium} \rightarrow \text{Aorta} \rightarrow \text{Carotid artery} \rightarrow \text{Brain}$
   - **D.** $\text{Ileum} \rightarrow \text{Mesenteric artery} \rightarrow \text{Liver} \rightarrow \text{Pulmonary vein} \rightarrow \text{Brain}$
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** Blood from the gut must pass through the **hepatic portal vein** to the **liver** first, then exit via the **hepatic vein** into the **inferior vena cava**, pass through the right heart $\rightarrow$ lungs $\rightarrow$ left heart $\rightarrow$ aorta $\rightarrow$ carotid artery $\rightarrow$ brain.

8. **Q8 (WAEC-style · Apply / Calculation):** In a freshly dissected goat heart at Surulere Market, the right ventricular wall thickness is $w_{\text{RV}} = 4.5\text{ mm}$ and the left ventricular wall thickness is $w_{\text{LV}} = 13.5\text{ mm}$. If peak systolic pressure is proportional to wall thickness ($P = 8.0 \times w\text{ mmHg}$), what are the wall-thickness ratio $R$ and the peak left ventricular pressure $P_{\text{LV}}$?
   - **A.** $R = 2.0$ and $P_{\text{LV}} = 72\text{ mmHg}$
   - **B.** $R = 3.0$ and $P_{\text{LV}} = 108\text{ mmHg}$
   - **C.** $R = 3.5$ and $P_{\text{LV}} = 135\text{ mmHg}$
   - **D.** $R = 4.5$ and $P_{\text{LV}} = 36\text{ mmHg}$
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** $R = \frac{w_{\text{LV}}}{w_{\text{RV}}} = \frac{13.5\text{ mm}}{4.5\text{ mm}} = 3.0$, and $P_{\text{LV}} = 8.0 \times 13.5 = 108.0\text{ mmHg}$.

9. **Q9 (JAMB-style · Apply / Calculation):** Echocardiography of a patient at LUTH shows an End-Diastolic Volume $\text{EDV} = 130\text{ mL}$ and an End-Systolic Volume $\text{ESV} = 52\text{ mL}$. What are the patient's Stroke Volume ($\text{SV}$) and Ejection Fraction ($\text{EF}$)?
   - **A.** $\text{SV} = 78\text{ mL}$ and $\text{EF} = 60.0\%$
   - **B.** $\text{SV} = 182\text{ mL}$ and $\text{EF} = 40.0\%$
   - **C.** $\text{SV} = 78\text{ mL}$ and $\text{EF} = 66.7\%$
   - **D.** $\text{SV} = 52\text{ mL}$ and $\text{EF} = 50.0\%$
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** $\text{SV} = \text{EDV} - \text{ESV} = 130\text{ mL} - 52\text{ mL} = 78\text{ mL}$. Then $\text{EF} = \frac{\text{SV}}{\text{EDV}} \times 100\% = \frac{78}{130} \times 100\% = 60.0\%$.

10. **Q10 (WAEC Practical · Understand):** When drawing the longitudinal section of the mammalian heart in a front (ventral) view during WAEC Paper 3, which chamber must be drawn on the **viewer's right-hand side** with the thickest muscular wall?
    - **A.** Right atrium
    - **B.** Right ventricle
    - **C.** Left ventricle
    - **D.** Superior vena cava
    - **Correct:** `2` (`C`)
    - **Explanation:** Anatomical diagrams are drawn as if facing the specimen: the specimen's right side is on the viewer's left, and the specimen's thick-walled **left ventricle** is on the viewer's **right**.

11. **Q11 (NECO-style · Understand):** What is the role of the muscular **interventricular septum** in the mammalian heart?
    - **A.** It pumps blood from the atria into the venae cavae
    - **B.** It completely separates the right and left sides of the heart so deoxygenated blood and oxygenated blood cannot mix
    - **C.** It manufactures red blood cells in adults
    - **D.** It closes the entrance to the pulmonary veins
    - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
    - **Explanation:** The septum is the continuous muscular partition between the right and left chambers; a congenital gap in the septum ("hole in the heart") allows deoxygenated blood to mix with oxygenated blood.

12. **Q12 (WAEC Practical · Apply / Calculation):** A student draws a longitudinal section of a sheep's heart of actual length $A = 9.0\text{ cm}$ so that the drawing measures $I = 13.5\text{ cm}$ from base to apex. What is the linear magnification $M$ of the drawing?
    - **A.** $\times 0.67$
    - **B.** $\times 1.2$
    - **C.** $\times 1.5$
    - **D.** $\times 4.5$
    - **Correct:** `2` (`C`)
    - **Explanation:** $M = \frac{I}{A} = \frac{13.5\text{ cm}}{9.0\text{ cm}} = \times 1.5$.

## LESSON 03: Working of the Heart — Cardiac Cycle, Pacemaker (SAN/AVN), Heart Sounds, Pulse & Blood Pressure

### Part 1: 12 Interactive Quiz Questions (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **Q1 (JAMB-style · Understand):** What does it mean to say that the mammalian heartbeat is **myogenic**?
   - **A.** The heartbeat is initiated by motor neurones from the spinal cord
   - **B.** Rhythmic contraction originates spontaneously within the cardiac muscle itself (at the sinoatrial node) without requiring external nervous stimulation
   - **C.** The heart beats only when skeletal muscles in the legs contract
   - **D.** The heart contracts only when adrenaline is injected into the blood
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** Cardiac muscle is myogenic: its natural pacemaker (the sinoatrial node) depolarises rhythmically on its own. Nervous and hormonal signals only modify the rate and force of contraction.

2. **Q2 (WAEC-style · Remember):** Where is the **sinoatrial node (SAN)** — the natural pacemaker of the mammalian heart — located?
   - **A.** In the muscular wall of the right atrium near the entrance of the superior vena cava
   - **B.** At the apex of the left ventricle
   - **C.** Inside the bicuspid valve flaps
   - **D.** In the wall of the pulmonary artery
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** The SAN is a specialized patch of myogenic pacemaker cells embedded in the upper wall of the right atrium near the superior vena cava opening.

3. **Q3 (NECO-style · Understand):** Which sequence correctly traces the spread of an electrical impulse through the conducting system of the heart during one heartbeat?
   - **A.** $\text{AVN} \rightarrow \text{SAN} \rightarrow \text{Purkinje fibres} \rightarrow \text{Bundle of His}$
   - **B.** $\text{SAN} \rightarrow \text{Atrial muscle} \rightarrow \text{AVN} \rightarrow \text{Bundle of His} \rightarrow \text{Purkinje fibres}$
   - **C.** $\text{Bundle of His} \rightarrow \text{SAN} \rightarrow \text{AVN} \rightarrow \text{Vena cava}$
   - **D.** $\text{Purkinje fibres} \rightarrow \text{Bundle of His} \rightarrow \text{AVN} \rightarrow \text{SAN}$
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** The impulse starts at the $\text{SAN}$, spreads across both atria, pauses for $\sim 0.1\text{ s}$ at the $\text{AVN}$ (so atria finish emptying), travels down the $\text{Bundle of His}$ in the septum, and fans out via $\text{Purkinje fibres}$ so the ventricles contract from the apex upward.

4. **Q4 (JAMB-style · Understand):** What causes the first heart sound (**"Lub"**) and the second heart sound (**"Dub"**) heard through a stethoscope?
   - **A.** "Lub" is caused by closure of the semilunar valves; "Dub" is caused by closure of the venae cavae
   - **B.** "Lub" is caused by closure of the tricuspid and bicuspid (atrioventricular) valves at the start of ventricular systole; "Dub" is caused by closure of the aortic and pulmonary semilunar valves at the start of ventricular diastole
   - **C.** "Lub" is caused by blood hitting the lungs; "Dub" is caused by blood hitting the brain
   - **D.** Both sounds are caused by the opening of the coronary arteries
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** Valve closure (never valve opening) creates the heart sounds: AV valves closing at the onset of ventricular systole produce the longer, lower *"Lub"* ($S_1$); semilunar valves snapping shut at the onset of ventricular diastole produce the shorter, sharper *"Dub"* ($S_2$).

5. **Q5 (WAEC-style · Apply / Calculation):** At a resting heart rate of $\text{HR} = 75\text{ beats per minute}$, what is the total duration $T$ of one complete cardiac cycle, and if atrial systole lasts $0.10\text{ s}$ and ventricular systole lasts $0.30\text{ s}$, how long does complete cardiac diastole last?
   - **A.** $T = 0.80\text{ s}$; diastole $= 0.40\text{ s}$
   - **B.** $T = 1.25\text{ s}$; diastole $= 0.85\text{ s}$
   - **C.** $T = 0.60\text{ s}$; diastole $= 0.20\text{ s}$
   - **D.** $T = 1.00\text{ s}$; diastole $= 0.60\text{ s}$
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** $T = \frac{60\text{ s}}{\text{HR}} = \frac{60}{75} = 0.80\text{ s}$. Subtracting atrial systole ($0.10\text{ s}$) and ventricular systole ($0.30\text{ s}$) leaves complete diastole $t_{\text{dia}} = 0.80 - (0.10 + 0.30) = 0.40\text{ s}$.

6. **Q6 (JAMB-style · Understand):** Which of the following combinations **increases** the heart rate during vigorous exercise or sudden fright?
   - **A.** Impulses along the vagus (parasympathetic) nerve and low carbon dioxide in the blood
   - **B.** Impulses along the sympathetic cardiac nerve, secretion of adrenaline from the adrenal medulla, and elevated $\text{CO}_2$ concentration in the blood
   - **C.** Deep sleep and acetylcholine release at the sinoatrial node
   - **D.** Blockage of the sinoatrial node by low temperature
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** High blood $\text{CO}_2$ (low pH) stimulates chemoreceptors that signal the cardiac centre in the medulla oblongata to fire sympathetic impulses and release adrenaline, accelerating the SAN pacemaker. The vagus nerve slows the heart down.

7. **Q7 (WAEC-style · Apply / Calculation):** A sphygmomanometer reading on an adult at LUTH Clinic is $125/80\text{ mmHg}$. What are the patient's **Pulse Pressure ($\text{PP}$)** and **Mean Arterial Pressure ($\text{MAP} = P_{\text{dia}} + \frac{1}{3}\text{PP}$)**?
   - **A.** $\text{PP} = 45\text{ mmHg}$ and $\text{MAP} = 95.0\text{ mmHg}$
   - **B.** $\text{PP} = 205\text{ mmHg}$ and $\text{MAP} = 102.5\text{ mmHg}$
   - **C.** $\text{PP} = 40\text{ mmHg}$ and $\text{MAP} = 80.0\text{ mmHg}$
   - **D.** $\text{PP} = 45\text{ mmHg}$ and $\text{MAP} = 110.0\text{ mmHg}$
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** $\text{PP} = P_{\text{sys}} - P_{\text{dia}} = 125 - 80 = 45\text{ mmHg}$. Then $\text{MAP} = P_{\text{dia}} + \frac{\text{PP}}{3} = 80 + \frac{45}{3} = 80 + 15 = 95.0\text{ mmHg}$.

8. **Q8 (NECO-style · Understand):** Where on the human body can the arterial **pulse** be felt most easily with the fingertips, and why?
   - **A.** Over the radial artery at the wrist or the carotid artery in the neck, where an elastic artery passes close to the body surface over a firm bone or muscle
   - **B.** In the middle of the palm over a capillary bed
   - **C.** On the earlobe where veins contain pocket valves
   - **D.** Over the kneecap where lymph vessels drain
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** A pulse is the rhythmic wave of distension and elastic recoil of an arterial wall with each left ventricular systole; it is palpated where a superficial artery can be pressed gently against underlying bone (e.g., radial artery at the wrist, carotid in the neck, temporal at the temple).

9. **Q9 (WAEC-style · Analyse):** Why is the brief $0.1\text{ s}$ delay of the electrical impulse at the **Atrioventricular Node (AVN)** essential for efficient pumping?
   - **A.** It allows both atria to finish contracting and emptying their blood completely into the ventricles before the ventricles begin to contract
   - **B.** It allows blood to flow backward from the aorta into the left atrium
   - **C.** It stops the right ventricle from pumping blood to the lungs
   - **D.** It cools down the blood before it enters the brain
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** Without the AVN delay, the atria and ventricles would contract at the same instant, closing the AV valves prematurely and preventing atrial systole from topping up the ventricles.

## LESSON 04: Blood Vessels, Capillary Exchange, the Lymphatic System & Cardiovascular Disorders

### Part 1: 12 Interactive Quiz Questions (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **Q1 (WAEC-style · Understand):** Which of the following correctly contrasts the wall and lumen of an **artery** with those of a corresponding **vein**?
   - **A.** An artery has a thick muscular and elastic wall with a narrow lumen and no valves (except at the heart base), whereas a vein has a thinner wall, a wide lumen, and pocket semilunar valves
   - **B.** An artery has a one-cell-thick wall with pocket valves, whereas a vein has a thick muscular wall with a narrow lumen
   - **C.** Both arteries and veins have walls that are a single endothelial cell thick
   - **D.** A vein carries blood at high pulsatile pressure, whereas an artery carries blood at zero pressure
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** Arteries withstand high, pulsatile ventricular pressure using a thick tunica media rich in smooth muscle and elastic fibres around a narrow lumen; veins operate at low pressure, having thinner walls, wide lumens to reduce resistance, and semilunar valves to prevent backflow.

2. **Q2 (JAMB-style · Understand):** How are **capillaries** structurally adapted to serve as the site of material exchange between blood and body cells?
   - **A.** Their walls consist of a single layer of flattened endothelial cells (short diffusion distance), they form a vast network (huge total surface area), and blood flows very slowly through their narrow lumens
   - **B.** Their walls are coated with thick waterproof keratin to trap oxygen inside the blood
   - **C.** Blood rockets through capillaries at the highest speed in the body ($500\text{ mm/s}$)
   - **D.** They possess muscular valves that pump red blood cells out into the tissue spaces
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** A 1-cell-thick endothelium minimises diffusion distance, the enormous total cross-sectional area of capillary beds slows blood velocity to $< 1\text{ mm/s}$ (giving ample time for exchange), and the narrow lumen forces RBCs to pass in single file close to the wall.

3. **Q3 (NECO-style · Remember):** Which pair of blood vessels supplies oxygenated blood **to** the kidney and drains deoxygenated, filtered blood **from** the kidney?
   - **A.** Hepatic artery and hepatic vein
   - **B.** Renal artery and renal vein
   - **C.** Carotid artery and jugular vein
   - **D.** Mesenteric artery and subclavian vein
   - **Correct:** `1` (`B`)
   - **Explanation:** *Renal* refers to the kidney (renal artery $\rightarrow$ kidney $\rightarrow$ renal vein); *hepatic* refers to the liver; *carotid/jugular* refer to the head/brain; *subclavian* refers to the arms.

4. **Q4 (WAEC-style · Apply / Calculation):** At the arterial end of a skeletal muscle capillary bed, the hydrostatic blood pressure is $P_c = 36\text{ mmHg}$ pushing fluid outward, while the plasma protein osmotic pressure is $\pi_c = 25\text{ mmHg}$ pulling fluid inward. At the venous end, $P_c$ drops to $16\text{ mmHg}$ while $\pi_c$ remains $25\text{ mmHg}$. What is the Net Filtration Pressure ($\text{NFP} = P_c - \pi_c$) at the arterial end and at the venous end?

   - **C.** Arterial $\text{NFP} = +61\text{ mmHg}$; Venous $\text{NFP} = +41\text{ mmHg}$
   - **D.** Arterial $\text{NFP} = 0\text{ mmHg}$; Venous $\text{NFP} = 0\text{ mmHg}$

   - **Explanation:** At the arterial end: $\text{NFP}_{\text{art}} = 36 - 25 = +11\text{ mmHg}$ (positive pressure forces water, glucose, amino acids, and $\text{O}_2$ out as tissue fluid). At the venous end: $\text{NFP}_{\text{ven}} = 16 - 25 = -9\text{ mmHg}$ (negative value means osmotic pressure draws fluid back into the capillary).

   - **Correct:** `2` (`C`)
   - **Explanation:** Capillary endothelial pores allow water and small solutes (glucose, amino acids, ions, gases) to filter through, but erythrocytes, platelets, and large plasma proteins are too big to cross. Small lymphocytes can squeeze through into lymph.

   - **D.** Renal veins near the kidneys

   - **Explanation:** Excess tissue fluid enters blind-ended lymph capillaries, passes through lymph vessels and lymph nodes, collects in the thoracic duct and right lymphatic duct, and empties into the subclavian veins just before the superior vena cava.

## LESSON 05: Composition of Blood, Clotting Cascade, ABO/Rhesus Blood Groups & WAEC Paper 3 Practical Clinic

### Part 1: 12 Interactive Quiz Questions (`window.LESSON_QUIZ`)

1. **Q1 (WAEC-style · Understand):** If a sample of anticoagulated mammalian blood is spun in a centrifuge, it separates into approximately $55\%$ pale-yellow fluid (**plasma**) and $45\%$ blood cells. How does **blood serum** differ from **blood plasma**?
   - **A.** Serum is plasma from which fibrinogen and other clotting factors have been removed after clotting
   - **B.** Serum contains red blood cells, whereas plasma contains only platelets
   - **C.** Serum is found only in insects, whereas plasma is found only in fish
   - **D.** Serum is pure water without any antibodies or mineral salts
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** $\text{Serum} = \text{Plasma} - \text{Fibrinogen (and clotting factors)}$. Serum still contains water, salts, glucose, amino acids, urea, and antibodies (immunoglobulins).

   - **C.** It has a rigid cellulose cell wall to prevent bursting in pure water
   - **D.** It lives for 10 years and divides by mitosis inside the arteries
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** Mature mammalian RBCs extrude their nucleus and organelles during maturation in the red bone marrow, packing $\sim 280\text{ million}$ haemoglobin molecules into a flexible biconcave disc with a lifespan of $\sim 120\text{ days}$.

   - **B.** Phagocytes carry oxygen as oxyhaemoglobin, whereas lymphocytes convert fibrinogen to fibrin
   - **C.** Phagocytes lack a nucleus, whereas lymphocytes have three nuclei
   - **D.** Phagocytes are made in the kidneys, whereas lymphocytes are made in the lungs
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** Granulocytes/phagocytes (e.g., neutrophils) have multi-lobed nuclei and amoeboid movement for engulfing pathogens; lymphocytes have a large spherical nucleus and mediate specific humoral/cellular immunity via antibodies.

   - **A.** $\text{Platelets + damaged tissue} \rightarrow \text{Thromboplastin} \xrightarrow{\text{Ca}^{2+},\text{ Vit K}} \text{converts Prothrombin to Thrombin} \rightarrow \text{Thrombin converts soluble Fibrinogen to insoluble Fibrin mesh}$
   - **B.** $\text{Fibrin} \rightarrow \text{Fibrinogen} \rightarrow \text{Thrombin} \rightarrow \text{Prothrombin} \rightarrow \text{Heparin}$
   - **C.** $\text{Red blood cells} \xrightarrow{\text{Iron}} \text{Haemoglobin} \rightarrow \text{Oxyhaemoglobin clot}$
   - **D.** $\text{Lymphocytes} \rightarrow \text{Anti-a antibodies} \rightarrow \text{Agglutination scab}$
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** Damaged cells and platelets release **thromboplastin (thrombokinase)**, which requires **calcium ions ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$)** and **Vitamin K** (for prothrombin synthesis in the liver) to convert inactive **prothrombin** into active **thrombin**; thrombin then cleaves soluble **fibrinogen** into insoluble **fibrin** threads that trap RBCs.

   - **A.** Healthy vessels have a smooth endothelial lining and natural **heparin** (anticoagulant); blood banks add **sodium citrate** (or EDTA) to bind calcium ions ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$)
   - **B.** Healthy vessels contain excess thromboplastin; blood banks boil the blood to $100^\circ\text{C}$
   - **C.** Healthy blood lacks fibrinogen until exposed to sunlight
   - **D.** Blood banks add extra calcium ions and Vitamin K to stop clotting
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** Smooth endothelium prevents platelet activation and mast cells/basophils release **heparin** (which inhibits thrombin). In blood banks, **sodium citrate** chelates $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions so prothrombin cannot be converted to thrombin.

6. **Q6 (WAEC-style · Analyse):** An accident victim at LUTH Emergency Ward belongs to **Blood Group B**. Which antigens are on the victim's red blood cells, which antibodies are in their plasma, and which ABO blood groups can safely donate packed red cells to this patient?

   - **D.** No antigens on red cells; anti-a and anti-b in plasma; safe donor is Group AB only
   - **Correct:** `0` (`A`)
   - **Explanation:** A Group B person has Antigen B on RBCs and anti-a antibodies in plasma. Donor RBCs must not carry Antigen A (which would be clumped by the recipient's anti-a): therefore **Group B** (Antigen B) and **Group O** (no A/B antigens — universal donor) are safe.

Because Group O RBCs have no A or B antigens, they cannot be agglutinated by any recipient's plasma; because Group AB plasma has no anti-a or anti-b antibodies, it does not attack any donor's RBCs.
